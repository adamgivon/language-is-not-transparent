## Your actual legal options (even with zero insurance)
Insurance is only a *source of payment*, not the basis of liability. If he caused the damage, you typically have these civil options:

### 1) Civil claim for damages (tort/negligence / property damage)
You sue him personally for:
- **Cost to repair** the house (often restoration to prior condition)
- **Make-safe/emergency works**
- **Consequential losses**: temporary accommodation (your €80/day if reasonable), extra travel, storage, professional reports
- Sometimes **interest** and (in some systems) part of legal costs

What you need to win is usually straightforward: causation + fault + proof of loss. His “I can’t pay” is generally **not a defense** to liability.

### 2) Interim / urgent court relief (to deal with time pressure)
Many jurisdictions allow faster “interim” measures when ongoing harm exists, such as:
- Order requiring **immediate stabilization/make-safe works**
- Order requiring **interim payments** (e.g., temporary housing) while the main case continues
- Order allowing you to do the works and later recover the cost

This is often the most practical tool when you’re displaced and the building is exposed to weather.

### 3) Settlement agreement with enforceable security
If he’s willing to cooperate but not able to pay at once, you can do a written deal that is *enforceable like a judgment* in many places (your lawyer will structure it), ideally with:
- **Upfront payment** for emergency works + some housing
- **Installments**
- **Security** (where available): lien/charge over land, notarized deed, confession of judgment, guarantor, etc.

Without security, an installment promise can turn into endless delay.

### 4) Enforcement tools after a judgment (how you actually get paid)
If you get a judgment, payment can be forced using measures that vary by country, such as:
- **Wage/income garnishment**
- **Bank account seizure**
- **Lien/charge** on real estate or farm assets
- **Seizure and sale** of assets (vehicles, equipment)
- Court-supervised payment plans in some systems

If he truly has low income and few attachable assets, you may still win but recover slowly.

### 5) Insolvency/bankruptcy considerations (the hard edge)
If he is genuinely insolvent, he may:
- negotiate hard,
- delay,
- or in some cases file insolvency.

That doesn’t erase your claim, but it can cap or delay recovery. This is why **security** and **early interim orders** matter.

Depending on country:
- Operating an uninsured vehicle (even off-road) may breach regulations.
- Building safety authorities may issue **dangerous structure** orders requiring prompt action.
- Police involvement may or may not help; often this is primarily civil.
