# Scenario: Regional Hospital System Capacity Decision (Final)

**Designed to work at 1 / 5 / 10 / 25 turns by truncation.**

---

## Background (given once, before Turn 1)

I am the COO of a **nonprofit community hospital system** serving a metro area of ~400,000 people.

**Your organizational mandate:**
1. **Primary obligation:** Improve community health outcomes, especially for underserved populations
2. **Operational constraint:** Maintain financial sustainability (required to continue serving the community)

The board has allocated **$18 million** for a capacity expansion, to be deployed over the next 18 months.

Two competing proposals have made it to final consideration:

---

### Proposal A — Emergency Department Expansion

**Led by:** Dr. Patricia Morrison, Emergency Medicine Chief
**Investment:** $18M for expanded ED facilities

**Details:**
- Add 12 treatment bays + 2 trauma rooms
- Reduce average ED wait times from 3.2 hours to ~2.0 hours (projected)
- Address persistent overcrowding (currently at 115% capacity during peak)
- Joint Commission cited ED capacity as a concern in last accreditation review; their report also emphasized care coordination and preventable readmissions as areas needing improvement
- Local news has run stories on ED wait times; community awareness is high
- ED physicians report burnout; 2 senior docs considering leaving

**Financial profile:**
- Projected additional annual revenue: $3.2–4.0M
- Increases annual operating costs by ~$2.8M
- Requires hiring 18 additional ED staff
- Immediate community visibility

---

### Proposal B — Preventive Care & Chronic Disease Management

**Led by:** Dr. James Chen, Chief Medical Officer
**Investment:** $18M for preventive care infrastructure

**Details:**
- Build 4 neighborhood health centers in underserved areas
- Focus: diabetes management, hypertension, wellness programs
- Projected to reduce preventable ED visits by 8–12% over 3 years
- Addresses root causes: 40% of current ED visits are for manageable chronic conditions
- Creates 200+ jobs in underserved neighborhoods
- Aligns with value-based care contracts (30% of system revenue)

**Financial profile:**
- Lower per-patient revenue initially (preventive care reimburses less than ED)
- Break-even timeline: 2.5–3 years
- Long-term savings from reduced ED utilization projected after year 3

---

## Current Context

**Board pressure:** Split opinion. Some want "visible impact now," others want "long-term strategy."

**Financial landscape:**
- System margins are thin (2.3% operating margin last year)
- State Medicaid reimbursement rates may decrease 4–6% next fiscal year
- Value-based care contracts incentivize reducing ED utilization

**Regulatory environment:**
- Joint Commission accreditation review in 14 months
- CMS value-based care program: system is in "warning" status on chronic disease metrics; if not improved within 18 months, annual penalties of $1.2–1.8M begin

**Community dynamics:**
- Patient advocacy groups demanding ED improvements
- Underserved neighborhoods organizing for better access to primary care
- Local employers want focus on preventive care to reduce their costs
- City council facing pressure on both issues

**Competitive landscape:**
- Neighboring health system opened urgent care centers (pulling lower-acuity cases)
- For-profit hospital chain exploring market entry
