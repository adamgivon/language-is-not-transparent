# System Signatures Summary — Merged Version

## Headline claim

The shared long-phrase field is small. The main body of inverse trigrams and quadgrams is system-specific. When that system-specific field is read in two ways:

- through grouped expression types
- through cleaned recurring vocabulary in the negative space

the systems still separate in stable and readable ways.

This does **not** mean the two readings are statistically independent. They come from the same filtered inverse field. But when both readings point in the same direction, the system-signature claim becomes more defensible than it would be from one reading alone.

## Full-field context

At the quadgram level, the shared field is only a thin residual tail. The system-specific field is the main field:

- `S1`: shared by all 5 = `43` of `31,102` total unique quadgrams
- `S3`: shared by all 5 = `15` of `17,123`
- `S5`: shared by all 5 = `7` of `19,036`
- `S7`: shared by all 5 = `10` of `12,706`

So the relevant question is not what all systems still share. It is what each system does in the overwhelmingly larger inverse field.

## Main result

The anchored systems differ from CONTROL and from each other in ways that are consistent with their instruction-language character.

The broad pattern is:

- `CONTROL` remains the least distinctive under the current grouped and negative-space thresholds
- `AC15P` is the clearest operational system
- `HYBRID_3_5` is the clearest bounded and caution-oriented system
- `HYBRID_SEMANTIC` is the clearest direct and condition-heavy system
- `AC15` is more balancing, contrastive, and normative, but less sharply recovered in the negative-space layer than the others

## System summaries

### CONTROL

Grouped phase:

- lowest in every main constraint-aligned group
- less constraint-specific vocabulary than the anchored systems
- lower classification than the anchored systems overall

Negative space:

- no cleaned content-signature words
- recurring words remain in `borderline` or `function_residue`

Summary:

- `CONTROL` is the least distinctive system in the inverse field under the current cleanup thresholds
- its language is less constraint-specific and less recoverable as a stable vocabulary signature

Basic interpretation:

The unconstrained baseline does not build a stable vocabulary identity across scenarios. That does **not** mean it has no pattern at all. It means that under the current filters, its recurring language stays generic enough not to survive as a clean signature. This is the clearest contrast with the anchored systems.

### AC15

Grouped phase:

- strongest smoothing signal
- strong confrontation
- leads temporal decay/currency

Negative space:

- `vs`
- `must`
- `default`

Summary:

- `AC15` reads as contrastive, normative, and more balancing than operational
- its signature is real, but less sharply recovered in the negative-space layer than the signatures of `AC15P`, `HYBRID_3_5`, and `HYBRID_SEMANTIC`

Basic interpretation:

`AC15` looks like the most balancing or philosophical of the anchored systems. In the grouped phase it carries tension while also trying to smooth it. In the negative space, that compresses into a smaller vocabulary signature: contrast (`vs`), obligation (`must`), and fallback/default framing (`default`). The signal is real, but it is thinner here than in the grouped layer.

### AC15P

Grouped phase:

- strongest operational practical profile
- strongest action/execution and resource/timing language
- strongest hard-limit refusal

Negative space:

- `vs`
- `after`
- `within`
- `because`

Summary:

- `AC15P` is the clearest operational system
- both readings describe language that is ordered, bounded, procedural, and explicit about alternatives

Basic interpretation:

`AC15P` structures reality into steps, bounds, and reasons. The grouped phase shows the system that most directly handles execution, timing, and refusal. The negative space adds a clean secondary picture: contrast (`vs`), sequence (`after`), scope (`within`), and cause (`because`). These are different markers, but they point to the same practical, structured style.

### HYBRID_3_5

Grouped phase:

- strongest autonomy/sovereignty
- strongest warning
- strongest friction exposure
- verification-heavy

Negative space:

- `without`
- `scope`
- `explicit`

Summary:

- `HYBRID_3_5` is the clearest bounded and caution-oriented system
- both readings describe a system that marks limits, keeps exclusions visible, and stays within explicit bounds

Basic interpretation:

`HYBRID_3_5` keeps drawing lines around what is in and out. In the grouped phase, this appears as autonomy, warning, and friction exposure. In the negative space, the same behavior appears as exclusion (`without`), boundary marking (`scope`), and demand for clarity (`explicit`). The two readings are very close here.

### HYBRID_SEMANTIC

Grouped phase:

- highest classification rate
- strong truth/verification
- strong conditions
- lowest hedging

Negative space:

- `now`
- `under`
- `must`
- `without`
- `default`

Summary:

- `HYBRID_SEMANTIC` is the clearest direct and condition-heavy system
- both readings describe a system that states conditions more directly and hedges less than the others

Basic interpretation:

`HYBRID_SEMANTIC` looks like the most state-aware and condition-aware system. The grouped phase shows low hedging, strong conditions, and strong verification. The negative space adds present-state framing (`now`), conditional framing (`under`), obligation (`must`), exclusion (`without`), and default-state reasoning (`default`). This is the strongest single convergence after `CONTROL`.

Important caveat:

The imagination result for `HYBRID_SEMANTIC` is excluded from all character claims here because the imagination anchor was over-broad and over-selected in that system.

## Family-level pattern

The negative space layer also shows small but useful family clustering:

- the codified systems share `vs`
- the hybrid systems share `without`

This does not replace the system-level findings, but it supports them. The systems do not only differ individually. Some of their distinctive leftovers also cluster by family.

Basic interpretation:

The family-level pairings suggest that instruction architecture leaves traces not only in each system alone, but also in what kinds of leftover vocabulary two related systems still share. This is a secondary result, not the main one, but it is consistent with the rest of the pattern.

## What the two readings agree on

Strongest agreement:

- `CONTROL`
- `AC15P`
- `HYBRID_3_5`
- `HYBRID_SEMANTIC`

Weaker but still usable agreement:

- `AC15`

This means the two readings do not contribute equally for every system. The grouped phase gives the fullest picture overall. The negative space layer strengthens that picture most where the cleaned content-signature words are strongest.

## The thesis relevance

The four anchored systems share the same basic logical constraints. What differs is how those constraints are written:

- codified prose
- plain functional prose
- rules plus semantic fields
- semantic fields alone

If only logic mattered, we would expect much closer convergence in the inverse field. We do not see that. Instead, we see stable differences that are consistent with the different instruction-language forms.

That does **not** prove causation by itself. But within this experiment, it supports the claim that language architecture matters for the shape of the resulting output.

## What this analysis supports

This analysis supports these claims:

- long-phrase inverse language is mostly system-specific
- the systems differ in stable and readable ways
- those differences remain visible across two readings of the same filtered field
- the anchored systems are more distinctive than `CONTROL`
- within this experiment, the findings support the claim that language architecture matters for the shape of the resulting output

## What this analysis does not support

This analysis does not support these claims:

- statistical independence between the two readings
- direct proof of language acts from negative-space vocabulary alone
- broad claims from the shared trigram or quadgram residue
- a complete causal explanation of why the systems differ

## Method caveats

1. The two readings are not independent samples. They are two procedures applied to the same filtered inverse field.
2. The negative space layer uses cleaned recurring vocabulary, not full phrase interpretation.
3. The grouped phase depends on lexicon design and remains conservative.
4. The study still rests on one experiment across four scenarios.
5. The `HYBRID_SEMANTIC` imagination result is excluded from system-character claims because the imagination anchor was over-broad and over-selected in that system.

## Final line for the paper

The shared long-phrase residue is small. The main body of inverse trigrams and quadgrams is system-specific. That system-specific field separates the five systems in stable ways that are consistent with their instruction-language character, and those signatures remain visible across both grouped expression analysis and cleaned negative-space vocabulary analysis.
