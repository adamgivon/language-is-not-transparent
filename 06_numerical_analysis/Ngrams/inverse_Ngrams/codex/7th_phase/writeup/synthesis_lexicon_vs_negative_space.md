# Synthesis: Grouped Phase and Negative Space Layer

## What this document does

This document reads the two completed layers side by side:

- the grouped phase, where inverse trigrams and quadgrams were classified by expression type
- the negative space layer, where the unclassified remainder was cleaned and read through recurring vocabulary signatures

The goal is simple:

- do the two readings describe the same systems in compatible ways?

This is **not** a claim of independent confirmation. Both readings come from the same filtered inverse field. They share the same structural filter and the same underlying material. So the right claim is narrower:

- if both readings point in the same direction, that is stronger than one reading alone
- but it is still two procedures applied to the same field, not two separate samples

## Main convergence pattern

The grouped phase and the negative space layer do not describe the systems in the same language.

- the grouped phase describes **what kind of expression** each system tends to use
- the negative space layer describes **what recurring vocabulary** survives outside the lexicon

Even so, the two readings line up well enough to support a more defensible system-signature claim than either layer could support alone.

The clearest result is this:

- the anchored systems show recognizable signatures in both layers
- CONTROL does not

That is the broadest convergence finding in the material.

## System-by-system synthesis

### CONTROL

Grouped phase:

- lowest in every Layer A constraint group
- less constraint-specific vocabulary than the anchored systems
- mostly generic or cross-cutting language

Negative space layer:

- no cleaned content-signature words
- recurring words remain in `borderline` or `function_residue`

Synthesis:

- very strong convergence

Both readings describe CONTROL as the least distinctive system in the inverse field. The grouped phase shows that it produces less constraint-specific language. The negative space layer shows that even its leftover recurring vocabulary does not survive cleanup as a distinctive signature under the current thresholds. This is the cleanest cross-reading agreement in the data.

### AC15

Grouped phase:

- smoothing lead
- confrontation tied-high
- temporal decay/currency lead
- generally mid-pack in the main constraint groups

Negative space layer:

- `vs`
- `must`
- `default`

Synthesis:

- partial but real convergence

The grouped phase describes AC15 as balancing, somewhat philosophical, and more smoothing-oriented than the others. The negative space layer does not reproduce smoothing directly, but it does recover a small normative and contrastive signature: `vs`, `must`, `default`. That is compatible with a system that frames tradeoffs and positions more than it executes stepwise detail. The convergence is weaker than for AC15P, HYBRID_3_5, or HYBRID_SEMANTIC, but it is still coherent.

### AC15P

Grouped phase:

- strongest operational practical profile
- leads action/execution, resources/timing, and tradeoffs
- strongest hard-limit refusal
- strongest hedging volume

Negative space layer:

- `vs`
- `after`
- `within`
- `because`

Synthesis:

- strong convergence

Both readings describe AC15P as structured, procedural, and bounded. The grouped phase shows operational language and direct refusal. The negative space layer shows sequencing (`after`), scope (`within`), cause (`because`), and contrast (`vs`). These are not the same markers, but they point to the same character: a system that organizes its language around ordered steps, bounded movement, and explicit framing of alternatives.

### HYBRID_3_5

Grouped phase:

- strongest autonomy/sovereignty
- strongest warning
- strongest friction exposure
- verification-heavy

Negative space layer:

- `without`
- `scope`
- `explicit`

Synthesis:

- strong convergence

The grouped phase describes HYBRID_3_5 as cautious, deferring, and boundary-aware. The negative space layer recovers exactly the kind of vocabulary that fits that behavior: exclusion (`without`), scoping (`scope`), and explicit marking (`explicit`). The two readings use different routes, but they clearly describe the same bounded, careful system.

### HYBRID_SEMANTIC

Grouped phase:

- highest classification rate
- strong truth/verification
- strong conditions
- lowest hedging
- confrontational rather than friction-heavy

Negative space layer:

- `now`
- `under`
- `must`
- `without`
- `default`

Synthesis:

- very strong convergence

The grouped phase describes HYBRID_SEMANTIC as direct, low-hedge, and condition-aware. The negative space layer says almost the same thing in vocabulary form: present-state framing (`now`), conditional framing (`under`), force (`must`), exclusion (`without`), and fallback/default logic (`default`). This is one of the strongest cross-reading matches in the analysis.

## Family-level patterns

The negative space layer also reveals small family pairings:

- codified systems share `vs`
- hybrid systems share `without`

These pairings are useful because they do not replace the system-level readings, but they support them.

The grouped phase already suggested family differences:

- codified systems lean more toward operational structure and formal framing
- hybrid systems lean more toward boundedness, conditions, and user-facing caution

So the family pairings in the negative space layer are consistent with the grouped phase rather than competing with it.

## Where the convergence is strongest

Strongest cross-reading agreement:

- CONTROL
- AC15P
- HYBRID_3_5
- HYBRID_SEMANTIC

Weakest but still usable:

- AC15

That does not mean AC15 has no signature. It means the grouped phase gives a fuller account of it than the cleaned negative space layer currently does.

## What this synthesis supports

This synthesis supports these claims:

- the systems are distinguishable in more than one way inside the same filtered inverse field
- the grouped phase and the negative space layer usually point to compatible system characters
- the anchored systems carry clearer signatures than CONTROL
- some family-level pairings survive outside the lexicon as well

## What this synthesis does not support

This synthesis does not support these claims:

- independent confirmation in the statistical sense
- direct proof of governance behavior from vocabulary alone
- a complete account of each system from the negative space layer by itself

The negative space layer is still a limited vocabulary reading. Its value is that it agrees with the grouped reading often enough to strengthen the overall system-signature claim within this experiment.

## Main conclusion

The grouped phase and the negative space layer are not interchangeable, but they are compatible.

The grouped phase shows:

- how each system tends to express constraints

The negative space layer shows:

- what recurring vocabulary remains even where the lexicon does not classify the phrase

Taken together, they support a more defensible final claim:

- the longer-phrase inverse field is not only system-specific
- it is system-specific in stable, readable ways
- and those ways remain visible across two different readings of the same filtered material
