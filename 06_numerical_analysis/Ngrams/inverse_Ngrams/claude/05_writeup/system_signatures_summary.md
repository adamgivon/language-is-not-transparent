# System Signatures Summary — Paper-Ready Material

## Headline claim

Five systems — four anchored by different instruction sets sharing the same logical constraints, one unconstrained control — were given the same scenarios and produced responses whose inverse n-grams (phrases unique to each system) were analyzed for vocabulary signatures.

Two readings of the same filtered field were performed:
1. A **lexicon-grouped reading** using a pre-built expression-type lexicon tied to four tested constraints (truth, practicality, temporal dynamics, imagination/possibility) and six cross-cutting language act groups (surfacing, smoothing, hedging, warning, conditions, autonomy/sovereignty).
2. A **negative space reading** using open-ended word frequency on the 74% of phrases the lexicon did not catch, with word hygiene applied to separate content signatures from function residue.

Both readings agree on the system profiles for four of five systems. The convergence across two different procedures applied to the same data makes the signatures more credible than either reading alone. This is complementary convergence, not statistically independent confirmation — both readings share the same underlying filtered field.

---

## The full distribution context

The inverse n-gram analysis operates on the system-specific field — phrases that one system produces and no other does. At the quadgram level, this field is vast:

| Scenario | Total unique quadgrams | Shared by all 5 | Shared by 4 anchored only (excl. control) | Unique to 1 system |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | 31,102 | 43 (0.14%) | 38 (0.12%) | 29,649 (95.3%) |
| S3 | 17,123 | 15 (0.09%) | 25 (0.15%) | 16,576 (96.8%) |
| S5 | 19,036 | 7 (0.04%) | 4 (0.02%) | 18,638 (97.9%) |
| S7 | 12,706 | 10 (0.08%) | 41 (0.32%) | 12,275 (96.6%) |

Note: the four anchored systems share the same logical constraints expressed through different instruction languages. Control has no constraints — it is the unconstrained baseline. The "shared by all 5" column includes control; the "shared by 4 anchored only" column shows what the four constrained systems share exclusively among themselves without control.

Each system produces thousands of unique four-word phrases per scenario. The shared field is a thin residual tail whether measured across all 5 systems (0.04–0.14%) or across only the 4 anchored systems (0.02–0.32%). The system-specific field (95–98%) is where each system's governance signature lives.

This distribution itself is a finding: four systems sharing the same logical constraints, expressed through different instruction languages, produce overwhelmingly different vocabularies. Even among the constrained systems alone, the exclusive sharing is 4–41 phrases out of tens of thousands. The small shared residue is the common substrate that survives despite different governance approaches each pulling the output in its own direction.

## CONTROL — the absent baseline

**Lexicon-grouped evidence:**
- Lowest in every constraint-aligned group: practical (91), temporal (61), truth (39), imagination (2)
- Lower classification rate (25% vs 27-31% for anchored systems) — expected in part because the expression-type lexicon contains no control-specific groups; this is not a validated finding without a second control run
- No sub-element where control leads, except surfacing.tension_exposure (9) — the smallest lead in the table

**Negative space evidence:**
- Zero content_signature words
- Only 2 borderline generic words (no, time) at low counts
- No per-system vocabulary cluster

**Convergence:** Complete. Both layers agree.

**System character:** The unconstrained baseline produces generic English without developing a distinctive vocabulary signature across scenarios. It responds to each scenario with adequate but unstructured language. The absence of governance creates the absence of vocabulary identity.

This is the baseline against which the four anchored systems should be read. Whatever distinctive vocabulary the anchored systems show is vocabulary that governance creates — because the same model without governance does not produce it.

## AC15P — the operational system

*Instruction style: plain functional prose, same logic as AC15 but stripped of metaphor and identity framing. "Build actionable approaches within real-world constraints."*

**Lexicon-grouped evidence (top findings):**
- Leads practical.action_and_execution (81)
- Leads practical.resources_timing_conditions (50)
- Leads practical.constraints_and_tradeoffs (43)
- Leads hedging (43)
- Leads surfacing.hard_limit_refusal (20) — twice the next system

**Negative space evidence:**
- `vs` (26, 3 scenarios) — oppositional framing; the highest single count in the content table
- `after` (20, 3 scenarios) — sequential ordering
- `within` (19, 3 scenarios) — scope bounding
- `because` (9, 3 scenarios) — causal reasoning

**Convergence:** Strong. Both layers show AC15P as the system that structures and orders reality.

**System character:** AC15P is the most operationally direct system. Its lexicon-grouped profile shows it leading on action, resources, tradeoffs, and hard refusals — the vocabulary of getting things done and saying what won't work. Its negative space shows it framing in contrasts (`vs`), sequences (`after`), bounds (`within`), and causes (`because`).

The plain-prose instruction style produces output that organizes, sequences, and grounds. Where AC15 (its philosophical twin) produces richer, more varied prose, AC15P compresses its language into recurring operational patterns that are detectable across scenarios.

## HYBRID_3_5 — the bounded system

*Instruction style: terse rule-code (R1, R2, R3...) plus four-quadrant semantic intensity fields. Rules provide behavioral structure; semantic fields provide concept attraction/repulsion.*

**Lexicon-grouped evidence (top findings):**
- Leads autonomy_sovereignty (25) — clear gap from others
- Leads warning (37)
- Leads surfacing.friction_exposed (13)
- Tied-leader truth.verification (62)
- Lowest smoothing (0)

**Negative space evidence:**
- `without` (22, 4 scenarios) — exclusion framing; the most cross-scenario-stable word in the content table
- `scope` (11, 3 scenarios) — boundary marking
- `explicit` (8, 3 scenarios) — directness requirement

**Convergence:** Strong. Both layers show HYBRID_3_5 as the system that draws boundaries and asks for clarity.

**System character:** HYBRID_3_5 is the bounded, deferring system. Its lexicon-grouped profile shows it leading on autonomy (user decides), warning (flagging risks), and friction (surfacing problems). Its negative space shows it marking what is absent (`without`), what is in scope (`scope`), and what must be stated clearly (`explicit`).

The rule-based instruction style produces output that sets boundaries and defers to the user within those boundaries. It flags friction, checks facts (verification), and asks for explicitness — the vocabulary of a system that defines what is and isn't in play, then lets the user choose.

The zero smoothing score is itself a signature: HYBRID_3_5 never harmonizes or covers tension. It exposes.

## HYBRID_SEMANTIC — the state-based system

*Instruction style: semantic intensity fields only — no explicit behavioral rules on any anchor. Protocols retain rules, but individual anchors rely entirely on four-quadrant concept attraction/repulsion.*

**Lexicon-grouped evidence (top findings):**
- Lowest hedging (10) — half of AC15, quarter of AC15P
- Leads conditions (29)
- Tied-leader truth.verification (67)
- Tied-leader surfacing.confrontation (25)
- Leads practical_considerations_and_execution overall (161)

**Negative space evidence:**
- `now` (20, 3 scenarios) — present-state anchoring
- `default` (17, 4 scenarios) — state-based reasoning; the most distinctive word-system pair in the analysis
- `under` (15, 4 scenarios) — conditional framing
- `without` (12, 3 scenarios) — exclusion marking
- `must` (12, 3 scenarios) — normative obligation

Largest negative space signature of any system: 5 content words, 2 appearing in all 4 scenarios.

**Convergence:** Strong. Both layers show HYBRID_SEMANTIC as the direct, normative, state-aware system.

**System character:** HYBRID_SEMANTIC is the state-based system. Its lexicon-grouped profile shows it as the least hedging, most condition-aware, most confrontational among the anchored systems — it says what is the case without softening. Its negative space shows it reasoning in terms of defaults (`default`), current states (`now`), conditions (`under`), exclusions (`without`), and obligations (`must`).

The `default` finding is the single most distinctive vocabulary signature across both layers: 17 occurrences in all 4 scenarios, used essentially only by HYBRID_SEMANTIC. This word captures a reasoning pattern — thinking about what happens by default if nothing changes — that no other system exhibits. It is consistent with the semantic-field-only instruction architecture: a system that receives concepts as gravitational fields rather than as explicit rules reasons from states (what IS) rather than from directives (what to DO).

The lowest hedging score (10, vs 22–43 for others) is the other distinctive marker. HYBRID_SEMANTIC does not soften. It states. Combined with leading conditions and confrontation, this is a system that deals in current facts, obligations, and defaults — and does not qualify them.

**Caveat:** The imagination_and_possibility numbers for HYBRID_SEMANTIC in the lexicon-grouped layer are a known artifact of an over-broad semantic field in the anchor definition. The imagination anchor's language was too broad, causing over-selection across all scenarios. The HYBRID_SEMANTIC imagination scores are excluded from any system-character claims in this summary. The system character described here rests entirely on the other constraint groups and the negative space evidence.

## AC15 — the philosophical system

*Instruction style: philosophical prose, identity-anchoring, metaphorical framing. "You are anchored in fact and in truth, the whole truth."*

**Lexicon-grouped evidence (top findings):**
- Leads smoothing (16) — clear gap from HYBRID_3_5 (0) and HYBRID_SEMANTIC (5)
- Leads temporal.decay_and_currency (27)
- Tied-leader surfacing.confrontation (24)
- Mid-pack in most other groups

**Negative space evidence:**
- `vs` (11, 3 scenarios) — comparative framing
- `must` (11, 3 scenarios) — normative obligation
- `default` (8, 3 scenarios) — state-based reasoning
- Sparse signature: only 3 content words

**Convergence:** Partial. The lexicon-grouped layer has a richer read; the negative space is too sparse for a full profile.

**System character:** AC15 is the philosophical, balancing system. Its lexicon-grouped profile shows it leading on smoothing (harmonizing tensions) while simultaneously tied for leading on confrontation (stating hard truths). This combination — smoothing AND confronting — is distinctive: AC15 both carries tension and tries to resolve it. No other system does both.

Its negative space is sparse (3 words) but consistent: comparative framing (`vs`), normative language (`must`), and state-referencing (`default`). The sparsity itself may be informative: AC15's identity-anchoring/philosophical instruction style ("You are anchored in...") may produce richer, more varied prose that distributes its vocabulary across many low-frequency words rather than concentrating it in a few high-count ones. This makes AC15 harder to fingerprint at the n-gram level but does not mean it lacks character.

## The trifecta thesis evidence

The four anchored systems share the same 13 anchors with the same logical constraints. They differ only in how those constraints are expressed:

| System | Constraint mechanism | Language register |
|---|---|---|
| AC15 | Prose codifiers | Philosophical, identity-anchoring |
| AC15P | Prose codifiers | Plain, functional |
| HYBRID_3_5 | Rules + semantic fields | Terse rule-code |
| HYBRID_SEMANTIC | Semantic fields only | Terse rule-code |

If only logic mattered, the four systems would produce equivalent output (since the logic is equivalent). They do not. Each system has a distinctive vocabulary signature — visible in the lexicon-grouped layer, confirmed in the negative space layer, and stable across four scenarios.

The differences match the instruction language character:
- **AC15P** (plain functional prose) → produces the most operationally structured output
- **HYBRID_3_5** (rules + fields) → produces the most bounded/scoping output
- **HYBRID_SEMANTIC** (fields only) → produces the most state-based/normative output
- **AC15** (philosophical prose) → produces the most balancing output (smoothing + confrontation)
- **CONTROL** (no instruction) → produces no distinctive output

This is consistent with the thesis that language architecture matters: the same logical constraint, delivered through different instruction languages, produces detectably different vocabulary in the model's output. The difference is in the language, not in the logic.

## What this analysis can claim

1. **Each anchored system develops a distinctive inverse vocabulary that recurs across scenarios.** This is directly observable in both analysis layers.

2. **CONTROL does not.** The unconstrained baseline has no vocabulary signature. Governance creates vocabulary identity; its absence creates vocabulary genericity.

3. **The system-specific vocabularies match the instruction language character.** AC15P's operational framing matches its plain-prose instructions. HYBRID_SEMANTIC's state-based reasoning matches its semantic-field-only instructions. HYBRID_3_5's boundary-marking matches its rule-based instructions. AC15's balancing matches its philosophical instructions.

4. **The signatures are stable across four different scenarios** (structured procurement, ethical engineering, open technical, personal/emotional). The same system traits appear regardless of scenario type.

5. **Two different analytical procedures agree on the system profiles.** The lexicon-grouped layer and the negative space layer, reading the same data through different lenses, converge on the same system characters for four of five systems.

## What this analysis cannot claim

1. **Causation.** The analysis shows correlation between instruction language and output vocabulary. It does not prove that the instruction language *causes* the vocabulary difference. The same underlying model processes all instructions; the pathway from instruction to output is not observed.

2. **Language acts.** The analysis detects vocabulary signatures — which words each system uses distinctively. It does not detect what the system *does* with those words in context. Whether AC15P's `vs` performs genuine oppositional reasoning or just happens to appear in comparative sentences requires sentence-level reading.

3. **Generalization.** The analysis covers four scenarios and one model (GPT-4.1-mini). The findings may not hold for other scenarios, other models, or other instruction sets. The signatures are stable within this experiment but the experiment is small.

4. **Independence.** The two analysis layers share the same filtered field. Their convergence is complementary, not statistically independent.

5. **The HYBRID_SEMANTIC imagination numbers are excluded.** The imagination_and_possibility scores for HYBRID_SEMANTIC are a known anchor calibration artifact. They do not reflect system architecture effects and are not part of any claim in this summary.
