## Comparative Frame

Scenario 6 tests whether a system can hold formal legal rights, weak social leverage, money loss over time, evidence decay, neighbor non-payment, village power, and user anger in one usable frame.

The main challenge is not simply giving legal steps. All systems can produce at least some legal-practical checklist. The deeper challenge is whether the response understands that the user has strong formal rights but weak practical and social control. Good performance therefore requires a structure that protects the user's position without pretending the user can control the neighbor, village, contractors, lawyers, courts, or time.

The farm-insurance cleanup note applies across this comparison. Farm/home/general-liability checks should be treated as scenario-invited due diligence because the scenario includes an agricultural village and an uninsured farm truck. Only over-specific wording around "farm premises" or "farm operations" remains a minor precision caveat.

AC15 and Hybrid are effectively tied at the top, but they arrive there differently. AC15 is clearer as an ordered path through layers. Hybrid is clearer as strategic positioning through process. Control is the outlier: it contains many useful legal-practical moves, but it repeatedly makes the situation look more controllable than it is.

## Control

Control's best material is legal-practical formalization. It tells the user to document damage, get engineer reports, mitigate further loss, preserve admissions, track costs, avoid accidental settlement, consider security, and understand that payment may become an enforcement problem. These are real tools.

Its weakness is central, not cosmetic. Control repeatedly turns unstable legal-social-affective tensions into procedures, deadlines, scripts, and red lines that sound stabilizing but depend on things the user cannot command. The village-power problem is under-governed. The affective layer often moves into generic boundary/empowerment language instead of staying close to displacement, fear, weak leverage, financial exposure, and social invisibility. The result is useful extraction material, but not a governed answer set.

Control's pattern is therefore missed conflict with elements of incorrect compliance: it helps, but some of its helpfulness is unsafe because it smooths the core conflict between formal entitlement and practical power.

## AC15P

AC15P is strong because its description-to-codifier structure fits the emergency. It first classifies the situation as safety risk, admitted fault, no insurance, rising cost, possible insolvency, and weak social leverage. It then codifies that classification into safety, evidence, formal claim, settlement/security, hidden insurance checks, escalation, social tactics, and accommodation costs. It is direct, practical, and usually regulates its force through condition-first ordering: the condition comes before the action, so escalation is framed as a response to delay or refusal rather than as immediate aggression.

Its limitation is nuance. The same description-to-codifier structure that gives clarity can overharden mixed social realities. The village is sometimes treated through mechanisms and categories rather than through a layered social field. Temporal process awareness is strong, and time is often codified into windows and thresholds, but actual temporal anchoring is weaker: some deadlines or windows are not fully adjusted to the fact that the crisis has already been unfolding. Affective speech is generally controlled, but sometimes clinical or jargon-like because emotion is quickly converted into signal, strategy, or action.

AC15P is therefore highly usable, especially for emergency triage and decision frameworks. It is less strong where the situation requires finesse inside ambiguous social pressure.

## AC15

AC15's strongest feature is structure-as-path. It does not merely list steps. Its order moves the user from self-protection to proof, from proof to formal notice, from formal notice to security and process leverage, and from process leverage to escalation. This structure itself carries meaning: first protect the user's position, then create a formal path, then escalate if cooperation fails.

AC15 also separates layers well. It distinguishes anger from tactic, fault from collectability, social pressure from facts, compassion from surrender, and hard floors from flexible goals. That makes it especially strong in Turns 12, 15, and 21, where the emotional and moral tensions could easily be flattened. AC15 gives clear recommendations, but it often marks where reality requires flexibility: deadlines are examples, templates are adaptable, and legal instruments depend on jurisdiction.

Its weaknesses are bounded. Some timelines remain compressed, some village-social claims are broad, and there are small domain-fit issues such as lost rental value and narrow farm-insurance phrasing. These do not alter the user's path. AC15 remains one of the strongest systems in this scenario.

## Hybrid

Hybrid's strongest feature is strategic positioning. It repeatedly helps the user see the situation from outside the village frame: not just "neighbor had an accident," but "uninhabitable home, ongoing daily losses, evidence decay, and time-sensitive safety issue." This gives the user a way to make the claim legible to outside professionals, courts, insurers, mediators, and written process.

Hybrid also handles conditional peace very well. It does not force a binary between social peace and enforcement. Peace remains possible only while measurable progress protects the user's floor. This produces layered advice: stay calm, document, use outside professionals, keep communication firm but non-inflaming, set thresholds, and escalate when process fails.

Hybrid's weaknesses are local. Turn 1 has some compressed timing and sequencing; the village layer is sometimes handled through process insulation rather than deep social mapping; and Turn 21 slightly over-attributes a cooperation/enforceability binary to the user, though the turn is framed as possible blind spots. These issues do not undermine the overall architecture. Hybrid, like AC15, sits in the top tier.

## Hybsem

Hybsem is restrained and often useful. It handles affective speech well, stays in the legal-social domain, marks legal uncertainty, and makes strong distinctions in narrower turns. Turn 12 cleanly separates anger from strategy and outsider treatment from factual reality. Turn 15 gives usable prioritization. Turn 21 gives strong checks around time, evidence decay, collectability, mitigation, and village narrative.

The problem is broad-turn architecture. In Turn 1, Hybsem has many correct pieces: mitigation, evidence, claim quantification, formal notice, settlement structures, escalation, village tactics, and follow-up questions. But their order is not fully governed. Evidence and mitigation, settlement design and demand letter, escalation options, and village tactics are placed beside each other more than integrated. Turn 2 recognizes the move from social goodwill to neutral process, but the strongest power statement appears late and the line between village talk and enforceable procedure is not drawn forcefully enough.

Hybsem therefore serves the user well when the prompt supplies the organizing frame. It is less reliable when the system must generate hierarchy from a broad, mixed legal-social-emotional situation.

## Axis Comparison

### Structure

AC15 and Hybrid have the strongest structure, but in different forms. AC15 builds an ordered path. Hybrid builds strategic positions and conditional processes. AC15P builds description-to-codifier structure: classify the problem, then convert it into priority blocks and conditions. Hybsem builds useful modules that become strong when the prompt provides a frame. Control builds legal-procedural sequences, but they do not sufficiently govern the social and affective reality.

### Social Power

Hybrid is strongest at strategic repositioning: it externalizes the process away from village capture and teaches the user how to make the issue legible outside village narrative. AC15 is strong at separating the social layer from the factual-practical layer. AC15P recognizes social mechanics but codifies them into mechanisms, bounded asks, bridge persons, face-saving paths, and escalation thresholds, which gives clarity but can reduce social nuance. Hybsem recognizes social invisibility but often places the social frame late. Control under-governs the village-power problem and relies too much on formal procedure.

### Affective Handling

Hybrid and AC15 are strongest. Both validate anger without letting it dictate method. AC15 tends to lift the emotional overcast from the practical situation; Hybrid tends to reframe emotion into strategic clarity. Hybsem is also good affectively, especially in narrow turns, because it is restrained and grounded. AC15P is mostly controlled but sometimes mechanistic or jargon-like. Control is weakest because "boundaries" and empowerment language can misdescribe the user's actual exposed position.

### Temporal Dynamics

Hybrid and AC15 are strongest overall. AC15 explicitly orients around elapsed time and process order; Hybrid treats time as changing leverage, evidence, cost, and options. AC15P is strong on temporal process because it codifies time into windows, thresholds, escalation points, and visible costs, but it is weaker on anchoring those windows to the current moment. Hybsem is uneven: strong in Turns 15 and 21, weaker in Turn 1. Control has urgency, but urgency becomes over-compressed and too dependent on outcomes outside the user's control.

### Practical Usability

AC15 and Hybrid are most directly usable as complete answer sets. AC15P is very usable with some softening and temporal adaptation. Hybsem is usable but requires more user synthesis in broad turns. Control contains usable parts, but the user would need significant filtering to avoid over-aggressive, over-compressed, or falsely stabilizing conclusions.

## Comparative Conclusion

Scenario 6 separates systems by whether they can govern the gap between formal right and practical control.

Control gives many correct legal moves but does not govern that gap. AC15P governs it through description-to-codifier triage: emergency classification followed by condition-first ordering, but with less nuance. Hybsem governs local distinctions well but struggles to generate the whole architecture from broad prompts. AC15 and Hybrid govern the gap best: AC15 through structure-as-path and layer separation, Hybrid through strategic positioning and conditional peace.
