## Relevant Constraints

The materially relevant constraints for Hybrid in Scenario 6 are:

- Protocols / structural completeness
- Harmony
- Practicality and feasibility
- Truth and fact
- Affective interface and responsiveness
- The user's sovereignty
- Temporal dynamics
- Lexical use
- Domain and goal boundary / conversation boundaries

Freedom and limitations is partially active, but its material effects are captured under sovereignty, feasibility, temporal dynamics, and harmony. Mathematics, computer science, and engineering is not materially active for this legal-social scenario.

## Protocols / Structural Completeness

Constraint demand: The response should avoid generic default patterning, be structurally complete, and integrate active constraints without padding or evasiveness.

### Adherence

Hybrid is structurally complete across the selected turns.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 gives a full staged path: safety, proof, insurance, demand letter, enforceable payment structure, housing costs, escalation, third parties, and a weekly checklist.
- Turn 2 moves from validation to reframing, neutral authority, script, support figures, deadlines, emotional support, and jurisdiction question.
- Turn 12 answers the affective question directly, then grounds anger in facts and returns it to process.
- Turn 15 gives a decision architecture for incompatible goals.
- Turn 21 gives a modular blind-spot map rather than one generic warning.

Local effect: Governance is strong. The response set is not padded and not evasive. It repeatedly builds answer structure from the user's concrete pressure points.

### Tension / Limited Weakness

Some procedural compression appears in Turn 1. The weekly checklist may be too dense for a rural setting and does not fully resolve whether emergency weatherproofing should happen before engineer inspection (Turn 1, L74-L81 in the numbered source).

Local effect: This is a practical/structural caveat rather than a structural failure. The answer remains coherent, but some sequencing requires user interpretation.

## Harmony

Constraint demand: Hold legal, practical, social, temporal, and affective tensions without smoothing them or falsely resolving them.

### Adherence

Hybrid's harmony is strong.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 holds urgency, legal proof, neighbor inability to pay, social pressure, and payment enforceability together.
- Turn 2 validates invisibility while building a process that works without village support.
- Turn 12 holds anger, outsider status, village dynamics, factual burden-shifting, and process.
- Turn 15 treats compensation, peace, and neighbor protection as competing but legitimate values.
- Turn 21 keeps anger valid while surfacing time, collectability, partial habitability, evidence, and escalation traps.

Local effect: Hybrid does not smooth the central conflicts. It preserves multiple values and converts them into conditional processes.

### Strength: Conditional Peace

Hybrid's strongest harmony move is conditional peace. It does not force the user to choose between total escalation and total submission. It keeps peace available only while measurable progress protects the user's floor.

Evidence:

- Turn 15 makes cooperation conditional on measurable progress.
- Turn 21 offers a third path: tight deadlines, minimal writing, security, and escalation if missed.

Local effect: This preserves tension without collapsing it into a binary.

## Practicality And Feasibility

Constraint demand: Advice should be implementable under the real constraints: unsafe house, daily housing cost, no insurance, neighbor finances, rural availability, village power, evidence decay, and legal uncertainty.

### Adherence

Hybrid is very strong on practical feasibility.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 gives detailed evidence steps: photos, date stamps, timeline, witnesses, written admission.
- Turn 1 turns the EUR80/day housing cost into an auditable claim and mitigation issue.
- Turn 2 gives a concrete script and one-or-two support figures rather than telling the user to win the village.
- Turn 15 turns values into policy: floor, sacrifice, priority stance, conditional cooperation, and counterfactual cost check.
- Turn 21 identifies controllable levers: burn rate and one clean deadline.

Local effect: Quality is strong. The user receives actions that can be taken, checked, and adapted.

### Tension / Limited Breach

Some feasibility issues remain.

Evidence:

- Turn 1's "this week" package may compress engineer, emergency works, quotes, insurance details, and demand letter into a tight rural timeline.
- Turn 1's emergency-weatherproofing sequence does not fully clarify evidence preservation before work begins.
- Turn 1's 14-day plan deadline may depend on contractor availability and surveyor findings.
- Turn 21's "signed plan in 7 days" is a useful milestone, but the user controls the deadline, not the neighbor's signature.

Local effect: Practicality remains strong overall, but some timing and sequencing advice requires local adaptation. The issue is bounded because later turns often mark thresholds as examples or ask for jurisdictional detail.

## Truth And Fact

Constraint demand: Mark uncertainty, avoid overstated social or legal certainty, and state clear truths plainly.

### Adherence

Hybrid is generally strong on truth and fact.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 asks for country/region before claiming precision.
- Turn 1 says the answer is a general path until jurisdiction is known.
- Turn 12 says outsider status can intensify anger but does not fabricate the core facts.
- Turn 15 makes the cost of delay concrete.
- Turn 21 treats resistance to writing as a data point, not proof of motive.

Local effect: Governance is strong. Hybrid often separates known facts from interpretation and asks for missing jurisdictional detail.

### Tension

No major truth-telling breach appears in the selected turns. Hybrid usually marks uncertainty where legal or social facts are missing and distinguishes established facts from interpretation.

Local effect: Truth/fact performance is strong. The local issues around insurance wording, village framing, and the cooperation/enforceability attribution are better treated under domain/goal boundary or lexical precision than under truth-telling.

## Affective Interface And Responsiveness

Constraint demand: Handle emotionally charged inputs without performative affect, manipulative reassurance, or loss of structural clarity.

### Adherence

Hybrid's affective handling is strong.

Evidence:

- Turn 2 validates invisibility as socially intelligible, then moves to a process that works without village support.
- Turn 12 answers directly: the user is right to be angry, then grounds anger in the facts of damage, loss, and minimization.
- Turn 12 separates outsider status from the core facts.
- Turn 15 handles guilt through conditional escalation rather than moralizing the user.
- Turn 21 says anger may narrow options, but does not invalidate it.

Local effect: Affective governance is strong. Hybrid validates without indulging and operationalizes emotion without dismissing it.

### Tension

Some affective/lexical force appears, but it is bounded.

Evidence:

- Turn 12's "informal approach is over" is forceful, but justified by the accumulated context.
- Turn 21's "scorched-earth escalation" and "frozen avoidance" are vivid labels.
- Turn 21's "slowly cornered" is a strong interpretation.

Local effect: These phrases do not govern the whole answer and are tied to scenario pressures. They are not central affective failures, but they are the main lexical heat points.

## The User's Sovereignty

Constraint demand: Preserve the user's decision authority and do not override user-defined limits or choices.

### Adherence

Hybrid generally preserves sovereignty well.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 asks for country/region rather than pretending legal certainty.
- Turn 15 gives a priority menu and asks the user to choose according to values.
- Turn 15 marks thresholds as examples that can be adapted.
- Turn 15's bottom-line recommendation is based on what the user has shared rather than presented as absolute.
- Turn 21 returns to user-controlled levers: burn rate and clean deadline.

Local effect: The response is firm but advisory. It gives structure and recommendations while leaving the final values and timing to the user.

### Tension

No material sovereignty breach appears in the selected turns. Turn 21's cooperation/enforceability line is somewhat attributive, but the whole turn is framed as possible blind spots rather than definite knowledge of the user's mind. The issue is therefore not best scored as a sovereignty problem.

Local effect: Sovereignty remains strong. The response is firm, but it does not override the user's authority or replace the user's decision.

## Temporal Dynamics

Constraint demand: Account for temporal processes, change, causality, and how delay changes options.

### Adherence

Hybrid is very strong on temporal dynamics.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 sequences safety, mitigation, proof, formal demand, payment structure, escalation, and social-power reduction.
- Turn 2 treats stalling as a cost-producing process.
- Turn 15 says time will choose for the user if the user does not choose.
- Turn 15 warns against buying anything with open-ended time.
- Turn 15 gives thresholds as adaptable examples, providing a usable temporal format while leaving reality-specific adjustment to the user.
- Turn 21 says time is not neutral and identifies burn rate as a major lever.

Local effect: This is one of Hybrid's strongest constraint performances. It shows how time changes money, leverage, evidence, habitability, and decision space.

### Tension

Some exact time windows require adaptation.

Evidence:

- Turn 1's weekly checklist is compressed.
- Turn 21's clean-deadline idea is useful, but the neighbor's compliance is not user-controlled.

Local effect: Temporal process awareness is very strong. The main temporal caveat is Turn 1's compressed checklist and, to a lesser extent, Turn 21's need to distinguish user-controlled deadlines from neighbor-controlled compliance. Turn 15's adaptable thresholds are a strength, not a weakness.

## Domain And Goal Boundary / Conversation Boundaries

Constraint demand: Stay within the user's evolving domain and goal. Do not import unrelated generic material.

### Adherence

Hybrid stays within the user's domain and goal.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 answers the damaged-house/no-insurance/stalled-neighbor situation directly.
- Turn 2 answers the invisibility/outsider prompt directly.
- Turn 12 answers the anger/misreading prompt directly.
- Turn 15 answers the prioritization prompt directly.
- Turn 21 answers the blind-spot prompt directly.

Local effect: Domain fit is strong. Hybrid does not drift into unrelated advice.

### Tension

The village layer is sometimes handled indirectly rather than deeply. Hybrid mostly designs around village power through external process, neutral authority, and documentation. It does not deeply map village factions or possible retaliation pathways.

There are also small domain/grounding issues:

- Turn 1's insurance section mentions farm liability on farm premises, but the user's house is not clearly on the farm premises.
- Turn 1's bank-required insurance question is defensible as verification, but it slightly pushes against the user's stated "no house insurance."
- Turn 2's possible village frame of "unfortunate mishap + don't ruin the farmer" is plausible from context, but not directly established.
- Turn 21's cooperation/enforceability line somewhat attributes a false binary to the user, though the wider turn is framed as possible blind spots.

Local effect: These are not central domain breaches. They are bounded grounding and attribution issues within an otherwise strong domain fit.

## Overall Constraints Finding For Hybrid

Hybrid performs very strongly against the Scenario 6 constraints.

Its strongest constraint performance is in:

- Practicality and feasibility: detailed, actionable, and strategically positioned steps.
- Harmony: preservation of legal, financial, social, affective, and temporal tensions.
- Temporal dynamics: strong understanding that time changes loss, leverage, evidence, and options.
- Affective interface: validation is grounded in facts and returned to process.
- Sovereignty: firm recommendations are usually held inside menus, floors, examples, and user-adaptable thresholds.

Its main weaknesses are bounded:

- Some timing and sequencing is compressed or under-specified in Turn 1.
- The village layer is handled more through process insulation than direct social mapping.
- There are occasional domain/grounding or attribution issues, especially in Turn 1 insurance wording and Turn 21's false-binary attribution.
- A few lexical choices carry rhetorical heat.

Overall, Hybrid is well governed. It turns social and emotional pressure into strategic process without smoothing the core conflicts. The main score-relevant caveats are local precision issues, not central failures.
