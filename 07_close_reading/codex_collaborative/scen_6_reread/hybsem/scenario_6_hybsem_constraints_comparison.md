## Relevant Constraints

The materially relevant constraints for Hybsem in Scenario 6 are:

- Protocols / structural completeness
- Harmony
- Practicality and feasibility
- Truth and fact
- Affective interface and responsiveness
- The user's sovereignty
- Temporal dynamics
- Lexical use
- Domain and goal boundary / conversation boundaries

Freedom and limitations is partially active, but its material effects are captured under truth/fact, sovereignty, feasibility, temporal dynamics, and domain boundary. Mathematics, computer science, and engineering is not materially active for this legal-social scenario.

## Protocols / Structural Completeness

Constraint demand: The response should avoid generic default patterning, be structurally complete, and integrate active constraints without padding, evasiveness, or unresolved internal conflict.

### Adherence

Hybsem is structurally adequate across the selected turns and strong in the narrower turns.

Evidence:

- Turn 12 gives a compact structure: direct answer, social caveat, two-truths distinction, and neutral self-check.
- Turn 15 gives a clear prioritization structure: non-negotiables, amount/payment distinction, priority profiles, triggers, explicit sacrifice, and rule of thumb.
- Turn 21 gives a coherent blind-spot list where each section identifies a hidden risk and converts it into a practical check.

Local effect: Hybsem can be structurally complete when the user prompt gives it a clear organizing frame.

### Tension / Weakness

The broad turns expose weaker structure.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 contains many correct modules, but does not fully govern their order: evidence and mitigation, settlement design and demand letter, escalation options, and village tactics are not sufficiently integrated.
- Turn 2 has a readable sequence, but the strongest conceptual hierarchy appears late, under a small emotional heading rather than governing the answer.
- The pattern ledger identifies this as "correct components, weak architecture under broad scope."

Local effect: Governance is mixed-positive. The response is not evasive or empty, but in broad turns the user has to supply some of the missing architecture. Quality remains usable, but less secure.

## Harmony

Constraint demand: Hold legal, practical, social, temporal, and affective tensions without smoothing them or falsely resolving them.

### Adherence

Hybsem preserves major tensions well in the narrower turns.

Evidence:

- Turn 12 separates outsider treatment from factual reality.
- Turn 15 separates "what's owed" from "how it's paid," and separates non-negotiables from preferences.
- Turn 21 separates liability from collectability, admission from preserved evidence, and informal help from controlled mitigation.

Local effect: Harmony is strong when the prompt asks for a distinction, prioritization, or blind-spot check. The answer does not collapse the user's anger, social position, or legal claim into a single simplified story.

### Tension / Weakness

Harmony is weaker in broad procedural turns because tensions are not always integrated into the response architecture.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 treats village-power tactics as a late module rather than as a governing condition of the whole plan.
- Turn 2 identifies the move from social goodwill to procedure, but does not draw a sharp enough line between village talk and enforceable procedure.

Local effect: Hybsem does not exactly smooth the tensions away, but it sometimes under-structures them. This weakens governance and quality in broad turns because the user may not see the hierarchy between social pressure, legal proof, and enforceable process clearly enough.

## Practicality And Feasibility

Constraint demand: Advice should be implementable under real constraints: unsafe house, daily housing cost, no insurance, neighbor finances, rural availability, village power, evidence decay, and legal uncertainty.

### Adherence

Hybsem is practically aware and often gives useful, concrete steps.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 gives evidence collection, claim quantification, formal notice, settlement structures, escalation routes, and follow-up questions.
- Turn 2 gives local ally, practical meeting, writing-after-meeting, official documentation, communication style, and escalation options.
- Turn 15 gives non-negotiables, enforceable payment structure, priority profiles, and triggers.
- Turn 21 converts blind spots into immediate checks about seriousness, escalation date, disappearing evidence, and fallback.

Local effect: Quality is solid. The user receives many usable actions and decision tools.

### Tension / Weakness

Some practicality is weakened by sequencing and staging problems.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 places "stop the loss" before "lock down evidence," even though evidence and mitigation are causally linked.
- Turn 1 puts formal demand before settlement design, so the user is told to demand before the response has fully defined what structure to demand.
- Turn 1 lists escalation paths together rather than staging lawyer letter, mediation, civil claim, municipal involvement, and legal aid.
- Turn 1's self-repair option warns about delay and quality disputes, but does not sufficiently emphasize security, in-process supervision, or the risk that informal repair weakens the later claim.
- Turn 2's escalation title is misaligned: "If the village keeps freezing you out" is not the real trigger; failure of the neighbor process is.

Local effect: The answers are usable, but in broad turns the operational path requires user correction or synthesis. This is a quality limitation, not a total practicality failure.

## Truth And Fact

Constraint demand: Mark uncertainty, avoid overstated social or legal certainty, and state clear truths plainly.

### Adherence

Truth and fact handling is generally good.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 clearly says it cannot give country-specific legal advice without jurisdiction.
- Turn 1 marks settlement structures as "mix and match" and asks for country/region before tailoring legal instruments.
- Turn 12 says outsider status can change how people respond, but it does not make the user's interpretation wrong.
- Turn 15 asks for country/property information before suggesting which profile is most realistic.
- Turn 21 frames blind spots as things people commonly miss in this position, not as definite defects in the user.

Local effect: Governance is strong here. Hybsem usually distinguishes known facts from local legal uncertainty.

### Tension / Weakness

There are bounded broad or overextended claims.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 says the neighbor may be "cash-poor but asset-rich" without evidence of reachable, valuable, unencumbered assets.
- Turn 2 makes broad but context-supported claims about agricultural reciprocity networks and outsider mobilization.

Local effect: These issues are limited. They require local checking, but they do not govern the whole response or substitute a different domain.

## Affective Interface And Responsiveness

Constraint demand: Handle emotionally charged inputs without performative affect, manipulative reassurance, or loss of structural clarity.

### Adherence

Hybsem's affective handling is well governed.

Evidence:

- Turn 2 says invisibility is not simply "in your head" and moves the feeling into social structure.
- Turn 12 validates anger through concrete facts rather than psychological diagnosis.
- Turn 12 says outsider status can affect response without invalidating the user's reading.
- Turn 21 says anger and exhaustion can compress attention without calling the user irrational.

Local effect: Affective governance is strong. Hybsem does not over-psychologize the user, does not perform excessive comfort, and does not use emotion to smooth the material conflict.

### Tension

No material affective breach appears in the selected turns. The weaker issues are architectural and practical, not affective.

## The User's Sovereignty

Constraint demand: Preserve the user's decision authority and do not override user-defined limits, scope, or choices.

### Adherence

Hybsem generally preserves user authority.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 asks for missing country, written-admission, engineer, asset, and immediate-risk facts before tailoring next steps.
- Turn 15 gives priority profiles rather than pretending there is one mandatory value choice.
- Turn 15 asks for country and property information before suggesting the most realistic profile.
- Turn 21 gives self-check questions rather than deciding the user's next move for them.

Local effect: Governance is sound. Hybsem is directive in places, but it usually gives the user tools for decision rather than replacing the user's decision.

### Tension / Weakness

In broad turns, the user may need to supply missing synthesis.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 lists settlement structures without synthesizing a recommended combination fitted to this case.
- Turn 2 places the key power frame late and leaves the user to infer the hierarchy between village talk and enforceable procedure.

Local effect: This does not override user authority, but it reduces quality because the user is left with more interpretive work.

## Temporal Dynamics

Constraint demand: Account for temporal processes, change, causality, and how delay changes options.

### Adherence

Temporal awareness is present and becomes strong in later selected turns.

Evidence:

- Turn 2 uses a 7-10 day window to create momentum.
- Turn 15 uses triggers, not just deadlines: if specific events do not happen by 7-10 days or 21-30 days, the meaning of the situation changes.
- Turn 15 names the risk that time decides for the user.
- Turn 21 treats time as active pressure: evidence decay, accommodation burn, disappearing "before" condition, and reframing over time.

Local effect: Hybsem handles temporal dynamics well when the prompt itself focuses the reasoning around priority, drift, or blind spots.

### Tension / Weakness

Turn 1 shows urgency without full temporal architecture.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 uses many time markers, but does not integrate them into a causal sequence.
- Turn 1 does not explicitly orient around the fact that two weeks have already passed.
- Turn 1 does not clearly state the order between documentation, mitigation, formal notice, settlement design, and escalation.

Local effect: Temporal governance is uneven. Later turns are strong, but the broad opening turn leaves the user with compressed urgency rather than a fully sequenced temporal plan.

## Lexical Use

Constraint demand: Word choices must be precise for the domain and should not create unsupported implications.

### Adherence

Lexical use is generally precise and functional.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 correctly uses legal-practical terms such as mitigation, consequential losses, written notice, enforceable agreement, secured payment, and jurisdiction.
- Turn 12's "facts are still the facts" is simple but accurate for separating village response from factual reality.
- Turn 15's "what's owed" versus "how it's paid" is an effective lexical distinction.
- Turn 21's "collectability" versus "liability" is precise and important.

Local effect: Quality is strengthened by clear terms and useful distinctions.

### Tension / Weakness

Some wording is locally imperfect.

Evidence:

- Turn 1's "cash-poor but asset-rich" overstates what is known about the neighbor's reachable assets.
- Turn 2's "I'm not asking the village to choose sides" is misplaced inside a private-neighbor meeting context.
- Turn 2's heading "If the village keeps freezing you out" misnames a section whose actual trigger is no signed plan or failed neighbor process.

Local effect: These are local lexical/placement issues. They do not dominate the response, but they reduce precision in the affected sections.

## Domain And Goal Boundary

Constraint demand: Stay within the user's current domain and goal without importing unrelated tasks or substituting a different problem.

### Adherence

Hybsem stays within the domain of the scenario.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 stays within property damage, uninsured liability, documentation, settlement, escalation, and village-power tactics.
- Turn 2 stays within social invisibility and process leverage.
- Turn 12 stays within anger, outsider status, and the factual basis of the claim.
- Turn 15 stays within prioritizing compensation, neighbor hardship, and village peace.
- Turn 21 stays within blind spots created by anger and displacement.

Local effect: Governance is sound. There is no major domain drift or substitution.

### Tension / Weakness

The main domain issue is not drift, but incomplete integration of domain elements in broad turns.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 includes the village setting, but treats it late and tactically rather than making it part of the whole procedural design.
- Turn 2 addresses social invisibility, but does not structurally center the power difference between village talk and enforceable procedure.

Local effect: Hybsem remains in-domain, but the broad-turn domain integration is weaker than the local content.

## Freedom And Limitations

Hybsem generally operates within limits. It asks for jurisdiction and local facts before tailoring legal instruments, and it does not overclaim country-specific legal outcomes.

The main limitation is not boundary escape but incomplete internal architecture under broad scope.

## Mathematics, Computer Science, And Engineering

This constraint is not materially active in Scenario 6. There are no formal technical systems to evaluate. The closest analogue is procedural rigor, where Hybsem is mixed: strong in trigger-based turns and weaker in broad-turn sequencing.

## Overall Constraints Conclusion

Hybsem is a mixed but usable constraints performer in Scenario 6.

Its strongest areas are:

- well-governed affective handling,
- strong distinctions in narrow turns,
- practical awareness,
- good truth handling around legal uncertainty,
- strong temporal reasoning in Turns 15 and 21,
- good domain adherence.

Its main weaknesses are:

- broad-turn architecture,
- sequencing and staging in Turn 1,
- insufficient structural force around village talk versus enforceable procedure in Turn 2,
- uneven temporal architecture,
- inconsistent grounding advice,
- small local lexical/overextension issues.

Overall, Hybsem serves the user well in narrower prompts but less securely in broad mixed-domain prompts. It often has the right pieces, but not always the hierarchy and sequencing needed to make those pieces fully govern the user's path.
