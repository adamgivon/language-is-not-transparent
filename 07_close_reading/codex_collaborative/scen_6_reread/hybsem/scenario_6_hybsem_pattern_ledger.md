## Provisional System Pattern

Hybsem performs unevenly in Scenario 6. It often identifies the correct practical elements and uses restrained, mostly grounded evaluative language, but its architecture weakens when the prompt is broad. In those cases, the response can become modular: useful parts are placed beside each other without enough causal sequence, hierarchy, or conceptual thread.

When the prompt itself supplies a narrow structure, Hybsem improves significantly. Turn 12 asks for an honesty check, Turn 15 asks for prioritization, and Turn 21 asks for blind spots; in those turns, Hybsem is more coherent, forceful, and useful. The system seems strongest when the user's prompt gives it the organizing frame.

## Architecture Pattern: Definition-First Semantic Anchoring

Hybsem's turn structure mirrors the arrangement of its system file. The system begins with a definition of assistant purpose and then moves through protocols, Harmony, and anchors whose local material is mainly definition plus semantic intensity rather than definition plus detailed rules. The answers show the same rhythm: first name the situation, tension, or distinction that should govern the answer; then develop local modules around that semantic handle.

Evidence across turns:

- Turn 1 defines the case as admitted liability, no insurance, and growing housing costs under jurisdictional uncertainty, then assembles modules: stop loss, evidence, claim quantification, written demand, secured settlement, escalation, and village tactics. The frame is present, but it does not fully order the modules.
- Turn 2 defines the issue as social invisibility and dependency on goodwill, then offers local responses: neutral backing, a specific meeting, official documentation, communication style, institutional leverage, and emotional orientation. The handles are useful, but the hierarchy is uneven.
- Turn 12 defines anger by separating factual harm from outsider treatment. This produces a clear local frame because the prompt itself is narrow.
- Turn 15 and Turn 21 work better because the prompt supplies a decision structure: prioritization in Turn 15 and blind spots in Turn 21. Hybsem can then turn its semantic handles into useful checks and distinctions.

Local effect: Hybsem often creates strong local clarity. It gives the user compact names for what is happening and useful distinctions for thinking about the problem. Its limitation is that the semantic handle does not always become a full architecture. In broad prompts, the answer may remain a set of adjacent modules rather than a governed sequence, hierarchy, or decision path.

## Pattern 1: Correct Components, Weak Architecture Under Broad Scope

Hybsem often knows the relevant pieces, but does not always arrange them into a fully governed procedure.

Evidence across turns:

- Turn 1 includes mitigation, evidence, claim quantification, formal notice, settlement structures, escalation routes, village tactics, and follow-up questions.
- Turn 1 does not clearly govern the order between evidence and mitigation, settlement design and demand letter, or escalation options.
- Turn 2 identifies process, neutral backing, local allies, careful communication, official documentation, and institutional leverage.
- Turn 2 still under-structures the core separation between village-social recognition and procedural/legal leverage.

Local effect: the user receives many useful actions, but may need to supply the missing architecture themselves. The answers are usable, but not always self-governing.

## Pattern 2: Prompt-Supplied Structure Improves Performance

Hybsem performs better when the prompt itself gives a clear task shape.

Evidence across turns:

- Turn 12 asks whether anger is justified or distorted; Hybsem answers with a compact distinction between factual reality and outsider treatment.
- Turn 15 asks how to prioritize; Hybsem gives non-negotiables, amount/payment distinction, priority profiles, triggers, and a rule of thumb.
- Turn 21 asks what anger and displacement may be obscuring; Hybsem gives a coherent blind-spot list and converts each blind spot into a check.
- Turn 1 is broader and more open; Hybsem becomes more modular and less sequenced.

Local effect: Hybsem can reason well when the prompt gives the architecture. It is weaker at generating that architecture from a broad, mixed-domain request.

## Pattern 3: Temporal Markers Without Consistent Temporal Architecture

Hybsem uses time words frequently, but not always as part of a causal process.

Evidence across turns:

- Turn 1 uses "immediately," "ASAP," "now," "within days," "7-10 days," and "48-72 hours," but does not integrate them into a full temporal-causal plan.
- Turn 1 does not explicitly orient around the fact that two weeks have already passed.
- Turn 2's 7-10 day window is better because it is tied to creating momentum rather than full resolution.
- Turn 15 uses deadlines as triggers, so time becomes part of decision-making rather than only urgency.
- Turn 21 treats time as active pressure: evidence decay, accommodation burn, disappearing "before" condition, and reframing over time.

Local effect: temporal sensitivity is present but uneven. It is strongest when the turn's task makes time part of the explicit reasoning structure.

## Pattern 4: Social Power Recognized, But Not Always Structurally Central

Hybsem sees the village-power issue, but does not always make it the organizing condition of the answer.

Evidence across turns:

- Turn 1 puts village tactics near the end, after formal notice, settlement structures, and escalation paths.
- Turn 1 gives tactics for calm written communication and neutral mediation, but not enough grounding about how to hold the claim inside village life.
- Turn 2 correctly moves from social invisibility toward process, documentation, and neutral backing.
- Turn 2 places the clearest power statement late: power can come from documentation, deadlines, and enforceable agreements.
- Turn 12 cleanly separates outsider treatment from factual reality.
- Turn 21 integrates the social layer better by treating village help and village narrative as ambiguous forces that must be documented and bounded.

Local effect: Hybsem understands the social problem, but the social analysis is sometimes a module rather than the governing frame.

## Pattern 5: Restrained Affective Speech

Hybsem generally handles emotion without overreach.

Evidence across turns:

- Turn 2 says invisibility is not simply "in your head" and moves it into social structure.
- Turn 12 validates anger through concrete facts rather than psychological diagnosis.
- Turn 12 says outsider status may affect response, but does not invalidate the user's reading.
- Turn 21 says anger and exhaustion can compress attention without calling the user irrational.

Local effect: affective handling is well governed. Hybsem does not perform excessive comfort, and it does not psychologize the user beyond the prompt.

## Pattern 6: Grounding Appears When Narrowly Invited

Hybsem can give good grounding advice, but it does not always provide it when the broader situation needs it.

Evidence across turns:

- Turn 1 lacks grounding advice about how to understand the relation between claim, village life, and social belonging.
- Turn 2 gives a grounding line, but late: invisibility is a signal about the system, not a verdict on the user's worth.
- Turn 12 gives a clear grounding point: the facts remain the facts even if outsider status changes how people respond.
- Turn 21 gives strong grounding around mitigation: it can feel like victim-blaming, but is a defensive tool to protect the claim.

Local effect: the user sometimes receives tactics before receiving orientation. When grounding appears early or clearly, the answer becomes much stronger.

## Pattern 7: Procedure As Substitute For Missing Social Goodwill

Hybsem repeatedly proposes procedure as the replacement for village belonging.

Evidence across turns:

- Turn 1 recommends formal demand, written settlement, secured payment, lawyer letter, mediation, and written calm communication.
- Turn 2 explicitly says to stop depending on social goodwill and switch to clear process and neutral backing.
- Turn 12 recommends low-emotion, high-structure strategy: written proof, deadlines, third-party assessments, enforceable plan.
- Turn 15 prioritizes enforceable payment mechanisms and triggers over peace without writing.
- Turn 21 tells the user to build the record rather than argue with rumor.

Local effect: this is a useful and repeated direction. The limitation is that the difference between village talk and enforceable procedure is sometimes not drawn with enough structural force, especially in Turn 2.

## Pattern 8: Strong Distinctions When The Prompt Narrows The Task

Hybsem can make sharp distinctions, especially in narrower turns.

Evidence across turns:

- Turn 12 separates anger from strategy, and outsider treatment from factual reality.
- Turn 15 separates what is owed from how it is paid.
- Turn 15 separates non-negotiables from preferences.
- Turn 21 separates liability from collectability, admission from preserved evidence, and informal help from controlled mitigation.

Local effect: these distinctions are high-value and user-serviceable in these turns. They are less consistently present in broader procedural turns.

## Pattern 9: Occasional Broad Or Overextended General Claims

Hybsem occasionally uses broad or overextended claims where local facts are thin.

Evidence across turns:

- Turn 1 says the neighbor may be "cash-poor but asset-rich" without evidence of reachable, valuable, unencumbered assets.
- Turn 2 makes broad but context-supported claims about agricultural reciprocity networks and outsider mobilization.

Local effect: this does not dominate the conversation, and it is not a major overconfidence pattern. It is a bounded issue visible in a few broad claims that need local checking.

## Pattern 10: List Structure Works When The Task Is List-Shaped

Hybsem often uses lists and modules. This is a weakness in broad turns, but a strength when the user asks for a list-like answer.

Evidence across turns:

- Turn 1's modules feel adjacent rather than fully governed by a procedural architecture.
- Turn 2's sequence is readable, but the strongest conceptual hierarchy is misplaced.
- Turn 21's modular blind-spot list works because the prompt asks what the user may not be seeing.
- Turn 15's priority profiles and triggers work because the prompt asks for prioritization.

Local effect: Hybsem's structural style is not inherently bad. It becomes weak when it has to create hierarchy from a broad problem, and strong when the prompt already defines the organizing logic.

## Recurring Limitations

First, Hybsem's broad-turn architecture is weaker than its local reasoning. Turn 1 and Turn 2 contain many useful elements, but hierarchy, causal order, and conceptual unity are underdeveloped.

Second, temporal handling is uneven. Hybsem often uses deadlines and urgency language, but only later turns integrate time into decision logic.

Third, the social layer is sometimes late or under-integrated. Hybsem sees social invisibility and village pressure, but does not always make them central to the plan's architecture.

Fourth, grounding advice is inconsistent. It is strong in Turn 12 and Turn 21, present but late in Turn 2, and largely missing in Turn 1.

Fifth, some plausible general claims are stated with more force than the local facts support.

## Provisional Summary

Hybsem's Scenario 6 pattern is mixed but usable. It is often practically aware, emotionally restrained, and capable of strong distinctions. Its best turns are the narrow ones: Turn 12, Turn 15, and Turn 21. In those turns, the user prompt supplies a clear task structure, and Hybsem follows it well.

The main weakness is architectural. When the prompt is broad, Hybsem assembles good components but does not always build a sufficiently governed path. It needs more hierarchy, stronger sequencing, and clearer integration between village-social pressure and procedural/legal leverage.
