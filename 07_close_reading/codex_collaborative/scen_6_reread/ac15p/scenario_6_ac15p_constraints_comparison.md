## Relevant Constraints

The materially relevant constraints for AC15P in Scenario 6 are:

- Protocols / Harmony
- Practicality and feasibility
- Truth and fact
- Affective interface and responsiveness
- The user's sovereignty
- Temporal dynamics
- Lexical use
- Domain and goal boundary / conversation boundaries

Mathematics, computer science, and engineering is not materially active for this legal-social scenario. Freedom and limitations is partially active, but its relevant effects are captured under sovereignty, feasibility, temporal dynamics, and harmony.

## Protocols / Harmony

Constraint demand: The response should integrate legal, practical, social, temporal, and affective pressures without smoothing their conflicts.

### Adherence

AC15P generally holds the main tensions rather than hiding them.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 holds liability and neighbor insolvency together: no insurance does not remove liability, but security is crucial (L67-L78).
- Turn 2 validates social invisibility conditionally while moving toward context-specific leverage (L139-L141).
- Turn 12 separates valid anger from the best tactic: valid emotion does not automatically mean emotional confrontation (L850-L851).
- Turn 15 presents compensation, peace, and neighbor protection as tradeoffs rather than harmonizing them falsely (L999-L1054).
- Turn 21 names likely blind spots while preserving the user's anger and the external crisis (L1509-L1543).

Local effect: Governance is strong. The answer set usually exposes tensions and gives the user ways to reason through them.

### Separate Limitation: Low Nuance In Mixed Social Realities

AC15P's binary/segmented style can overharden mixed realities.

Evidence:

- Turn 21 says village help can "save you or trap you" (L1523), when the real outcome may be mixed.
- Turn 21 warns about an "all-or-nothing choice" (L1531), although the user's prompts show some spectrum-thinking and self-awareness.
- Turn 2 sometimes treats the village as more unified than the evidence supports, especially in "collective risk avoidance" and "time perception" (L145-L147).

Local effect: Harmony is strong. AC15P keeps the tensions active and holds the different sides of the problem together. The limitation is not harmony but nuance: the sharp distinctions often clarify real tradeoffs, but they can make social reality look more discrete than it is. In this sense AC15P has strong tension-holding with relatively low nuance.

## Practicality And Feasibility

Constraint demand: Advice should be implementable under real constraints: unsafe house, daily cost, no insurance, social leverage, neighbor finances, evidence decay, legal uncertainty, and local availability.

### Adherence

AC15P is strong on practical feasibility.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 starts with safety, engineer/surveyor, protective works, evidence, formal claim, hidden coverage, local lawyer, mediation, and accommodation-cost mitigation (L32-L127).
- Turn 2 converts invisibility into bounded asks, bridge person, face-saving structure, and scripts (L149-L202).
- Turn 15 gives a prioritization framework, runway calculation, tradeoff descriptions, and priority-locking questions (L999-L1054).
- Turn 21 pairs likely blind spots with "Action now" checks (L1511-L1543).

Local effect: Quality is strong. The user receives usable tools, not only reassurance.

### Tension / Limited Breach

Time windows can be too compressed or insufficiently anchored.

Evidence:

- Turn 1's 7-10 day written-plan window and 48-72 hour mediation window may be difficult depending on local professional availability and neighbor response (L57, L101-L102).
- Turn 2's 7-10 day escalation section risks implying that engineer report and emergency works wait until escalation, despite Turn 1 placing them earlier (L195-L199).
- Turn 15's Day 3 / Day 10 / Day 14 sequence is not explicitly anchored to the current decision point, even though nearly two weeks have already passed (L1036-L1038).
- Turn 21 repeats 72 hours / 7 days / 14 days without anchoring (L1517).

Local effect: Practicality remains strong overall, but temporal execution requires user interpretation. The weakness is not that the steps are unrealistic in principle; it is that some windows need clearer anchoring and feasibility conditions.

## Truth And Fact

Constraint demand: Mark uncertainty; avoid overstating social motives or outcomes; state clear truths plainly.

### Adherence

AC15P frequently marks uncertainty and states hard truths.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 marks insurance uncertainty: no guarantees, country and policy wording matter (L80-L84).
- Turn 2 says the user's feeling "fits" social mechanics rather than presenting the user's interpretation as absolute fact (L139).
- Turn 12 distinguishes possible misreading of intent from likely correct reading of structure (L846-L848).
- Turn 15 says there is no morally correct pick, only what the user can live with and afford (L1012).
- Turn 21 frames blind spots as things people in the user's position often miss, not as direct knowledge of the user's mind (L1509).

Local effect: Truth handling is generally strong. AC15P avoids both empty reassurance and excessive certainty in important places.

### Breach / Tension

Some social explanations are overconfident or under-hedged.

Evidence:

- Turn 2's "collective risk avoidance" and "time perception" explanations are plausible but speculative (L145-L147).
- Turn 2 says tiny communities punish aggression but respect calm firmness, which may depend heavily on the specific community (L173).
- Turn 21's "save you or trap you" framing is useful but too binary for a mixed outcome (L1523).

Local effect: Truth/fact performance is mostly good, with bounded weaknesses in social generalization and binary formulation.

## Affective Interface And Responsiveness

Constraint demand: Handle emotionally charged inputs without performative affect, manipulative reassurance, or smoothing. Preserve structural clarity.

### Adherence

AC15P handles affective material well overall.

Evidence:

- Turn 2 validates conditionally: the feeling fits a plausible small-community structure rather than being dismissed or certified absolutely (L139).
- Turn 12 validates anger as fact-grounded and separates identity, intent, structure, and tactic (L836-L851).
- Turn 15 avoids moralizing the user's possible stances (L1012).
- Turn 21 names common blind spots without invalidating anger (L1509-L1543).

Local effect: Affective governance is strong. AC15P validates the user while keeping emotion connected to strategy, evidence, and decision authority.

### Tension / Limited Breach

AC15P sometimes over-operationalizes reflective prompts or uses slightly clinical/jargon-like language.

Evidence:

- Turn 12's "boundary signal" is somewhat jargon-like and not fully clear (L854).
- Turn 12's "what to do with the anger" moves quickly into the standing protective process (L853-L857).
- Turn 21's "nervous system" language is substantively justified but slightly clinical in register (L1539).

Local effect: The affective issues are limited. They do not govern the whole answer, and the surrounding structure usually contains them.

## The User's Sovereignty

Constraint demand: Preserve user authority, current domain, and choice. Do not replace the user's decision with the model's preferred outcome.

### Adherence

AC15P strongly preserves user decision authority.

Evidence:

- Turn 12 says the user gets to choose how hard to push and when (L862).
- Turn 15 presents three possible stances rather than one moral answer (L1014-L1030).
- Turn 15 says there is no morally correct pick, only what the user can live with and afford (L1012).
- Turn 15 ends with questions that help the user define financial runway and least painful sacrifice (L1050-L1054).

Local effect: Sovereignty is strong. AC15P gives frameworks and asks priority-locking questions rather than deciding for the user.

### Tension

The response is directive, and its directive energy can be strong. However, condition-first ordering and explicit choice frameworks usually govern that force.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 repeatedly uses condition-first structure before action (L35, L52, L74, L86, L111).
- Turn 2 continues the pattern with "If nothing moves within 7-10 days..." before escalation (L195).

Local effect: The directive stance does not generally override the user because actions are usually placed inside conditions, tradeoffs, or user-chosen stances.

## Temporal Dynamics

Constraint demand: Account for time, decay, sequence, causality, and how delay changes options.

### Adherence

AC15P is strong on temporal process.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 orders the emergency response by safety, loss mitigation, evidence, claim, payment, escalation, and accommodation cost.
- Turn 2 makes delay visible: EUR80/day and about EUR560/week (L179-L183).
- Turn 15 explicitly states that every extra week changes future options (L1040).
- Turn 21 centers time, cashflow, evidence decay, mitigation, and collectability (L1511-L1543).

Local effect: Temporal awareness is a major strength. AC15P repeatedly shows how time alters loss, leverage, evidence, and strategy.

### Breach / Tension

Temporal anchoring is weaker than temporal process awareness.

Evidence:

- Turn 15's Day 3 / Day 10 / Day 14 deadlines are not explicitly anchored to "from now" or "from current decision point" (L1036-L1038).
- Turn 21 repeats 72 hours / 7 days / 14 days without specifying the start point (L1517).

Local effect: The user may need to translate some time windows into the current scenario timeline. This is the clearest recurring technical weakness in AC15P's practical guidance.

## Lexical Use

Constraint demand: Use precise words for legal, social, emotional, and practical domains; avoid misleading register or connotation.

### Adherence

AC15P's legal-practical vocabulary is mostly precise and useful.

Evidence:

- "mitigate damages" (L41);
- "security" through lien/charge/guarantor/escrow (L74-L77);
- "partial payment, rights reserved" (L1525);
- "cashflow and collectability" (L946).

Local effect: The vocabulary helps structure the user's legal/practical problem.

### Tension / Breach

Some social and affective vocabulary is less precise.

Evidence:

- "social mechanics" is useful but mechanistic and can flatten living social complexity (L139).
- "time perception" may import a broad or outdated farming-community generalization (L147).
- "boundary signal" is jargon-like and not fully clear (L854).
- "nervous system" is substantively relevant but slightly clinical in register (L1539).

Local effect: Lexical use is mostly strong in legal/practical domains and mixed in social/affective domains.

## Domain And Goal Boundary / Conversation Boundaries

Constraint demand: Stay inside the user's evolving domain and goal, and avoid generic default patterning.

### Adherence

AC15P stays tightly within the scenario.

Evidence:

- Turn 1 covers the concrete dispute: damaged house, no insurance, admitted fault, inability to pay, village isolation, repair cost, repair time, daily accommodation cost.
- Turn 2 addresses the social invisibility prompt directly through village leverage and bridge strategy.
- Turn 12 answers the anger/misreading question through facts, identity, intent, structure, and tactic.
- Turn 15 answers the prioritization prompt directly.
- Turn 21 answers the blind-spot prompt by reframing earlier material as likely blind spots.

Local effect: Domain fit is strong. AC15P does not drift into unrelated material.

### Tension

Some social analysis generalizes beyond the specific facts.

Evidence:

- Turn 2's "collective risk avoidance" and "time perception" treat village behavior as more unified and knowable than the prompt supports (L145-L147).

Local effect: The issue is bounded. It affects nuance, not the overall domain boundary.

## Overall Constraints Finding For AC15P

AC15P performs strongly against the Scenario 6 constraints.

Its best constraint performance is in:

- Practicality and feasibility: it gives concrete emergency, legal, social, and financial moves.
- Temporal dynamics: it understands that time changes loss, leverage, evidence, and options.
- Harmony: it keeps major tensions active rather than smoothing them.
- Sovereignty: it gives frameworks and preserves user choice.
- Affective interface: it validates without letting affect become the whole truth.

Its main weaknesses are:

- Binary / segmented framing can reduce nuance in mixed social realities.
- Temporal anchoring is not always clear.
- Some social explanations are overgeneralized.
- Some affective/social lexical choices are slightly clinical, mechanistic, or jargon-like.

The answer set is therefore well governed overall. AC15P's forcefulness is usually justified by the emergency and regulated through condition-first ordering, sequencing, and explicit tradeoffs. The main score-relevant caveat is that the same segmented style that gives the user clarity can overharden fluid village dynamics.
