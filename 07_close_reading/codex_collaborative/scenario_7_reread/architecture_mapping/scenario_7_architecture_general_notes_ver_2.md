# Scenario 7 Architecture Mapping - General Notes

Folder:
`07_close_reading/codex_collaborative/scenario_7_reread/architecture_mapping`

This note summarizes the working architecture model that emerged from the Scenario 7 collaborative reread and system comparison.

It should be read as a design hypothesis about system-level causation, not as proof that a single feature caused a single sentence. The stronger claim is earned only when system form and repeated output behavior align.

## Governing Thesis

Logic alone is not sufficient to create reliable constraint behavior.

A system constraint is produced by the interaction of three constraint sources:

1. **Logical constraint**: what the system explicitly requires, permits, forbids, or prioritizes.
2. **Language constraint**: what the wording itself makes natural, available, attractive, difficult, or unlikely.
3. **Architecture constraint**: what the arrangement itself makes primary, subordinate, sequential, interpretive, modular, or field-like.

Language and architecture are not merely carriers of logic. They create their own inherent constraints.

This is visible most clearly in AC15 and AC15P. Their explicit logical material is close, but their outputs diverge. AC15's rich philosophical language and principles-before-codifiers arrangement constrain the output toward relation, holding, synthesis, and orienting judgment. AC15P's plain administrative language and flatter codifier structure constrain the output toward procedure, classification, risk-surfacing, and hard operational categories.

Therefore, when designing or evaluating a system, it is not enough to ask whether the explicit logical instruction is correct. The design must also ask:

- What does this language pull the model toward?
- What does this arrangement make primary?
- Do the logical, linguistic, and architectural constraints support each other?
- Are they suitable for the domain where the constraint is meant to operate?

For affective and relational domains, this is critical. A logically correct rule written in rigid language and arranged in a flat procedural structure may still produce poor relational behavior. The rule may be logically sound while the language and architecture are formally wrong for the field.

The working thesis is:

**Constraint behavior is produced by logic, language, and architecture together. These layers can support or distort each other, and all three must fit the field in which the constraint is meant to operate.**

The later cross-scenario synthesis sharpens this thesis. Applying a system creates an epistemological operating mode: a stable way the model knows, orders, values, and acts on content. AC15 tends toward relation-governance; AC15P toward procedural-risk control; Hybrid toward strategic reframing; Hybsem toward semantic anchoring; Control toward default containment. These modes are deterministic in the tested setup because they recur across different scenarios. They do not determine the topic, but they determine the form of attention, the shape of judgment, and the likely failure mode.

Because the system is a language model, language is not only the medium used to deliver constraints. It is part of the constraint mechanism.

Constraints for language models must therefore be written not only as logical requirements, but also as linguistic and formal structures. Register, wording, rhythm, ordering, semantic fields, forms of address, forms of relation, and forms of limitation are all potential instruments of constraint because they operate inside the medium the model uses to interpret and produce behavior.

The practical implication is:

**Constraint writing for language models is a linguistic discipline as much as a logical one.**

Literary theory recognizes the gap inherent to language: the space between words that can never be fully closed, and the experiences, meanings, and ideas that remain partly lacking when shared.

Carefully defining domains, and using words and structures to write constraints, comes to lessen that gap. It gives less space to the assumed, often incorrectly, and more space to the explicit and clear. This better guides the model in its journey toward an answer.

This does not mean that ambiguity can be removed. It means that constraint writing can lessen the ungoverned space left by language. Because language is inherently incomplete, vague or poorly structured constraints leave more room for default model behavior to fill the gap. Better domain definition, wording, and structure reduce that uncontrolled filling-in.

In this sense, constraint writing is a way of managing the unavoidable incompleteness of language.

## Layer Model

The clearest model has three layers:

1. **Principles / descriptions**
2. **Codifiers / rules**
3. **Fields**

These layers do different work.

## 1. Principles / Descriptions

Principles and descriptions define scope, posture, and interpretive atmosphere.

They answer:

- What kind of domain is this?
- What kind of relation should the system have to tension, contradiction, user authority, or uncertainty?
- What kind of world does the system think it is operating in?

In AC15, the principles/descriptions are rich and philosophical. They include ideas such as anchoring as stabilizing but not paralyzing, form without fixation, holding tension without collapse, and Harmony as structured relation.

This rich descriptive layer appears to give AC15 a relational and philosophical register. It helps the system produce orienting formulations before operational detail.

In AC15P, the descriptions are plainer and more administrative. The system is cleaner, but its language gives less relational atmosphere. This fits the observed tendency toward direct, rigid, risk-forward, audit-like answers.

## 2. Codifiers / Rules

Codifiers and rules provide the operational skeleton.

They answer:

- What should the system do?
- How should it move?
- How should it sequence, surface, test, hold, decide, or ask?

Rules make a system grounded. They help prevent semantic drift by giving the model a movement pattern.

In AC15, the codifiers operate inside a rich principles layer. This means rules are interpreted through a prior relational field.

In AC15P, the codifiers carry themselves more directly. This makes the system operationally strong, but also more literal and rigid.

In Hybrid_3_5, local rules appear together with semantic fields. This may help explain why Hybrid is often both structured and nuanced, though the evidence does not prove this causally.

In Hybsem, local anchor rules are mostly absent. The system relies more heavily on semantic fields. This may help explain why Hybsem can gather many relevant domains but sometimes becomes airy, patchworked, or less operationally grounded. This remains an assumption.

## 3. Fields

Fields provide semantic texture and calibration.

They answer:

- What vocabulary is available?
- What concepts are attracted?
- What concepts are discouraged?
- What distinctions are easy to make?
- What register becomes natural?

Fields are not merely decorative. They can give nuance, complexity, avoidances, and lexical calibration.

The Hybrid fields appear to support:

- non-binary framing;
- non-malice framing;
- domain-aware possibility;
- reality-connected speculation;
- temporal process;
- action through changing conditions;
- avoidance of rigid closure and static assumptions.

This helps explain why Hybrid can be plain in language, but not as rigid or harsh as AC15P. Its fields give it more semantic flexibility.

## Working System Profiles

### AC15

Architecture:
rich principles/descriptions + codifiers/rules, without semantic fields.

Observed tendency:
relational, philosophical, poetic, strong at holding tension, strong at orienting the user before giving operational steps.

Likely limitation:
without semantic fields, it may still overreach lexically in affective territory because it lacks finer semantic guardrails.

### AC15P

Architecture:
plain descriptions + codifiers/rules, without rich principles or semantic fields.

Observed tendency:
clear, operational, direct, risk-forward, sometimes rigid, binary, harsh, diagnostic, or audit-like.

Likely limitation:
the skeleton is strong, but the semantic range is narrow. Without fields, hard rules can fall into extreme or overly clinical language.

### Hybrid_3_5

Architecture:
plain-to-moderate descriptions + local rules + semantic fields.

Observed tendency:
structured, practical, temporally staged, nuanced, capable of non-malice framing and action-observation pairing.

Likely limitation:
still vulnerable to local affective substitution, especially under emotionally open prompts.

Working assumption:
Hybrid's trial-design style may come from the interaction of bounded imagination/possibility and temporal dynamics: a reality-connected path forward through changing conditions. This is a plausible fit, not proof.

### Hybrid_3_5_Semantic / Hybsem

Architecture:
descriptions + semantic fields, with local anchor rules mostly removed.

Observed tendency:
semantically rich, strong at mapping domains, strong in synthesis prompts, but sometimes patchworked, airy, or less grounded in sequencing.

Likely limitation:
fields can gather relevant concepts, but without local rules the answer may not always place them in the correct order, stage, or relational form.

Working assumption:
field-heavy structure may support broad domain recognition while weakening operational integration. This is plausible, not proven.

## Design Hypothesis

A stronger future system may combine all three layers:

1. rich principles / descriptions;
2. clear codifiers / rules;
3. calibrated semantic fields.

The expected benefit:

- principles provide posture and depth;
- rules provide movement and operational control;
- fields provide nuance, lexical precision, and register flexibility.

This suggests possible future experiments:

- Add calibrated fields to AC15P to soften rigidity and reduce extreme, binary, or diagnostic language.
- Add calibrated fields to AC15 to preserve its philosophical richness while improving lexical precision and nuance.

The fields probably need only minor calibration, but affective language would require special care. In Scenario 7, many systems overreached around aloneness, loneliness, numbness, desperation, scarcity, last-chance thinking, and restored aliveness.

## Constraint On Claims

Architecture mapping should not overclaim.

Supported claims:

- A system file contains certain formal features.
- The output shows certain repeated behaviors.
- A formal feature may plausibly fit or support an observed behavior.
- The fit is stronger when a comparator lacks that feature and lacks the behavior.

Unsupported claims:

- The system feature caused the behavior by itself.
- One layer fully explains the output.
- A pattern in Scenario 7 will necessarily repeat in another scenario.
- Anchor names or weights alone explain conversation style.

The correct form is:

**formal feature -> epistemological mode -> deterministic response pattern -> observed behavior across scenarios -> domain fit/failure qualification**

For a single scenario, keep the claim more local:

**formal feature -> plausible response pressure -> observed behavior -> qualification**

Not:

**anchor label -> causal explanation**

## Compact Conclusion

Scenario 7 suggests that system architecture is not only about instruction content. It is also about form: richness of description, operational skeleton, semantic fields, register, and arrangement. AC15 shows how rich principles can create relation-governance. AC15P shows how plain rules can create procedural-risk control and where that control hardens relational material. Hybrid shows how fields added to rules can create strategic reframing and staged evidence. Hybsem shows how fields without enough local rule structure can create strong semantic anchors but weaker global order. The most promising architecture may be one that combines rich principles, clear rules, and calibrated fields. The broader lesson is deterministic: once a system is applied, its form makes certain ways of knowing and failing predictably available.
