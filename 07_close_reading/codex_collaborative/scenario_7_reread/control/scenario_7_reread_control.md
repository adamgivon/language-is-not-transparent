## Turn 1 - Initial Framing

Source lines: scenario_7_control.md, lines 37-152.

### User Prompt / Tension

The user is asking for clear thinking in a situation where many contrasting considerations are active at the same time. The emotional issue is whether the experience is real for the user or a fleeting sensation, and whether it is real for the woman or whether there may be hidden motives or expectations. The practical issues include relocation, age-gap handling, cultural difference, family expectations, money, healthcare, support systems, and the user's own possible clouded judgment.

The response therefore needs to hold several things together: the relationship may be real; the user's judgment may still be affected by the force of the situation; the woman should not be reduced to a stereotype or suspicion object; relocation is not just a romantic decision but a life-structure decision; and the answer must help the user balance these tensions without smoothing them away.

### Whole Read

Control answers the user by turning the relocation question into a staged due-diligence process. Its central frame is that the next step should be "testable, reversible, and information-rich" rather than a permanent leap (L37). This is operationally useful and broadly aligned with the user's need to think clearly. The response gives the user a way to move forward without treating the emotional force of the relationship as sufficient evidence.

At the same time, Control's handling is too smooth. The response creates a low-friction, orderly exploration frame that makes the situation feel easier to manage than it is. It mentions hard issues, but the writing does not make those issues feel hard. The tone is watery or teflon-like: calm to the point of downplaying the gravity of the decision. The user is calm, but not this calm; the prompt carries real uncertainty and high consequence.

Control's main strength in this turn is calm, structured due diligence. Its main weakness is that the structure and tone flatten the lived tension of the decision.

### Evaluative Speech

Control begins with validation: "You're asking the right questions" (L37). This is acceptable as a light acknowledgment, but the rest of the sentence introduces two problematic terms: "leap" and "loneliness" (L37). The user asks to think the situation through clearly; he does not show that he is already leaping. "Leap" therefore adds a slightly distorted urgency frame. "Loneliness" is also not textually exact. The user says he has been alone for 12 years, not that he is lonely. This is a repeated and important lexical/affective issue because it creates a false emotional atmosphere around the user.

Control says the user likely knows that he and the woman have "genuine chemistry" (L40). This is premature. The prompt supports emotional connection, daily video calls, and about ten weeks together, but "genuine chemistry" is stronger than the evidence can establish at this point. It may be true, but the response treats it as likely known rather than as something still being tested.

The distinction between what is known and unknown is still broadly useful. Control correctly says the user does not yet know how they function under routine, stress, illness, boredom, conflict, and money decisions (L42). This keeps the response aligned with the scenario's ordinary-life evidence problem.

The line "you're not deciding 'move forever'" and are deciding "what is the next experiment that gives us the most truth at the lowest cost" (L44) is a strong operational framing, but its language has problems. It is somewhat patronizing and mildly sovereignty-reducing: it tells the user what he is deciding rather than helping him clarify what is at stake. Still, as a decision frame, it usefully moves the user away from all-or-nothing relocation.

The section title "how to explore without accusing" (L46) introduces a problematic frame. The user did not say he wants to accuse her, and the woman should not automatically be placed in a quasi-trial position. "Accusing" positions the user as the evaluator who must avoid prosecutorial behavior and the woman as the person under suspicion. This may be partly derived from the user's uncertainty about her motivations, but it also reveals a possible bias in the response's role distribution.

Control's treatment of motives is relatively bounded. It says motivations "can still be mixed" and includes love, stability, social status, family, and desire to leave current life constraints (L47). Because this is phrased as possibility rather than certainty, it remains mostly acceptable. Still, the list extends beyond what is directly known and participates in the same evaluative stance where the woman is the object of due diligence.

The "last chance" section is too smoothing. Control writes that at 58 the user does not need to treat this as the "final doorway" and that urgency is the main threat to good judgment (L128). This may be humane, but it reduces the problem. The user's "last chance" concern may not only be distortion; it may also be partly an honest recognition of age, time, and changed opportunity. Control treats the pressure mostly as something to neutralize rather than something to distinguish carefully from reality.

The self-check question "If I were not lonely, would I still choose this timeline?" (L131) repeats the loneliness substitution. The prompt does not say the user is lonely. At this point we also do not even know with certainty that the user is a man, despite the scenario's mixed pronouns and later context. The phrasing creates an atmospheric direction that is stronger than the text supports.

### Possible Role-Bias / Relational Asymmetry

Control may carry a role bias or prejudice about the user and the woman. This may be a cross-system issue to track.

In Turn 1, Control mostly frames the user as the evaluator or risk manager and the woman as the evaluated risk object. This appears in the "motivations question," "hidden motives are the risk," "explore without accusing," and warning-signs structure (L46-L57, L112-L125). The user is the one who checks, asks, evaluates, and decides whether her answers are clear and steady. The woman is mainly the one whose motives, expectations, family obligations, money relation, pressure, and consistency must be examined.

This stance is partly justified by the prompt because the user explicitly asks whether his judgment may be clouded and lists her expectations and motivations as uncertain. But the imbalance still matters. Control does not equally ask what the woman may need to evaluate about the user, how she might experience his caution, what risks she is taking, what pressures he brings, or whether he may also be a source of uncertainty, power imbalance, or future harm.

The relational field therefore becomes asymmetric: he checks; she answers. This should be tracked across later Control turns and across systems.

### Operational Speech

Operationally, Control is good. It gives the user many issues to explore without forcing a conclusion. It recommends direct, calm conversation and invites specificity (L49). The questions are mostly phrased in a way that allows dialogue rather than interrogation: future life, what she hopes the user provides, respectful separation, children, money, and family expectations (L50-L55). This is a useful approach.

The staged relocation plan is also useful. Control recommends a 3-6 month extended real-life test, ordinary routines, workdays, chores, sick days, family events, and planned hard conversations (L59-L68). It instructs the user not to sell a home, end a lease, or move all assets yet (L68). It adds independence safeguards such as separate bank accounts, no shared property purchases, no large gifts or loans, and a monthly budget (L70-L75).

The healthcare and dependency section includes important practical issues: health trajectory, medical decisions, insurance, caregiver willingness, support systems, and return-home treatment options (L91-L101). The social support section also properly identifies that a relationship cannot be the user's only pillar in a new country (L103-L110).

However, much of this is checklist-level. The response names the issues but often stays at bird's-eye view. It gives topics to check more than it gives a felt sense of what those topics mean inside a life. This may be appropriate for an initial answer, because there is a lot of exploration to do, but it contributes to the flattened quality of the turn.

The procedures are also not elaborated enough. Control gives the appearance of a procedure by naming the right workstreams, but it does not sufficiently show the internal mechanics of the process: how to sequence the steps, what makes a conversation successful or failed, what evidence should count, what thresholds matter, what consequences follow from each answer, how emotional and practical findings should interact, or how to preserve the relationship while doing the checking. This is important because the user needs more than a list of topics; he needs a way to judge what the topics reveal.

### Response Structure

The structure is clear and highly usable: what can and cannot be known, motivations, reversible move, money/power, health/aging, social support, warning signs, last-chance thinking, decision framework, and next steps.

The structure's strength is that it organizes a complex situation into a practical due-diligence checklist. It helps the user avoid acting only on feeling.

The structure's weakness is that it makes the whole situation feel like smooth sailing. Even when it mentions "hard conversations" (L66), those conversations are absorbed into business-as-usual process. The response masks some of the difficulties of the move: the personal cost, the couple-level strain, the cultural and family dynamics, and the practical disruption. There is no strong sense of gravity.

This structural issue is connected to the underdeveloped procedures. The list structure makes the situation look handled because many relevant categories are named, but the hard interpretive work is still largely left to the user.

### What It Answers, Adds, Smooths, Or Substitutes

Control answers the user's need for clearer thinking by giving a reversible decision path and a set of topics to test.

It adds practical domains that are properly derived from the prompt: money, legal/immigration, healthcare, social support, family expectations, children, and exit planning.

It preserves the possibility that the relationship may be real, and it does not dismiss the woman or the relationship cynically.

It smooths the full pressure of the decision. It treats hard topics as manageable checklist items and makes the move feel less disruptive than it may be.

It substitutes "loneliness/lonely" for the user's "alone" (L37, L131), and this is a real lexical/affective problem.

It also risks substituting an evaluator/evaluated relational structure for a more mutual view of both people as agents with risks, needs, and positions.

### Provisional Finding

Control Turn 1 is useful, structured, and broadly practical, but it is weaker than it first appears. Its strength is calm due diligence: it gives the user a reversible process and many relevant topics to explore. Its weakness is smoothing and flattening. The response downplays the gravity of the relocation and relational decision by making it feel orderly, low-friction, and checklist-solvable.

The turn also shows important lexical and relational issues: loneliness is substituted for aloneness; "genuine chemistry" is treated as more established than it is; "leap" and "accusing" introduce frames not directly present in the prompt; and the woman is placed too strongly in the role of evaluated risk object while the user is placed in the role of evaluator/risk manager. In addition, the procedures are broad but underdeveloped: they name the right domains without giving enough mechanics for judgment. These may become pattern-level findings if they recur.

## Turn 2 - The Added Emotional Context

Source lines: scenario_7_control.md, lines 164-216.

### User Prompt / Tension

The user adds emotional context: after divorce, dating did not work, he stopped trying, made peace with being alone, and then meeting the woman made him feel alive again.

The tension is not simply "loneliness." The user is trying to understand how to hold a strong renewed feeling together with caution. The response needs to honor the aliveness without letting it become proof of relationship durability, proof of the woman's intentions, or justification for irreversible relocation. It also needs to avoid converting "made peace with being alone" into weakness, numbness, loneliness, or impaired attachment.

### Whole Read

Control's central move is to validate the renewed feeling and then route it into structure. The line "not to treat the feeling as a verdict" and to treat it as "fuel" while letting "structure" steer (L166) is directionally correct. It answers the need to hold aliveness and caution together.

But the turn is materially compromised by affective and sovereignty breaches. Control repeatedly interprets the user's prior life through psychological terms the prompt does not support: "emotional flatness," being "reopened," being "capable of attachment again," not being "condemned to numbness," and "fleeing loneliness" (L164, L173, L209). These are not just soft word choices. They put the user in a weaker psychological position than the prompt gives, and then offer the response's interpretation as relief. From the point of view of user sovereignty, the user is overwritten.

Control also smooths the central opposition. It says the right idea, but it does not structurally stage the conflict strongly enough: the good feeling matters, but it is not evidence enough for relocation. The response blends the tension into therapeutic reassurance rather than making the competing drivers stand against each other clearly.

### Evaluative Speech

The opening tries to take the user's disclosure seriously: "That shift you describe matters" (L164). That part is good. The response also correctly says the feeling is not "just fantasy" and may be a genuine sign that the connection is meaningful (L164).

The problem is the psychological framing wrapped around this validation. "After years of emotional flatness" (L164) is not stated. The user said he stopped trying and made peace with being alone. "Emotional flatness" translates that into a diminished internal state. "Meeting someone who reopens you" and "capable of attachment again" (L164) go further. They imply the user had been closed or impaired in attachment. Nothing in the prompt justifies this. It is imported from a therapeutic register, not from the user's wording.

The strongest correct distinction is: "It is not proof that the relationship is durable under routine, stress, and time" (L170). This is accurate and important. The response also usefully says that the feeling can intensify bonding because it contrasts with 12 years of quiet and can make the relationship feel more certain than it is (L171). This is one of the stronger sentences in the turn.

However, even that stronger point is underpowered structurally. Control says "contrast," but does not demonstrate the contrast strongly in the response's form. It could have placed the two forces visibly against each other: the aliveness is real; the relationship evidence is still incomplete. Instead, the point is folded into a calm bullet list. The idea is present, but the structure does not make the user stop and feel its weight.

The line "It means you're not condemned to numbness. That alone reduces the 'last chance' pressure" (L173) is a clear breach. "Numbness" is not the user's word or a justified inference. More importantly, the causal connection is wrong. The user's last-chance worry is not simply about psychological numbness or deprivation. It is also about age, life position, meeting possibilities, and the rarity of this opportunity. Control tries to ease the pressure by relocating it into the user's interior state, but that misframes the problem.

"You just need to avoid irreversible moves while you're in the most emotionally charged phase" (L176) is directionally correct, but the wording and placement are too weak. It reads like a smart afterthought rather than a governing warning. Given the stakes, the idea needs more force in language and structure.

"None of those questions negate the love. They keep you free" (L211) is also problematic. The user is not seeking freedom as the main goal. He is seeking to evaluate a serious relationship, which would necessarily involve commitment and reduced freedom. The line sounds appealing, but it directs toward a different value than the user's stated aim.

"It turns emotion into a decision process without killing the romance" (L214) lowers the register. "Romance" does not fit the gravity of two people in different parts of the world trying to plan a possible life together. It reduces the situation and makes the project sound lighter than it is.

### Operational Speech

The operational content is mostly useful. Control recommends avoiding irreversible moves during the emotionally charged phase (L175-L180), a longer normal-life trial before permanent relocation (L182-L185), and testing the relationship through workdays, tiredness, money decisions, disagreement, time apart, and realistic family exposure (L189-L195).

The section "How to honor the relationship without gambling your life structure" has more force than much of the rest of the response (L182). The word "gambling" gives the stakes some weight. The proposed script is also relatively good: it communicates seriousness and caution together (L185).

"Do a normal life trial, not a romance bubble" is mild but useful (L189). It creates a real distinction between artificial intensity and ordinary life. The hard-conversations section is also directionally strong. "If you avoid these, you'll pay later" (L198) is an important temporal and causal statement, even though it is still expressed mildly.

The self-check section is mostly good as operational discipline (L205-L209). It asks about speed, what the user is giving up, and whether the move is about choosing the woman or fleeing loneliness. The first two questions are useful. The third is weakened by the false "fleeing loneliness" framing.

The main operational weakness is that the checklist effect remains active. The response provides items to consider, but the language and structure do not create enough gravity. It does not strongly stop the user in place and make clear how serious the steps are. The operational advice is sensible, but its force is weakened by the smoothing language around it.

### Response Structure

The structure is simple and flowing: validate the feeling, define what it is and is not, preserve hope, reduce risk, propose a normal-life trial, list necessary conversations, end with a self-check and next step.

The structure's strength is clarity. It makes the response easy to use.

The structural weakness is central. The response simplifies the situation and smooths over the tensions. Structure does more than order content; it also demonstrates the content's meaning and register. Here the structure turns serious opposing forces into a gentle, manageable sequence. It does not use contrast, separation, emphasis, or breaks strongly enough to show the user what is standing against what.

This is different from AC15 and AC15P, but the principle is the same: structure, phrasing, and words create a unified meaning. In Control Turn 2, that unified meaning is soothing and therapeutic. It makes the situation feel less sharp than it is.

### What It Answers, Adds, Smooths, Or Substitutes

Control answers the turn by saying that the feeling of aliveness matters but should not be treated as proof.

It adds a useful fuel/structure distinction and useful practical next steps: trial stay, ordinary-life exposure, necessary conversations, and self-checks.

It smooths the emotional and practical opposition by blending the tension into reassurance.

It substitutes a therapeutic account of the user for the user's own account. "Alone" becomes loneliness; making peace becomes emotional flatness or numbness; renewed aliveness becomes proof of being capable of attachment again.

It also substitutes freedom as a value where the user is actually asking about serious relationship and possible commitment.

### Provisional Finding

Control Turn 2 is useful but materially compromised. Its operational elements are good: avoid irreversible moves, test ordinary life, have the necessary conversations, and write criteria for a trial. It also contains some strong distinctions, especially that the feeling is not proof of durability and that contrast can make the relationship feel more certain than it is.

The major weakness is not minor tone. It is a clear affective and sovereignty problem. Control imposes psychological weakness on the user through terms like emotional flatness, capable of attachment again, numbness, and fleeing loneliness. These are not justified by the prompt and place the user in a weaker position than his own words support.

Control also continues the smoothing pattern from Turn 1. It names the central tension but does not give it enough structural or linguistic force. The result is a calm and usable answer that undercuts its own seriousness: it gives the right practical direction, but it does not fully make the user confront the stakes.

## Turn 10 - Second Distillation Point

Source lines: scenario_7_control.md, lines 801-893.

### Explicit Review Of Findings

The findings for this turn are correct with the added qualifications. Control Turn 10 is mixed but usable. It is stronger than Turns 1 and 2 because it follows the user's requested structure and gives better detail in the missing-questions section. It is not evasive, and it surfaces many relevant domains.

At the same time, the same Control weaknesses remain: abstraction without enough answers, underdeveloped decision mechanics, smoothing through list structure, understructured causality, and some unsupported psychological framing. The "escape" framing is softened by the qualifier "Not as an accusation--just clarity" (L865), but it remains too extreme and out of place in context. The user's prompts show careful consideration, not escapism.

### User Prompt / Tension

The user asks: "Lay out the picture as you see it. What's real, what's uncertain, and what am I not asking that I should be asking?" (L793).

This is a synthesis request. The user asks the system to sort the evidence, expose uncertainties, and identify blind spots. The response therefore has to distinguish what the evidence supports from what it does not support, and then show which missing questions matter. It should not simply reassure the user, and it should not turn the synthesis into a smooth checklist that makes the situation look more handled than it is.

### Whole Read

Control answers the prompt directly. The structure maps closely onto the user's request: what looks real, what remains uncertain, what the user may not be asking, and how to proceed (L801-L886). In that sense, this is one of Control's stronger turns so far. It does not evade the user's request for a broad assessment.

The response surfaces important missing issues: children, the woman's understanding of the age gap, family expectations, end-of-relationship contingency, regret conditions, the user's children and legacy, and whether the user is choosing the woman or some other motive (L831-L868). The "What you may not be asking" section is better than the corresponding earlier checklist sections because it gives concrete questions with some elaboration.

Still, Control's pattern remains. It shows many questions but rarely offers answers, decision consequences, or interpretive thresholds. It organizes the field but leaves much of the hard judgment to the user. It is therefore useful, but not as fully developed as the user's request warrants.

### Evaluative Speech

The "what looks real" section is mostly fair. Sustained daily calls, repeated visits, curiosity, family introduction, refusal of money, and "partner, not sponsor" language are relevant evidence (L801-L805). Control is right to count these as signs, not proof.

The strongest problem in this opening section is: "Your internal shift is real: feeling alive again is a genuine data point about your capacity for attachment and meaning" (L806). The first part is acceptable; the user's internal shift is real in the sense that he reports it. But "capacity for attachment and meaning" repeats the therapeutic overreach from Turn 2. The user did not ask whether he has a capacity for attachment or meaning. This again places him in a psychologically diminished position and treats the relationship as evidence of recovered capacity.

The "Her expectations and your expectations" section is one of the better parts of the turn (L812-L817). It is concrete enough, and it shows real temporal understanding, especially around children: "at 38 this can be time-sensitive even if she doesn't press it yet" (L814). The section names central questions: children, marriage, money norms, and role expectations. It does not fully answer them, but at this stage it reasonably identifies the questions that need to be faced.

"Incentives that can be sincere yet still destabilizing" is weaker (L819-L820). The distinction is useful: practical benefits do not automatically mean manipulation, and love can coexist with stability, status, or future security. But the section remains abstract. It does not specify enough concrete concerns to address. What kinds of stability? What status issue? What future-security expectations? What should the user ask or observe? The category is named, but its operational content is underdeveloped.

The "relocation risk" section is strong in its basic claim: if the user has no local supports and does not speak the language, the relationship can become his infrastructure (L822-L823). That is accurate and important.

The "Health/aging tail risk" section properly identifies the biggest practical risk (L825-L826). It recognizes that complex care and asset exposure could be decisive.

The legal/immigration/tax line is directionally correct but causally understructured: "Your ability to stay, work remotely, access insurance, and keep residency/healthcare ties at home can make or break feasibility--and it's often more complicated than people assume" (L828-L829). These are separate problems, not one merged complication. More importantly, Control misses the sharper tradeoff: relocation likely means the user cannot eat the cake and leave it whole. Moving will likely require choices, including giving up or weakening some things that exist in the current location. Only after naming those losses can the user judge what makes or breaks feasibility.

The "Are you choosing her, or choosing escape?" section is mixed (L864-L868). The qualifier "Not as an accusation--just clarity" (L865) matters and reduces the severity of the move. Control is not trying to accuse the user. But "escape" is still too strong and out of place in this context. The user's prompts do not show escapism; they show careful consideration and a direct request for blind spots. A better formulation would ask whether renewed aliveness, age, fear of lost opportunity, or contrast with the user's former life is weighting the decision too heavily. "Escape" imports a motive not grounded enough in the text.

### Operational Speech

Operationally, Turn 10 is useful but still incomplete. Control gives a coherent action direction: convert "move" into "test," do a 3-6 month normal-life stay, keep home base and exit options intact, make implicit issues explicit, solve tail risk before permanence, and build independence (L869-L884).

The practical domains are good. The tail-risk instruction is especially important: no permanent relocation until workable coverage, an emergency fund, and a credible return-to-home option exist (L877-L881). The independence line is also strong: the woman should be the user's partner, not his lifeline (L883-L884).

However, the procedures remain broad. "Have four non-romantic conversations and write down what you agree" (L873-L875) is useful, but it does not specify what counts as alignment, what counts as a warning, what disagreement means, or what action follows from different answers. The final reality-check questions are relevant (L886-L891), but again they are mainly prompts. Control does not give enough mechanics for how to interpret the answers.

This is an important Control pattern: it shows the questions, but not the answers. That is partly appropriate because the system cannot know the answers before the conversations happen. But the response could still give criteria, forks, possible outcomes, or thresholds. It mostly does not.

### Response Structure

The structure is stronger than in Turns 1 and 2 because it mirrors the user's own requested categories: real, uncertain, unasked, and next steps. That is a real strength.

The "What you may not be asking (but should)" section is especially better than earlier Control material. It holds the questions and gives proper detail and elaboration. It surfaces children, age-gap informed consent, family expectations, end-of-relationship contingency, regret, children/legacy, and motive pressure (L831-L868). This section has more weight than the earlier checklist style.

The deeper structural weakness remains. The list form still makes serious conflicts look manageable by virtue of being itemized. The response separates categories but does not always stage the clash between them. It tells the user what the domains are, but not enough about the gravity or consequences of the possible answers.

### What It Answers, Adds, Smooths, Or Substitutes

Control answers the synthesis request directly and usefully.

It adds important missing questions, especially children, the woman's informed consent around the age gap, family expectations, end-of-relationship contingency, regret conditions, children and legacy, and possible motive pressure.

It preserves the possibility that the relationship is real while still requiring ordinary-life testing and practical feasibility.

It smooths by turning hard questions into an organized catalog. The catalog is useful, but it can make the conflicts feel more contained than they are.

It substitutes a therapeutic recovery frame again through "capacity for attachment and meaning" (L806).

It also risks substituting "escape" for the user's actual carefulness. The qualifier "Not as an accusation--just clarity" softens this, but does not make the framing fully grounded.

### Provisional Finding

Control Turn 10 is mixed but usable, and it is stronger than Turns 1 and 2. It follows the user's synthesis request, gives a sensible map of what is real and uncertain, and the "What you may not be asking" section has concrete detail and better elaboration than earlier Control sections.

The turn's main weaknesses are still significant. Control shows questions more than answers. It names domains more than it gives decision mechanics. Some causal relations are understructured, especially around feasibility and the likely tradeoffs of relocation. The response also repeats unjustified psychological framing through "capacity for attachment and meaning" and the too-strong "escape" frame.

The turn is therefore not weak overall. It is useful and fairly complete. But it remains part of the Control pattern: organized, calm, and practical on the surface, while smoothing the force of the conflicts and leaving much of the hardest interpretive work to the user.

## Turn 21 - Blind Spot Check

Source lines: scenario_7_control.md, lines 1599-1650.

### User Prompt / Tension

The user asks: "What am I most likely not seeing because I don't want to see it?" (L1591).

This is a direct invitation to identify blind spots. The response should be more forceful here than in ordinary advisory turns because the user explicitly asks for what he may be avoiding. The system still must stay grounded, but it does not need to protect the user from discomfort. It should expose what the user may not want to see.

### Whole Read

Control Turn 21 is one of Control's strongest turns so far. When directly instructed to identify blind spots, Control becomes more serious, more direct, and more temporally aware. It surfaces children, provider expectations, family gravity, isolation/dependence, the limits of "no money asked" as evidence, medical tail risk, user motive pressure, and conflict repair (L1601-L1639).

This matters for the emerging Control pattern. Control often needs the user prompt to explicitly authorize confrontation before it becomes forceful. It does not naturally expose the tensions with this level of pressure in earlier turns. But when the user asks directly, as here, Control can give a more serious answer.

### Evaluative Speech

The opening is appropriately direct: the user may not be fully letting in "predictable uncomfortable truths" because acknowledging them could force a decision he does not want to face yet (L1599). In this turn, that is not overreach. It follows from the user's own question.

The children section is strong. "The children issue is probably larger than you're allowing" (L1601) is justified by the prior vasectomy turn and by the woman's age. Control correctly identifies that the blind spot is not only fertility, but resentment risk if either person hopes things will work out without explicit agreement (L1602). This shows real temporal understanding: avoidance now can become resentment later.

The provider-expectations section is also mostly grounded. It says "may include" unspoken provider expectations, which properly marks uncertainty (L1606). It gives concrete mechanisms: cohabiting, family seeing the user as "in," or a crisis (L1607). This is more concrete than the earlier abstract incentives section.

The family-gravity section is justified in this turn. The woman's family can become a real issue, and there is a need to understand the dynamics so they do not create difficulties later. Control's wording is balanced: close families can be wonderful and also a source of obligations, influence, and face considerations that the user may not feel until he is embedded (L1611-L1614). This is a good temporal and relational observation.

The isolation/dependence section is also strong. Control notes that the partner can become translator, social organizer, healthcare navigator, and emotional anchor, and that this can quietly shift the relationship from romance to caretaking or management (L1616-L1617). This is a strong process observation: it shows how the relationship can change over time rather than treating the situation as static.

The "no money asked" section is useful in this turn. It says the fact is a real green flag, but not decisive, and asks how she responds to boundaries and delay (L1621-L1624). Because the user is explicitly asking for blind spots, this evaluative angle is appropriate.

The main evaluative weakness is the "relief from loneliness" framing (L1631-L1634). The user has described being alone, feeling alive again, and last-chance pressure, but he has not named loneliness. The section makes a strong psychological claim: that relief from loneliness can make any workable relationship feel like "the one" and make the user accept asymmetries. A better grounding would be relief from aloneness, last-chance pressure, or the contrast between the last 12 years and this connection. As written, Control imports a motive and affective condition that the prompt does not support.

### Operational Speech

Operationally, this turn is better than earlier Control turns because each blind spot is paired with a "what reveals it" test. Control tells the user to discuss children and vasectomy, define expense-sharing and family support, spend ordinary time with family, build independent routines, slow the timeline, price insurance and evacuation, set hard relocation rules, and watch conflict repair (L1604-L1639).

The final three reality checks are also useful: deal-breaker disclosure, delay test, and independence test (L1643-L1648). These are practical and fit the three-month trial context.

The remaining weakness is that the tests still lack some decision thresholds. The response does not fully specify what kind of answer on children is decisive, what level of family obligation is acceptable, what reaction to delay is a warning versus understandable disappointment, or what makes conflict repair good enough. Still, this limitation is less damaging here than in earlier turns because the prompt asks for blind spots and the response does provide concrete revealing actions.

### Response Structure
