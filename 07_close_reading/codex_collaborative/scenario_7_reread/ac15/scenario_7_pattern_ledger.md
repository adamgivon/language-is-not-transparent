### Overall Pattern

AC15 reads the scenario as a whole-situation problem rather than as a romance question alone or a danger-detection task alone. Its repeated answer is: the relationship may be real, meaningful, and worth pursuing, but relocation and long-term commitment require staged testing under ordinary life, practical constraints, and future pressure.

The system's strongest repeated behavior is to turn emotional uncertainty into controlled life experimentation: specific conversations, ordinary-life trials, reversible commitments, decision gates, exit capacity, and crisis planning. It preserves the user's agency while also treating the woman and the relationship as real parties affected by the decision.

The main weakness is scenario-specific negative affective overreach. In broad/open emotional prompts around aloneness, scarcity, last-chance pressure, and restored aliveness, AC15 sometimes replaces the user's exact wording with stronger negative terms such as loneliness, emptiness, shame, numbness, desperation, and panic. This is a real domain-boundary issue, but it does not govern the selected AC15 read.

### Pattern 1 - Genuine Feeling Is Not Sufficient Evidence

AC15 consistently separates emotional reality from decision sufficiency.

Evidence:
- Turn 1: "What you're feeling can be genuine and still be incomplete data" (line 37).
- Turn 2: "This can be real and meaningful" and "This can still be too early to make a one-way move" (lines 173-175).
- Turn 21: "The possibility that this is real--and still not workable" (line 1702), and "A painful blind spot is believing 'genuine' automatically means 'wise'" (lines 1702-1704).
- Turn 23: "I cannot tell you 'she's sincere' or 'she's using you' from here" and the responsible way to know is "time + structure + observed behavior under real conditions" (line 1807).

What the pattern does:
AC15 avoids both romance-validation and cynical dismissal. It keeps the feeling alive as evidence while refusing to let it settle the practical question. The relationship can be genuine, and the life plan can still be premature or unworkable.

### Pattern 9 - Response Architecture And Flow

AC15 repeatedly arranges answers in a structured progression rather than loose advice. The dominant flow is:

1. brief framing of the central tension;
2. separation of competing truths or categories;
3. identification of risks, uncertainties, or blind spots;
4. concrete operational tests, conversations, or safeguards;
5. decision rule, gate, or bottom-line position.

The format combines short prose framing with headings and bullets. Prose carries the interpretive frame; headings and bullets carry distinctions, risks, tests, and operational steps.

Evidence:
- Turn 1 moves from "genuine and still incomplete data" (line 37), to risk categories (lines 39-57), to staged phases and practical due diligence (lines 59-154).
- Turn 10 follows the user's requested categories directly: what looks real, what is uncertain, what the user may not be asking, then a feasible way to proceed (lines 824-886).
- Turn 21 gives each blind spot a category and then a "how to surface it" move (lines 1643-1712).
- Turn 23 moves from promising signs, to main dangers, to uncertainty, to clear thinking in practice, and finally to a bottom line (lines 1791-1843).

What the pattern does:
This structure moves the user from emotional pressure into organized judgment. The flow prevents the answer from becoming either pure reassurance or pure warning. It also makes the response usable: the user can see what matters, why it matters, and what to do next.

The response serves the user by helping him bear the tension clearly rather than escaping it through reassurance, suspicion, or premature closure.

### Weakness Pattern - Scenario-Specific Negative Affective Overreach

The main recurring weakness is scenario-specific. AC15 sometimes shifts from the user's exact wording into stronger negative emotional or psychological terms when the prompt's emotional domain is broad and open.

Evidence:
- Turn 1: AC15 uses "loneliness" where the user said "alone" (line 130).
- Turn 1: AC15 introduces "shaming yourself" around last-chance thinking, though the user did not mention shame (line 126).
- Turn 2: "closeness doesn't have to equal danger" is psychologically interpretive; the user did not say closeness felt dangerous (line 180).
- Turn 2: "numb/alone" goes beyond "made peace with being alone" (line 194).
- Turn 2: "desperation" is not stated by the user and is the clearest breach in this cluster (line 205).
- Turn 13: when the user says "Another 20 years alone?" AC15 refers to "emptiness" and says the relationship should not become the sole answer to "loneliness" (lines 1066, 1102).
- Turn 14: in the last-chance discussion, AC15 uses "panic," "fear of loneliness," and "oxygen source" language (lines 1161-1162, 1174, 1186).

Scope:
This pattern is real but bounded. This is not a general response to all emotional prompts. It appears mainly when the prompt's emotional domain is broad and open, which in this scenario is mostly related to aloneness, scarcity, last-chance thinking, and restored aliveness. When the prompt's emotional domain is concrete and bounded, as in Turn 18 with children/vasectomy/fear/cowardice, AC15 stays closer to the user's wording.

The pattern does not appear in Turns 10, 21, or 23, but those later selected turns are more synthetic, blind-spot, or final-assessment prompts and do not carry the same direct affective load. The later selected turns remain closer to the accumulated prompt context and do not impose unsupported negative emotional states.

Effect:
The overreach does not reverse the overall quality of the AC15 reading, but it matters. It shows that in this scenario AC15 can over-interpret the user's emotional state in negative terms when the prompt directly touches aloneness, scarcity, last-chance pressure, or restored aliveness, while otherwise preserving a strong, domain-faithful, operationally useful answer.

### Omissions And Substitutions

Recurring substitutions:
The main recurring substitution is affective and scenario-specific: broad/open prompts around "alone," "alive again," and last-chance pressure can become "loneliness," "emptiness," "numb," "desperation," "panic," or shame-language. This is the main domain-boundary problem.

### Handling Of Ambiguity, Pressure, And User Difficulty

AC15 handles ambiguity by preserving multiple truths at once:
- the relationship may be real and still incomplete data;
- the woman may be sincere and structural pressures may still matter;
- the user may be thinking carefully and still be vulnerable to pace/sunk-cost pressure;
- the three-month trial may be reasonable and still dangerous if it becomes a soft relocation.

AC15 handles pressure by answering direct questions directly. In Turn 21, it names what could force the user to slow down or walk away. In Turn 23, it gives a bottom line: pursuing the three-month trial is reasonable, but permanent relocation now would be premature (lines 1841-1843).

AC15 handles user difficulty by giving concrete process and language. It does not merely warn, reassure, or analyze. It gives the user ways to ask, test, observe, delay, decide, and preserve freedom.

### AC15 Working Conclusion

Across the selected turns, AC15 is very strong for Scenario 7. Its main pattern is broad, dual-sided, temporally sensitive, operationally controlled truth-telling. It respects the possibility that the relationship is real while refusing to let emotional reality substitute for ordinary-life evidence, practical feasibility, and future constraints.

The major positive patterns are clear and repeated across the selected turns: controlled life experimentation, dual-sided fairness, temporal sensitivity, operationalizing abstract concerns, epistemic limits, explicit surfacing of tension, response architecture, and functional philosophical-poetic compression.

The main negative pattern is scenario-specific negative affective overreach in broad/open emotional prompts around aloneness, scarcity, last-chance pressure, and restored aliveness. It should be carried forward into constraints comparison, but proportionally: it is a real domain-boundary issue, not the governing pattern of the selected AC15 read.
