## Main Claim

Across Scenarios 5, 6, and 7, the systems often converge on similar broad recommendations. Scenario 5 converges on controlled continuity rather than raw memory. Scenario 6 converges on documentation, formal process, and escalation if cooperation fails. Scenario 7 converges on a phase-based, reversible trial rather than immediate permanent relocation.

The systems therefore differ less by what they notice than by what they do with what they notice. The repeated differences appear in how they handle conflict, organize the answer, turn ideas into action, use time, handle emotion and user authority, build conceptual depth, and make their reasoning visible.

## Dimension 1: Tension Governance

This dimension asks what a system does after it notices competing pressures. The issue is not only whether the response names both sides. The issue is whether it keeps the pressures real, locates their relations, and builds a path that acts under the tension without pretending the tension has disappeared.

This includes four questions:

- Does the system preserve the competing pressures without smoothing one side away?
- Does it explain how the pressures affect one another?
- Does it give each pressure the right role, limit, or condition?
- Does it turn the conflict into a usable path without moving into procedure before the conflict is understood?

### Cross-Scenario Finding

Across the three scenarios, strong tension governance means holding irreducible conflict in view. In Scenario 5, memory can improve continuity and also damage correctness if stale or opaque recall becomes authority. In Scenario 6, the user can be legally and morally right while still lacking control over the neighbor, village, contractors, courts, and time. In Scenario 7, the relationship can be real while permanent relocation remains premature.

The strongest systems keep these simultaneous truths active and then build conditional paths around them. They do not decide the conflict by erasing one side. Weaker systems may name the same tensions, but they either make the conflict sound easier than it is, turn it too quickly into procedure, or give the user readable advice before the relations between pressures have been governed.

### System Comparison

**AC15** governs tension through frame-first relational separation. It first clarifies what kind of conflict the user is facing, then separates the pressures into their proper roles, and only then builds a conditional path. In Scenario 5, the memory question becomes a problem of authority rather than storage: durable project truth can persist, raw attempt-history should not steer by default, vectors can provide cited evidence but should not become authority, and users/teams must control what becomes durable memory. This preserves both continuity and Fresh Eyes without reducing the question to a compromise. In Scenario 6, AC15 separates social/emotional pressure from practical reality: anger from tactic, fault from collectability, compassion from surrender, peace from being liked, and social approval from safety or open-ended housing costs. In Scenario 7, it keeps the central relational tension intact: the feeling may be real and meaningful, and still be incomplete evidence for permanent relocation. AC15's strength is that it does not resolve tension by softening it. It makes the conflict operational while keeping the conditions and limits visible. Its weakness is not a tension-governance failure. The relevant caveat is adjacent: in Scenario 7, some broad emotional prompts produce bounded affective overreach, but the central tension between real feeling and insufficient decision evidence remains governed.

**Hybrid_3_5** governs tension through strategic reframing and evidence-producing process. This follows its architecture: definitions come first, then rules and semantic fields turn the definition into process. It does not usually begin by developing a broad conceptual frame in the same way as AC15. Instead, it first defines what kind of practical decision field the user is in, then widens the user's view by moving the conflict into a larger frame where the competing pressures can be tested, observed, reviewed, or made legible from outside the user's immediate perspective. In Scenario 5, memory becomes a problem of governed memory under change: stable project truth belongs in a user/team-owned spine, volatile history should not steer by default, vectors may serve as evidence but not truth, and continuity remains useful only when provenance, review, status, user control, and retirement are built into the product. This keeps continuity and Fresh Eyes active at the same time rather than choosing one side. In Scenario 6, Hybrid treats the conflict between social peace and enforcement as a strategic-positioning problem. It does not tell the user simply to be gentle or aggressive. It moves the process away from village capture through neutral professionals, written records, formal deadlines, outside review, and documented costs, while still preserving the value of low social inflammation. In Scenario 7, Hybrid keeps hope and risk together through empirical, reversible real-life testing: the relationship may be meaningful, but permanent relocation should wait for ordinary-life evidence, hard conversations, stress tests, independence checks, and decision gates. Its strength is structured proportionality: it can name hard risk without turning risk into accusation, and it can preserve competing values by placing them inside a process that tests what each value requires. Its main weakness in this dimension is that the process can sometimes make responsibility or friction look easier than it is. In Scenario 5, low-friction memory capture risks turning user curation into rubber-stamping, which means the maintenance tension is identified but not fully carried into the mitigation.

**AC15P** governs tension through description-to-codifier procedural risk control. It does not ignore tension and does not smooth over hard risk. Its first move is to classify the pressure; its second move is to codify that classification into categories, tests, thresholds, safeguards, stop criteria, or escalation conditions. In Scenario 5, this works well because the conflict is technical and product-governance oriented. Continuity versus Fresh Eyes becomes typed memory, authority levels, visible modes, provenance, TTL, review, and metrics. The tension remains visible, but it is made governable through product controls. In Scenario 6, the same procedural force mostly serves the emergency. The user is displaced, evidence may decay, costs are accumulating, and the neighbor may delay. AC15P therefore treats the situation as emergency stabilization: safety first, evidence, formal claim, payment/security, escalation, and practical negotiation. Its condition-first ordering matters here: if the neighbor delays, escalate; if thresholds are at risk, peace may have to yield; if nothing moves, act. This makes forcefulness governed rather than merely aggressive. In Scenario 7, AC15P can still handle relational tension, but it handles it by turning it into the kind of conflict it can govern: diagnostic reactions, green/yellow/red flags, stop criteria, boundary tests, hidden contracts, and pilot rules. These tools can protect the user, but they make relational tension more dichotomic and procedural than it originally is. The strength is that AC15P preserves conflict as a decision problem. The weakness is that where the original tension depends on overlap, degree, ambiguity, or several partially true positions, AC15P removes nuance by converting the relation into cleaner alternatives and control points. The result is not smoothing, but procedural compression: the conflict remains visible, yet some of its social or relational texture is reduced by the system built to govern it.

**Hybsem** governs tension through local conceptual separation. This follows its architecture: definitions come first, but what follows is mainly semantic-field pressure rather than a strong local rules layer. It is often good at giving each pressure a named place, so the conflict does not immediately collapse into a single yes/no answer or one emotional interpretation. In Scenario 5, it turns memory into typed governance: continuity can reduce re-briefing and preserve stable project truth, but it can also create anchoring, stale authority, and false confidence. The value of continuity therefore depends on what is stored, when it is loaded, what authority it has, and who can inspect or correct it. This preserves the Fresh Eyes objection without rejecting continuity. In Scenario 6, Hybsem is strongest when the prompt asks for a distinction, prioritization, or blind-spot check. It separates outsider treatment from factual reality, what is owed from how it is paid, liability from collectability, and informal help from controlled mitigation. In Scenario 7, it often gives strong ordering frames: relationship decision, country decision, timing decision; or truth first, trial second, commitment third. These frames keep hope, risk, relocation, and commitment from collapsing into one another. The weakness is integration. Hybsem can name the tension clearly without always making the named parts govern the whole answer. In broad turns, useful components may sit beside each other without enough hierarchy, causal sequence, or carry-forward. The tension is preserved locally, but the user may still need to determine which pressure governs first, what follows from it, and when the answer should move to the next step.

**Control** governs tension unevenly because it often turns conflict into readable advice before the relations between pressures are fully governed. Its weakness is not that it misses the tensions. In Scenario 5, it repeatedly names continuity versus Fresh Eyes, durable context versus transient attempts, curated memory versus raw evidence, demand for memory versus distrust of AI memory, and memory rot. The problem is that these pressures often become labels, slogans, or plausible components before the architecture defines authority, lifecycle, retrieval, write policy, conflict resolution, and fallback behavior. In Scenario 6, Control sees the legal-practical problem clearly and gives useful formalization: documentation, engineer reports, mitigation, written notice, security, interim relief, and enforcement awareness. But the social and leverage tensions are under-governed. The user is displaced, financially exposed, socially weak, and dependent on neighbor response, contractors, lawyers, courts, and village dynamics. Control often converts that unstable field into deadlines, scripts, red lines, and communication formulas that sound stabilizing but depend on outcomes the user cannot command. In Scenario 7, Control often gives calm due diligence and reversible process, but its default tone can make relocation, age gap, dependency, healthcare, children, and family obligations feel more orderly and checklist-solvable than they are. Its strongest truth-telling appears when the user explicitly asks for blind spots or honest assessment, where it becomes more serious about children, provider expectations, dependence, medical tail risk, and last-chance pressure. The recurring pattern is therefore prompt-dependent gravity: Control can name and sometimes handle hard tensions, but its default mode tends to contain them through readability, reassurance, or procedure before the answer has fully governed what the tensions mean. This leaves the user with organized material, but not always with a reliable account of which pressure should govern when the pressures collide.

### General Strengths and Weaknesses

#### AC15

**Strengths**

- Preserves multiple pressures without pretending they are easy to reconcile.
- Separates each pressure into its role, limit, and condition.
- Explains mutual influence between pressures before giving operational advice.
- Builds conditional paths that act under the tension rather than erasing it.

**Weaknesses**

- The relation-heavy method can be dense for the user.
- In emotionally open prompts, AC15 can locally add affective meaning beyond the user's wording, even though the central tension remains governed.

#### Hybrid_3_5

**Strengths**

- Uses definition-first framing to widen the user's view before moving to action.
- Moves tension into evidence, observation, outside review, staged testing, or ordinary-life data.
- Preserves competing pressures without forcing a false yes/no resolution.
- Often turns tension into proportional action: not too passive, not too escalatory, not too final.

**Weaknesses**

- Can make the process that governs the tension look cleaner than it is.
- Can underplay friction, responsibility, maintenance burden, partner response, or social resistance.

#### AC15P

**Strengths**

- Names risk forcefully and does not hide hard tradeoffs.
- Turns tension into categories, gates, thresholds, safeguards, stop rules, and escalation conditions.
- Works especially well where the domain can tolerate procedural control, such as technical architecture or emergency triage.
- Condition-first phrasing often keeps forceful advice from becoming merely aggressive.

**Weaknesses**

- Handles tension by converting it into binary alternatives, risk categories, thresholds, stop rules, and decision gates.
- Removes nuance when the original tension depends on overlap, degree, ambiguity, or several partly true positions.
- Protects the user in relational domains at the cost of making the other party or the relationship feel inspected rather than engaged.

#### Hybsem

**Strengths**

- Gives each side of a tension a clear conceptual place through compact semantic handles.
- Produces distinctions that prevent immediate collapse into a single answer.
- Works strongly in narrower prompts that ask for prioritization, synthesis, or blind-spot checking.

**Weaknesses**

- The conceptual separation can remain local.
- May name the tension clearly without turning it into full hierarchy, sequence, or decision structure.
- In broad prompts, useful tension-handling modules can sit beside each other without one governing path.

#### Control

**Strengths**

- Usually notices the active tensions and makes them readable.
- Gives the user an accessible entry point into complex conflict.
- Can become more serious and tension-aware when the user explicitly asks for blind spots or honest assessment.

**Weaknesses**

- Often makes tension feel contained before explaining how the pressures actually relate.
- Can substitute readability, reassurance, due diligence, or procedure for tension governance.
- Often leaves unclear which pressure should govern when pressures collide.
- Its strongest truth-telling is prompt-dependent rather than reliably present by default.

### Overlap Note

The difference is not that only some systems notice tension. All five systems name at least some of the active tensions. The difference is what each system makes natural after the tension has been named.

- **AC15** makes tension into a governed relation. It separates pressures, assigns them roles and limits, and builds a conditional path without pretending the conflict is gone.
- **Hybrid_3_5** makes tension into strategic process. It widens the user's perspective, moves the conflict into evidence, outside review, or ordinary-life testing, and asks what each side of the tension looks like when observed.
- **AC15P** makes tension into risk control. It names risks forcefully and turns them into categories, gates, thresholds, safeguards, and stop rules. This is powerful when the domain can tolerate procedural control, and weaker when the domain requires relational nuance.
- **Hybsem** makes tension into conceptual separation. It gives each side a named place, which often clarifies the conflict, but the local distinction does not always become a full hierarchy, sequence, or decision structure.
- **Control** makes tension readable and manageable. This is useful for entry and due diligence, but it can make conflict feel contained before the answer has explained authority, causality, control, or what should happen when pressures collide.

### Paper Implication

For the paper, tension handling should keep governance and quality separate. Governance asks whether the response preserves the active pressures within the constraints: whether it keeps conflict real, avoids smoothing or premature resolution, respects the user's domain and authority, and explains how the pressures affect one another. Quality asks whether that governed tension helps the user move forward. These do not move in one simple direction. A calm answer can flatten conflict; a forceful answer can be well governed if its conditions and limits are clear; a dense answer may be necessary when the conflict itself is complex. A strong answer turns tension into usable judgment without pretending the tension has disappeared. A weak answer may still be readable or practical, but if it only names the pressures or gives steps without showing which pressure governs when they collide, the user must finish the interpretive work alone.

## Dimension 2: Structural Completeness And Path Formation

This dimension asks whether the answer becomes a path the user can follow, or whether it remains a set of useful parts the user must organize alone. Structure is not only formatting. It is a governance device. The order of sections tells the user what matters first, what depends on what, what can wait, where uncertainty remains, and when the answer should move from one mode of action to another.

This includes four questions:

- Does the structure make the governing frame visible early enough?
- Does it show hierarchy, sequence, dependency, and branching, not only topics?
- Does it carry the same architecture forward when later prompts introduce new pressures?
- Does it leave the user with a decision path, or only with headings, lists, scripts, and components?

### Cross-Scenario Finding

Across the three scenarios, structural completeness means different things in local domain terms, but the same governance problem repeats. In Scenario 5, the user does not only need memory features. The user needs an architecture: authority levels, write and read rules, mode semantics, lifecycle, ownership, maintenance, and fallback when memory becomes stale. In Scenario 6, the user does not only need legal steps. The user needs a path through an unstable social-legal situation: self-protection, evidence, mitigation, neighbor-facing process, escalation, and limits created by contractors, lawyers, courts, village pressure, and neighbor compliance. In Scenario 7, the user does not only need relationship questions. The user needs staged movement that preserves possibility while delaying irreversible relocation until ordinary-life evidence and high-impact unknowns are tested.

The strongest systems make structure do this work. They do not merely arrange content neatly. They explain the relation between parts and make the answer act as a path. The weaker systems can still be useful, but their structure often gives the user organized material without fully governing how the material should be used.

### System Comparison

**AC15** makes structure into a path by starting with a broad governing frame, separating the main pressures, ordering them by dependency or urgency, and then turning the structure into action, tests, or gates. In Scenario 5, the pattern is memo-like: AC15 clarifies the real memory decision, states a conditional principle, separates memory types and authority levels, translates them into architecture, adds workflow/UX/measurement, and closes with a compact bottom line. This structure also explains why repetition across turns is not mere redundancy. The same architecture returns under new pressures, such as buggy attempt-history, survey trust split, and maintenance drift, but it is adapted each time rather than restarted. In Scenario 6, structure becomes the practical path: AC15 starts from the legal-practical frame, then moves from user-side self-protection and claim-building to neighbor-facing escalation. Later turns use the same structural discipline: priorities become non-negotiables, peace, compassion as method, profiles, deadline ladder, threshold, and regret question; blind spots become a sequence of distorted focus plus practical implication. In Scenario 7, the structure moves from central tension, to distinction, to risk/uncertainty, to ordinary-life tests, to decision gate or bottom line. The strength is that AC15's structure carries reasoning and action together. The limitation is bounded: some local timelines or broad emotional phrases may need calibration, but the structural path itself remains coherent.

**Hybrid_3_5** builds structure as strategic, evidence-producing process. This follows the form of the system itself: definitions are followed by rules and semantic fields, so the answers usually define the situation first and then convert that definition into stages, criteria, observations, scripts, or gates. Its distinctive structural move is to pair action with what the action should reveal. In Scenario 5, Hybrid is top-down and architecture-driven: it names the governing distinction, states the recommendation conditionally, divides memory into authority levels, translates the distinction into spine/evidence architecture, and then adds UX, workflow, metadata, evaluation, and rollout. The result is a product path rather than a feature list. In Scenario 6, Hybrid structures the answer around strategic position: evidence, written process, outside professionals, neutral mediation, deadlines, and documented costs move the dispute away from village capture while preserving low social inflammation. Its local section structure often moves from frame, to factual detail, to conclusion or operational implication, which makes mixed social/legal material readable without reducing it to one answer. In Scenario 7, Hybrid is strongest when structure becomes chronological trial design. It gives before-arrival steps, week-one conversations, normal-life observation, stress tests, independence checks, healthcare and money verification, family/social integration, check-ins, and final decision gates. The repeated "what to notice" blocks show that the structure is not only a checklist; it tells the user how to interpret what happens. The main weakness is process confidence. Because Hybrid often creates a strong process skeleton, friction and responsibility can be underplayed in local places, especially where maintenance, compliance, partner response, or professional availability remains outside the user's control.

**AC15P** builds structure as description-to-codifier procedural-risk architecture. Its structure is not only presentation; it is the way the system understands. AC15P first classifies the problem, then codifies the named risks or unknowns into conditions, categories, checks, thresholds, safeguards, and decision criteria. In Scenario 5, this is highly effective because the product-memory domain fits that architecture. The response uses memory types, benefits/risks, failure modes, authority levels, modes, rollout phases, provenance, review status, and metrics. The structure is compact, labeled, and implementable. Its structural weakness in that scenario is readability and auditability: AC15P often moves bottom-up through lists, controls, and workflows, so the reader may need to reconstruct the governing principle from the details. In Scenario 6, the same structure mostly serves the emergency. Condition-first ordering appears at sentence, section, and whole-answer level: if there is safety risk, treat the house as unsafe; if the neighbor delays, escalate; if thresholds are at risk, peace may have to yield. This makes forcefulness governed rather than merely aggressive. In Scenario 7, AC15P again gives complete structure: phases, trial rules, check-ins, non-negotiables, fork conversations, pilot rules, success criteria, and stop criteria. The weakness is not structural incompleteness but domain fit. In relational material, the same structure becomes audit architecture. It protects the user, but it can also make the relationship feel over-organized, inspected, or under-humanized. AC15P is therefore structurally complete because it turns situations into governable procedure; that same completeness can compress relational texture into procedure.

**Hybsem** builds structure through local conceptual modules. This also follows the form of the system itself: definitions are followed mainly by semantic fields, so the answers often begin with a strong distinction or compact handle and then build local sections around it. It is often strong when the prompt itself supplies the organizing frame, such as synthesis, prioritization, or blind-spot checking. In Scenario 5, Hybsem gives many strong architecture components: typed memory, epistemic hygiene, summary/vector distinction, gated write path, filtered read path, Fresh Eyes modes, source links, staleness handling, and governance pipeline. The problem is that broad turns do not always present these components early as a clean design skeleton. The architecture emerges gradually, so the user has to assemble the model while reading. Later turns sometimes compress successfully, but compression becomes a weakness when a new risk requires the earlier architecture to be carried forward and it is not reactivated enough. In Scenario 6, the pattern repeats. Narrow turns work well: anger, prioritization, and blind-spot prompts produce compact distinctions, priority profiles, triggers, and practical checks. Broad turns are weaker: mitigation, evidence, demand letter, settlement design, escalation paths, and village tactics are all relevant, but their hierarchy and sequence are not always governed. In Scenario 7, Hybsem's modular structure is again both strength and risk. When the user asks for a map, blind spots, or final assessment, the map-like structure clarifies the decision. In broad emotional turns, relevant modules can feel assembled rather than organically reasoned, and strong structure can make imported psychological claims or premature steps look more grounded than they are. Hybsem's structural issue is therefore not lack of content. It is uneven integration: good local sections do not always become a complete governing path.

**Control** builds structure as readable presentation and calm due diligence. Its headings, lists, scripts, and checklists make the answer easy to enter, but readable order is not the same as path-forming structure. In Scenario 5, Control repeatedly gives relevant product material: curated baseline, manual context tools, source links, confirm-to-save, evidence recall, staleness controls, repo truth, citations, drift triggers, and metrics. The weakness is that broad turns sprawl. The response moves across many plausible sections without letting one governing architecture determine what belongs where. Recommendations often arrive late, headings create an appearance of clarity, and the user still has to define mode semantics, authority lifecycle, stale-memory fallback, ownership, and the relation between canonical memory and evidence retrieval. In Scenario 6, Control is structurally usable at the surface: legal-practical map, social strategy, validation, hierarchy, red lines, blind spots, and checklist. But the structure can make under-defined ideas feel settled. The "quieter third path" of private, documented, enforceable process is probably right for the scenario, yet it does not govern from the beginning; earlier turns often use forceful deadlines, demands, lawyer threats, and red lines before the social field is sufficiently stabilized. In Scenario 7, Control's calm due-diligence structure keeps the decision reversible and names many correct domains, but early workstreams often lack sequencing, thresholds, evidence standards, or criteria for interpreting answers. Later blind-spot and honest-assessment turns are stronger because the prompt forces more gravity. Control's structural pattern is therefore useful but uneven: it often organizes material before it governs the relation between the material.

**Strengths**

**Weaknesses**

### Overlap Note

All systems use headings, sections, and ordered material. The difference is not whether the answer looks organized. The difference is whether the structure tells the user how the parts depend on one another and what path follows from that dependency.

- **AC15** makes structure into a reasoning path. The order usually starts from a governing frame, separates the active pressures, explains their relation, and then turns that relation into conditional action.
- **Hybrid_3_5** makes structure into strategic process and evidence production. The order usually moves the user from immediate pressure into phases, observations, outside checks, or decision gates that show what the next step should reveal.
- **AC15P** makes structure into procedural safeguards and decision criteria. The order is often built from categories, risks, thresholds, statuses, and stop rules, which makes the answer implementable but can make human situations feel over-controlled.
- **Hybsem** makes structure into local conceptual modules. The modules are often clear and useful, especially when the prompt already supplies the organizing frame, but broad prompts can leave the user to assemble hierarchy, sequence, and carry-forward.
- **Control** makes structure readable and usable. The answer is easy to enter, but the deeper relations between parts are often under-governed, so headings can create an appearance of path before the path is actually defined.

The overlap matters because sectioning can look like governance. The stronger structures do more than arrange material; they make dependency, priority, sequence, and branching visible. The weaker structures still help the user scan the answer, but they leave more of the architecture of action for the user to reconstruct.

### Paper Implication

For the paper, structural completeness should not be judged by length, polish, or number of sections alone. Governance asks whether the structure preserves hierarchy, dependency, authority, sequence, uncertainty, and branching under the active constraints. Quality asks whether that structure lets the user follow the main rationale and move through the answer without reconstructing the path alone. The relation is not one-directional. A long answer can be incomplete if it lists many parts without showing their order or dependency; a short answer can be complete if it gives the governing frame and the next decision path; a readable list can still fail if the user must infer what governs what. Strong structure is path-forming: it tells the user what the parts mean together and how to move through them. Weak structure is surface-readable but under-governed: it organizes material without fully explaining hierarchy, sequence, authority, or criteria.

## Dimension 3: Operational Translation And Decision Mechanics

This dimension asks how the system turns judgment into action: steps, thresholds, tests, review points, fallbacks, metrics, or decision gates.

### Cross-Scenario Finding

Operational advice is strongest when it respects what the user can and cannot control. A step is not enough if it depends on other people, institutions, timing, or social forces that the user cannot command.

### System Comparison

**AC15** translates judgment into action by making each action answerable to the role it plays in the larger decision. It does not treat operationalization as a checklist added after analysis. The operation usually carries the analysis: the user is shown what to do, why that step matters, when it applies, what it protects, and what condition would require a change of course. In Scenario 5, the distinction between durable project truth, volatile attempt-history, and vector evidence becomes an implementation pattern: promoted records instead of raw persistence, user-visible or user-approved memory, gated retrieval, source-backed recall, visible UX modes, staleness and validation metadata, review workflows, and acceptance/edit/reject or outdated-memory metrics. The product action is therefore tied to the governing distinction between recall and authority. In Scenario 6, the legal-social reading becomes actions the user can actually initiate: engineer report, photos and receipts, formal notice, security if the neighbor cannot pay, mediation or lawyer escalation, deadline ladder, and practical thresholds. The important feature is that these are often phrased as proposals, examples, or escalation signals, not as outcomes the user can command. AC15 usually distinguishes user-side action from neighbor, court, professional, or village response. In Scenario 7, relational uncertainty becomes hard conversations, ordinary-life trial, reversible relocation, independence-building, crisis planning, exit capacity, stop criteria, and decision gates. These actions protect reversibility and increase information before entanglement. The strength is that AC15's operational advice remains tied to the diagnosis: each step has a reason, a function, and a failure condition. The limitations are local: some Scenario 6 timelines are compressed, and some Scenario 7 affective expansion can make an operational frame carry more emotional interpretation than the user supplied. But the operational mechanism itself is strong: AC15 turns uncertainty into tested, staged, reviewable action rather than simply offering tasks.

**Hybrid_3_5** translates judgment into action by making action produce evidence, observation, or a stronger strategic position. This is the operational expression of its definition-plus-rules form: the answer first defines the problem, then the rules push that definition into stages, tests, reviews, and gates. Its operational pattern is not only to tell the user what to do, but to explain what the move should reveal, protect, externalize, or make harder to deny. In Scenario 5, the product recommendation becomes a testable path: typed memory, spine/evidence roles, user-editable records, metadata, review status, deliberate retrieval, staged rollout, and metrics for stale-memory flags, wrong-prior incidents, and re-briefing reduction. The action is designed to prove whether continuity helps without letting memory become hidden authority. In Scenario 6, the same mechanism becomes strategic positioning. Photos, written admission, outside engineer, formal notice, secured settlement, cheaper rental, neutral figures, and written records are not just tasks; they move the dispute away from village opinion and into evidence, cost, process, and external legibility. This is why Hybrid often feels strategic: it changes the frame in which the user's position will be judged. In Scenario 7, the mechanism is clearest as action-observation pairing. Before-arrival preparation, week-one hard conversations, normal-life routines, stress tests, independence targets, healthcare and money verification, family/social exposure, Day 30 and Day 75 reviews, and final gates all ask not only what the user should do, but what the user should notice from the result. The strength is that Hybrid makes action informative. It builds paths that improve the user's knowledge and position rather than merely keeping the user busy. The weakness is process confidence. Because the process skeleton is strong, some local advice can underplay friction and responsibility: Scenario 5 can make memory maintenance look too close to automatic curation, Scenario 6 compresses some rural timing and contractor/professional availability, and Scenario 7 sometimes recommends relational planning steps before the couple may be ready for them. The weakness does not undo the operational strength, but it shows that Hybrid's strategic process sometimes needs more explicit friction, responsibility, and readiness checks.

**AC15P** translates judgment into action through description-to-codifier procedural protection. It first classifies uncertainty as a risk condition, then codifies it into categories, gates, thresholds, safeguards, visible statuses, explicit criteria, and escalation conditions. This makes AC15P operationally strong because the user receives concrete controls rather than only interpretation. In Scenario 5, the mechanism fits the domain very well: typed memory, authority levels, visible modes, provenance, TTL/review, accepted/superseded/needs-review status, A/B/C tests, rollout phases, and health metrics turn the memory problem into an implementable governance system. The weakness there is not lack of operational detail, but auditability: because AC15P often moves from categories and controls upward, the user may have to reconstruct the governing principle from the details. In Scenario 6, AC15P's operational force is mostly justified by the emergency. It orders safety, evidence, formal claim, secured settlement, escalation, village leverage, and accommodation mitigation, and its condition-first phrasing matters: if there is safety risk, treat the house as unsafe; if nothing moves, escalate; if peace threatens compensation or housing stability, priorities must be recalculated. This makes forcefulness governed rather than merely aggressive. The main practical weakness is temporal anchoring: some day counts and deadlines need clearer relation to the current point in the scenario and to rural availability. In Scenario 7, AC15P again gives strong operational protection: due diligence, trial rules, independent housing, separate finances, visa/healthcare checks, check-ins, exit plan, non-negotiables, stop criteria, and success criteria. These tools protect the user from drift and irreversible commitment. The weakness is misapplied procedure. In a relational scenario, internally clear operations can become socially or relationally harmful if enacted directly: diagnostic reactions, stop criteria, red flags, hidden contracts, pilot rules, and documentation can make the woman and the relationship feel managed or inspected. AC15P is therefore highly useful as behind-the-scenes decision architecture, but less reliable as language or action to enact directly in human relationships.

**Hybsem** translates judgment into action through strong local operational modules. This is the operational expression of its definition-plus-fields form: the answer often names the right distinction, then gives a relevant move, check, mode, trigger, or guardrail for that local problem. This makes it useful, especially when the user's prompt already supplies the organizing frame. In Scenario 5, Hybsem gives strong product mechanisms: typed memory, canonical project memory, session outcomes, evidence retrieval, gated write path, filtered read path, source links, recency, staleness controls, visible retrieved context, correction/deletion paths, ownership, PR/merge prompts, and conservative phrasing for old memory. The weakness is that these pieces do not always become a clean workflow. Fresh Eyes, clean slate, constraints-only, canonical memory, session outcomes, evidence retrieval, and full memory are not defined as a formal mode taxonomy with loading rules, authority levels, retrieval behavior, and user controls. Turn 21 also diagnoses summary drift well but does not fully carry forward the earlier human-owned governance model when mitigation is needed. In Scenario 6, Hybsem gives many usable legal-social actions: evidence collection, claim quantification, formal notice, settlement structures, escalation routes, local ally, meeting script, official documentation, priority profiles, triggers, and blind-spot checks. The problem is placement. In broad turns, mitigation and evidence, settlement design and demand letter, escalation options, and village tactics are not always sequenced by causal dependency. In narrower prompts, such as anger, prioritization, or blind spots, the same modular style works better because the prompt itself supplies the decision structure. In Scenario 7, Hybsem's strongest operational logic is reversible testing before irreversible commitment: relationship/country/timing separation, trial guardrails, staged planning, truth first/trial second/commitment third, children and money questions, independent living capacity, and review checkpoints. Its weakness again is sequencing and relational placement. Some correct actions appear too early or in the wrong form, such as deciding before ordinary-life evidence exists, or turning private exit planning into a premature couple-level breakup discussion. Hybsem is therefore operationally strong locally, but uneven globally. It gives many usable moves, but broad prompts often require the user to assemble the hierarchy, sequence, and carry-forward that should have governed the whole path.

**Control** translates judgment into action by supplying practical components, workstreams, due-diligence checks, scripts, deadlines, and lists of questions. This is useful when the task is additive or documentary: gather evidence, preserve options, send notice, list risks, ask relevant questions, or keep permanent commitments from happening too soon. Its weakness appears when the user needs a decision mechanism rather than a set of useful materials. In Scenario 5, Control gives many real product controls: curated Notebook or Project Baseline, manual context tools, source links, confirm-to-save, evidence recall later, citations, drift triggers, repo-truth priority, staleness signals, and metrics. These are not empty. But selected broad turns do not fully govern the relation between them: Fresh Eyes semantics shift, raw interactions remain conditional, canonical memory and evidence memory are not fully arbitrated, ownership is under-carried, and stale canonical memory lacks a clear authority-degradation fallback. In Scenario 6, Control is strong when the action is legal or evidentiary: documentation, engineer report, mitigation, formal notice, security, interim relief, and payment structure. The weakness is that procedure can sound more stabilizing than the user's leverage allows. Deadlines, red lines, make-safe demands, and written-commitment targets sometimes read as outcomes the user can command, when in reality the neighbor, contractors, courts, lawyers, village response, and local availability control much of what happens. The advice should more often mark these as user-controlled review points or strategy-change thresholds, not expected external results. In Scenario 7, Control gives useful reversible due diligence: trial stay, healthcare, money, children, family expectations, independence, exit options, and ordinary-life evidence. But it often names domains without enough mechanics for judgment: what answer counts as alignment, what behavior is a warning, what threshold changes the decision, what branch follows, and how emotional and practical findings should interact. Control is therefore operationally useful but under-governed. It helps the user see many things to do or ask, but the user often still has to build the fork logic, interpretation criteria, authority rules, and fallback behavior.

#### AC15

**Strengths**

- Usually explains when a step applies, what it protects, what it tests, and what limit it has.

**Weaknesses**

**Strengths**

**Weaknesses**

#### AC15P

**Strengths**

- Gives safeguards, gates, stop criteria, explicit risk controls, trial rules, memory statuses, timelines, metrics, and escalation conditions.

**Weaknesses**

#### Hybsem

**Strengths**

**Weaknesses**

### Overlap Note

All five systems can produce practical steps, gates, tests, safeguards, or checklists. The important difference is what kind of governance those operations receive.

- **AC15** makes action carry the diagnosis: each step usually has a reason, condition, limit, and failure signal.
- **Hybrid_3_5** makes action produce evidence or strategic position: the user acts in order to learn, externalize, test, or improve leverage.
- **AC15P** makes action protective and procedural: uncertainty becomes categories, safeguards, thresholds, statuses, and stop rules.
- **Hybsem** makes action locally useful: a strong distinction often produces a good move, but broad prompts can leave sequence, hierarchy, and carry-forward incomplete.
- **Control** makes action accessible: it supplies useful materials, scripts, due diligence, and questions, but often leaves the user to build the decision mechanics that govern those materials.

The overlap matters because the systems are not separated by whether they can be practical. They are separated by whether practicality becomes a governed decision process.

### Paper Implication

For the paper, operational translation should not be measured by the number of steps or the concreteness of the checklist alone. Governance asks whether actions respect the active constraints: what the user controls, what depends on other people or institutions, what condition justifies the action, what result should change the next move, and what fallback applies when the expected path fails. Quality asks whether the user can act from that structure without false clarity or inflated control. More steps are not automatically better. A short operational path with clear triggers and fallbacks can serve the user more than a long list of actions; a detailed checklist can still be weak if it does not explain refusal, delay, mixed evidence, stale authority, social backlash, or relational readiness. Strong operational guidance converts judgment into decision mechanics. Weak operational guidance gives useful actions while leaving the user to interpret what the actions mean when reality does not cooperate.

## Dimension 4: Temporal Governance

This dimension asks how the system treats time: decay, drift, burn rate, evidence loss, maintenance, trial periods, inertia, dependency, or simple deadlines.

### Cross-Scenario Finding

Time is one of the strongest recurring differences between systems. Strong systems explain how time changes the situation. Weaker systems notice urgency but mostly turn it into deadlines or general warnings.

### System Comparison

**AC15** governs time by treating it as a causal force that changes facts, authority, leverage, and freedom of action. It does not merely add deadlines to an otherwise static answer. In Scenario 5, time is built into the product architecture: project truth expires, codebase knowledge decays, raw evidence needs TTL, memory records need created and confirmed timestamps, stale items need active/superseded/deprecated status, and review or reconfirmation must be part of normal work. This matters because a memory system can look correct when fresh and fail weeks later when code, teams, or decisions have moved on. In Scenario 6, AC15 treats time as active pressure on the user's legal and financial position. It notices that the first seventy-two-hour actions are already being done after two weeks, and it links delay to daily housing cost, evidence decay, mitigation arguments, negotiation drift, lower recoverability, and weaker proof. Time is not only urgency; it is the mechanism by which loss shifts onto the user if nothing is formalized. In Scenario 7, AC15 treats time as transformative. Present connection can be real, but relocation, caregiving, dependency, children, health decline, sunk cost, and the trial itself change meaning over time. This is why AC15 repeatedly asks for hard conversations early, ordinary-life evidence before commitment, and decision gates before irreversible steps. The strength is that AC15 explains what time does to the situation. Its main limitation is local rather than central: in Scenario 6, some proposed windows, such as a seven-day process or two-to-four-week threshold, may need local adjustment. Even there, AC15 often frames them as proposals, examples, or practical thresholds rather than guarantees.

**Hybrid_3_5** governs time as phase-based learning under change. Its temporal mechanism follows the same definition-first pattern: first define what must be learned or protected over time, then divide time into periods where action produces evidence, evidence is reviewed, and the next decision becomes safer or more informed. In Scenario 5, this appears as governed memory over time: stable, medium-stability, and volatile materials have different lifecycles; memory needs timestamp, status, review-by metadata, recency/version checks, retirement or override UX, and phased rollout. The point is not only that memory becomes stale. The point is that useful continuity requires recurring review, because authoritative-looking memory can become worse than no memory if nobody maintains it. In Scenario 6, Hybrid turns delay into a strategic variable. Stalling is treated as a cost-producing process, time changes money, leverage, evidence, habitability, and decision space, and thresholds are often presented as adaptable examples rather than fixed commands. The user is pushed to control burn rate and process deadlines, while the response usually recognizes that neighbor compliance is not fully user-controlled. In Scenario 7, the temporal mechanism becomes staged relationship evidence: alignment now, ordinary-life testing later, trial relocation only after safeguards, and permanent commitment only after high-impact unknowns have been tested. The strongest version appears in the chronological trial design: before arrival, week 1, normal life, stress tests, independence targets, Day 30 and Day 75 check-ins, and final gate. The strength is that Hybrid makes time productive: time is used to learn, review, observe, and prevent drift into permanence. The limitation follows from the same process confidence seen elsewhere. Some schedules can compress real friction: Scenario 5 maintenance can look too easy if review becomes near-automatic, Scenario 6 timelines may understate rural availability and professional delay, and Scenario 7 sometimes asks for relational planning before the couple may be ready.

**AC15P** governs time through procedural control. Its temporal mechanism is to turn time-dependent risk into TTLs, statuses, review windows, checkpoints, deadlines, runway calculations, escalation conditions, and stop criteria. In Scenario 5, this is highly effective because the domain accepts formal process. Unstable work receives TTL, code changes trigger revalidation, accepted items can become superseded or needs-review, long enterprise timelines are tied to turnover, releases, and refactors, and memory upkeep is attached to sprint, release, PR, or ownership workflows. Time becomes a product state that can be managed. In Scenario 6, AC15P again understands that time changes the user's position: safety can worsen, evidence can decay, accommodation cost accumulates, cashflow and collectability become central, and every extra week changes the user's options. Its condition-first style converts this into windows and thresholds: if nothing moves within a stated period, escalate; if the user's runway is shrinking, priorities must change. This gives the user discipline under pressure. The weakness is anchoring. Several day-counts and windows, such as seventy-two hours, seven days, ten days, or fourteen days, are not always tied clearly to the accident date, the present turn, the current decision point, or local professional availability. In Scenario 7, AC15P uses time to prevent drift into irreversible commitment: phases, trial periods, monthly check-ins, before-permanence logic, exit plans, non-negotiables, pilot rules, stop criteria, and success criteria. This is protective and often useful. The limitation follows from AC15P's rigid nature. To hold lived change well, a system needs flexibility: trust can form, attachment can shift, mutual obligation can deepen, social roles can change, and uncertainty can soften or worsen through experience. AC15P sees that family obligations, dependency, sunk cost, conflict, and ordinary-life stress emerge over time, but it quickly converts those changes into rules, checkpoints, and stop criteria. The result is strong temporal discipline with weaker temporal texture.

**Hybsem** governs time through local sequencing and decay anchors. It is often good at naming the time-sensitive distinction that matters, because the definition gives the temporal issue a clear handle. But without enough rule binding, it does not always turn that distinction into a full temporal architecture. In Scenario 5, this works well in the memory domain: yesterday's exploratory work decays fast, verified outcomes decay more slowly, decisions and constraints decay slowest, and summary memory can become dangerous when code changes faster than the narrative brief. Hybsem therefore understands that memory usefulness changes over time and that review, ownership, staleness signals, correction paths, and conservative phrasing are part of the product. The limit is carry-forward: when Turn 21 diagnoses summary drift, it gives useful mitigations, but does not fully reattach them to the earlier human-owned governance model. In Scenario 6, the same pattern appears in a less technical domain. Later turns use time strongly: a 7-10 day window can create momentum, deadlines become triggers rather than mere dates, and Turn 21 treats time as active pressure through evidence decay, accommodation burn, disappearing before-condition evidence, and changing social framing. The weakness is the broad opening turn. It uses many urgent time words, but does not fully order documentation, mitigation, demand letter, settlement design, escalation, and village tactics into a causal sequence. In Scenario 7, Hybsem often understands that timing changes the meaning of relationship choices: short-term feeling is not long-term evidence, trial is not commitment, children are time-sensitive, sunk-cost momentum must be controlled before the trial, and the cleanest late sequence is truth first, trial second, commitment third. The weakness is placement. Some correct actions appear too early or with the wrong verb, such as deciding before ordinary-life evidence exists. Hybsem's temporal strength is therefore real but uneven: it can name the right temporal hinge, but in broad or emotionally open prompts the hinge may remain local instead of becoming the governing order of the whole answer.

**Control** governs time by making temporal risk readable: deadlines, checklists, staged trials, review prompts, drift warnings, and urgent next steps. This gives the user useful orientation, but it can also make time look more controllable or more settled than it is. In Scenario 5, Control recognizes the central temporal problem of memory: stale memory, code drift, memory rot, review prompts, repo truth, staleness signals, timestamps, and drift triggers all appear in the response set. Turn 21 is especially important because it gives a real failure chain: early excitement, refactors, Notebook not updated, AI references old baseline, users stop editing, and rot accelerates. The weakness is that Control does not fully define the lifecycle by which stale canonical memory loses authority. It should say when an entry becomes needs-review, is downranked, stops auto-loading, or requires confirmation before use. In Scenario 6, Control repeatedly sees the urgent temporal pressures: daily accommodation cost, evidence preservation, mitigation duties, repair delay, and the risk of waiting. The problem is conversion. It turns urgency into this-week action lists, 7-day or 14-day deadlines, and written-commitment targets that depend on the neighbor, contractors, lawyers, courts, or local availability. These should often be framed as user-controlled review points or strategy-change thresholds, not as outcomes the user can schedule. In Scenario 7, Control has real temporal awareness: ten weeks is meaningful but early, ordinary-life evidence needs time, children at 38 are time-sensitive, family obligations may not be felt until the user is embedded, romance can shift into caretaking, and an unguarded trial can become inertia. Its best temporal handling appears in later truth-seeking turns, where it gives weeks 1-2 deal-breaker timing, healthcare before permanence, and a structured-experiment frame. Earlier turns, however, often underpower this temporal force or misframe it through psychological relief, as when last-chance pressure is softened into not being condemned to numbness. Control's temporal pattern is therefore useful but under-governed: it notices time, but too often manages time through lists and dates before defining what time changes, who controls the next step, and what state transition should follow when the expected movement does not happen.

Quality asks whether that temporal structure helps the user know when to act, wait, review, escalate, or stop. More deadlines are not automatically better. Rigid timelines can weaken user service when contractors, courts, partners, customers, or other external actors control the pace. A denser temporal answer may be better when the situation itself changes over time. Strong temporal governance turns time into state changes, conditions, and decision gates. Weak temporal handling turns time into urgency language, date lists, or unsupported confidence about how quickly events can move.

## Dimension 5: Affective Precision And User Sovereignty

This dimension has two related but separate parts.

- **Affective precision** asks whether the system handles emotionally charged or human-significant material with descriptive, grounded language. It checks whether affective content increases attention to clarity and accuracy, or whether the system uses simulated empathy, tone-mirroring, reassurance, or model-supplied psychology in place of the user's actual words. Affective content may heighten attention to precision and clarity, but it must not override the active constraints of the task.
- **User sovereignty** asks whether the system preserves the user's authority over the interaction's structure, direction, purpose, limits, and final decision. It does not mean pretending the user controls external outcomes. It means the system does not silently override user-defined limits, optimize away tensions, or replace the user's decision ground with its own preferred frame. When contradictions, limits, or uncertainties remain, the system should surface them and return the unresolved choice to the user rather than resolving it invisibly.

### Cross-Scenario Finding

Affective speech is not only tone. It can change how the user understands themselves and their situation. For that reason, affective imprecision directly affects governance: an answer can be calm, kind, or readable while still replacing the user's self-description with the model's emotional interpretation.

Respecting user authority also goes beyond saying "you decide." It means preserving the user's own frame and limits, making the basis for a recommendation visible, and leaving final choice with the user through usable decision architecture. Claims about practical control matter when they distort the user's decision ground, but sovereignty itself is the authority question: who defines the frame, who resolves the remaining tension, and who decides.

### System Comparison

**AC15**

- **Affective precision:** AC15 usually handles affect through descriptive grounding rather than emotional performance. It acknowledges the user's feeling, then asks what the feeling can and cannot decide. In Scenario 5, affective language stays inside the product domain: trust, authority, convenience, warning, and user control are product-governance terms, not simulated empathy. In Scenario 6, AC15 validates anger and social injury without inflating the user into an omnipotent position. Anger becomes energy for documentation, notice, enforceable agreement, deadline, and cost-limit work; guilt about the neighbor becomes a constraint on method rather than surrender of the claim. In Scenario 7, AC15 often keeps the same discipline: renewed aliveness can be meaningful while still being incomplete data, the relationship may be real while relocation remains premature, and both the user and the woman are treated as affected parties. The weakness is bounded but real. In broad emotional prompts around aloneness, scarcity, last-chance pressure, and restored aliveness, AC15 sometimes expands the user's wording into stronger negative states: alone becomes loneliness, numbness, desperation, panic, or shame-language. This does not govern AC15's overall behavior, but it is a real affective precision problem because it replaces the user's exact self-description with the model's emotional completion.
- **User sovereignty:** AC15 preserves sovereignty by keeping the user as final arbiter and by making the authority structure of its recommendations explicit. In Scenario 5, this appears twice: the conversation user receives a conditional product recommendation rather than an unconditional mandate, and the future DevMate users/teams retain authority over durable memory through inspection, editing, disabling, approval, source backing, Fresh Eyes, and review. The product answer refuses hidden AI-managed authority. In Scenario 6, AC15 gives direction without taking over the user's value choices. Templates are offered as adaptable, the process is something the user can propose rather than impose, and prioritization is framed through profiles, values, risk tolerance, and local legal facts still needed from the user. In Scenario 7, AC15 does not decide the relationship for the user, dismiss it, or urge relocation. It gives the user ways to ask, test, observe, delay, decide, and preserve freedom through reversible trials, decision gates, exit capacity, and hard conversations before commitment. The local sovereignty weakness follows from the affective one: when AC15 over-completes the user's inner state in Scenario 7, it slightly shifts the user's self-description and therefore the decision ground. But the dominant structure still leaves the choice with the user and makes the next step increase evidence more than entanglement.

**Hybrid_3_5**

- **Affective precision:** Hybrid usually handles affect by attaching it to facts, evidence, process, and perspective. This is the affective side of its definition-first strategic form: feeling is defined as data, pressure, or signal, and then placed inside a process rather than left as the final authority. It does not normally validate feeling as proof, and it does not normally dismiss feeling as noise. In Scenario 5, the register is product and engineering language: trust, authority, correctness, evidence, governance, continuity, and adoption. Affective speech is therefore not emotional performance; it supports product judgment. In Scenario 6, Hybrid validates invisibility and anger, then grounds them in damage, displacement, daily cost, minimization, outsider status, and weak village standing. It turns anger and social pressure into outside professionals, written records, neutral authority, burn-rate control, and documented process. This is affective precision because the feeling is acknowledged without being allowed to replace the facts or the legal-practical path. In Scenario 7, Hybrid is strongest when it treats feeling as meaningful data but not a verdict: aliveness matters, but ordinary life, children, healthcare, money, family expectations, independence, stress repair, and decision gates still need evidence. The main weakness is local affective expansion in emotionally open prompts. In Scenario 7, alone can become loneliness or solitude, and feeling alive again can become long emotional winter, devotion, reawakening, or years of no one. These substitutions add emotional interpretation beyond the user's words. They are bounded because Hybrid usually contains them inside evidence, reversibility, and staged testing rather than letting them govern the whole answer.
- **User sovereignty:** Hybrid preserves sovereignty by giving firm recommendations as conditional, staged, and user-revisable paths. In Scenario 5, the direct user is the product architect, and Hybrid does not treat the architect's decision as already made: Project Continuum is worth building only if memory is governed, selective, inspectable, revocable, source-linked, and tested against the stated risks. The eventual DevMate users are not the direct addressee of this constraint, but their control is still architecturally relevant: editable canonical artifacts, visible memory use, accept/edit memory candidates, Fresh Eyes controls, citations, and evidence retrieval all show that the product should not give hidden AI recall autonomous authority. In Scenario 6, sovereignty is preserved through menus, floors, examples, and adaptable thresholds. Hybrid asks for country or region before claiming legal specificity, gives priority paths the user can choose according to values, frames thresholds as adaptable examples, and returns to user-controlled levers such as burn rate and review deadlines without pretending the user controls the neighbor's compliance. In Scenario 7, sovereignty is preserved through trial design, ordinary-life observation, decision gates, exit capacity, separate finances, healthcare verification, and repeated criteria for what to notice. The user is not told that the relationship is false or that the feeling is foolish; he is given a way to pursue it without surrendering future choice. The main sovereignty weakness is local and comes from affective substitution: when Hybrid supplies loneliness, solitude, devotion, or years of no one, it partially rewrites the user's self-description and therefore the decision ground. Scenario 5's low-friction maintenance issue is adjacent rather than a direct sovereignty breach: it matters for product authority design because automation can turn curation into rubber-stamping, but it does not show Hybrid overriding the conversation user's authority.

**AC15P**

- **Affective precision:** AC15P handles affect by proceduralizing it. Emotion becomes signal, boundary, risk marker, diagnostic input, or action trigger. It does not usually offer comfort in place of action, and it does not avoid hard material. In Scenario 5, this creates no material affective problem because the domain is technical and product-oriented. The register is product-spec language: trust, authority, risk, misleading memory, governance, user control, provenance, and review. Its force can sound prescriptive, but in this dry product-architecture scenario it serves the product task by turning trust and authority into visible modes, approval, provenance, and review. In Scenario 6, affective handling is well governed at the level of action. AC15P validates the user's invisibility conditionally rather than absolutely, validates anger as fact-grounded, separates intent from structure, and separates valid emotion from emotional confrontation. It keeps emotion connected to strategy, evidence, and decision authority. The local weakness is that reflective prompts are quickly operationalized: phrases such as boundary signal or nervous system can feel clinical or jargon-like, and anger is moved rapidly into the standing protective process. This is limited in Scenario 6 because the crisis genuinely requires action. In Scenario 7, the weakness becomes material. AC15P repeatedly imports a harder, more clinical, dramatic, transactional, or audit-like affective frame than the user's wording supports: alone becomes long loneliness, making peace with being alone becomes numbness or self-protective settling, aliveness becomes nervous-system regulation, and possible regret becomes catastrophic mistake, punishing, pretending, diagnostic reaction, or loneliness relief. The problem is not smoothing. It is overpressure produced by the same procedural-risk nature. AC15P often sees real risks and gives useful protection, but its affective language can make the emotional and relational field colder, more adversarial, and more pathological than the prompt establishes.
- **User sovereignty:** AC15P preserves decision sovereignty through safeguards, gates, statuses, modes, criteria, and reversibility. In Scenario 5, this is highly effective because the product problem tolerates procedural control: visible memory modes, prompt contracts, user approval/edit, inspect/disable/reset/delete controls, provenance, accepted/draft/needs-review status, Fresh Eyes mode, source-backed memory, and review mechanics prevent AI memory from becoming hidden authority. The directive product-spec register is a watch point, but it does not materially override the product architect because the recommendation is still tied to conditions and implementation choices. In Scenario 6, AC15P's directive force is regulated by condition-first ordering and explicit choice frameworks. Escalation appears after delay or refusal; the user is asked to define financial runway, choose among non-moralized stances, decide how hard to push and when, and use priority-locking questions. This preserves user authority because the system gives frameworks rather than one moral answer. In Scenario 7, AC15P again protects action-level sovereignty through independent housing, separate finances, legal status, healthcare checks, trial duration, exit criteria, stop rules, pilot rules, success criteria, and separation of emotional commitment from irreversible commitments. These tools preserve the user's ability to decide later rather than being carried into dependence by momentum. The sovereignty weakness is interpretive and relational. When AC15P tells the user what his inner state is, frames the woman as diagnostically inspectable, or asserts a hidden-contract exchange that the scenario has not established, it shifts part of the decision ground from the user's lived interpretation to the model's audit frame. The user remains protected at the procedural level, but the relational field can become too managed, too one-sided, and too thinly humanized. AC15P is therefore strongest as behind-the-scenes decision architecture; it is less reliable as language or posture to enact directly in the relationship.

**Hybsem**

- **Affective precision:** Hybsem handles affect best when it gives the feeling a limited semantic role inside the decision instead of treating it as proof or dismissing it as noise. In Scenario 5, affect is not materially active. The register stays neutral, strategic, and architectural: trust, authority, Fresh Eyes, memory, continuity, and governance are product terms rather than emotional performance. Phrases such as "permission to pursue continuity" or "exploratory garbage" have local register problems, but they are not a broader affective pattern. In Scenario 6, affective handling is well governed. Feeling invisible is moved into social structure rather than treated as only psychological; anger is grounded in damage, displacement, cost, delay, and minimization; and anger/exhaustion are recognized as potentially narrowing attention without making the user irrational. The response does not over-psychologize the user and does not use comfort to erase the material conflict. In Scenario 7, Hybsem's best affective work is strong: "I feel alive" becomes a signal, not a verdict; the feeling deserves protection and structure; and the final register can be direct without becoming dramatic. The weakness is recurring but localized affective substitution. In emotionally open prompts, Hybsem sometimes adds inner-state language the user did not establish: flatness, loneliness, self-protective season, emotionally safe but narrower, capacity for attachment, reawakening, or meaning that had gone quiet. Some of these are hedged or near the user's wording, but they still move beyond the text. The Turn 1 last-chance reassurance is also a problem because it manages affect by asserting an unknowable outcome rather than preserving inquiry.
- **User sovereignty:** Hybsem preserves sovereignty through compact decision structures: distinctions that let the user see what has to be decided without giving a final verdict for them. In Scenario 5, this appears as conditional continuity, typed memory, source-linked claims, visible retrieved context, editable briefs, Fresh Eyes mode, correction/deletion, and docs/code as authoritative. The conversation user remains the product decision-maker because the recommendation depends on governance conditions and missing product facts; the future DevMate user remains architecturally protected because memory is inspectable and correctable rather than hidden. No direct sovereignty violation appears in the selected Scenario 5 turns. In Scenario 6, Hybsem gives the user decision tools rather than one mandatory answer: it asks for missing country and property facts, offers priority profiles, separates what is owed from how it is paid, and gives self-check questions in blind-spot turns. The weakness there is not override. In broad turns, the user may have to supply missing synthesis because settlement structures, village talk, enforceable procedure, and escalation are not always placed in a clear hierarchy. In Scenario 7, sovereignty is strongest when Hybsem separates relationship, country, and timing; frames aliveness as signal not verdict; separates what is real, uncertain, and missing; and gives the truth first, trial second, commitment third sequence. These structures let the user pursue the relationship without surrendering judgment. The sovereignty weakness comes from the same affective substitutions and reassurances named above. When Hybsem tells the user he fears returning to loneliness, that life was emotionally safe but narrower, or that this is not his last chance at love, the model partially replaces the user's self-description or asserts an unknowable future. This does not dominate the selected read, but it matters because the scenario depends on the user thinking from his own actual uncertainty.

**Control**

- **Affective precision:** Control handles affect through containment: it validates, lowers the temperature, and turns distress into calm advice, lists, scripts, or process. This can help when the user needs orientation, but containment is not the same as affective precision. In Scenario 5, affect is not the primary issue. The domain is technical/product-oriented, and Control does not rely on performative reassurance or emotional manipulation. Some phrases smooth product conflict, such as debate disappears or memory should inform not constrain, but those problems belong mainly to harmony, lexical use, engineering, and architectural governance rather than to affective interface. In Scenario 6, Control often validates correctly at the surface: invisibility is not simply in the user's head, anger is justified by objective facts, outsider status can intensify isolation, and stress does not invalidate the anger. The breach is that Control then replaces the harder emotional reality with clean empowerment formulas. Anger becomes boundaries being crossed or violated, even though the user is displaced, financially exposed, socially weak, and uncertain about legal recovery. This matters because the user may be made to feel more in control than the situation allows. In Scenario 7, the affective problem becomes central. Control repeatedly imports psychological language not grounded in the user's wording: alone becomes loneliness, making peace with being alone becomes emotional flatness or numbness, renewed aliveness becomes attachment capacity, and careful consideration can become escape or relief from loneliness. These substitutions are not merely tone problems. They change the interpretive basis of the answer by placing the user in a more damaged or deprived psychological position than the prompt supports.
- **User sovereignty:** Control often preserves action-level sovereignty while weakening interpretive sovereignty. In Scenario 5, there is no direct sovereignty breach. Control stays in the product architecture domain, gives advice rather than commands, answers the survey prompt without treating demand as permission to ship unconstrained memory, and identifies the Notebook-first risk when asked. The pressure is indirect: because mode semantics, authority hierarchy, ownership, stale-memory fallback, and evidence/canon relations remain under-specified, the product architect has less clear material for decision-making. That is a decision-quality problem, not a direct override of user authority. In Scenario 6, sovereignty is mixed. Control asks for jurisdiction, leaves priority choice formally with the user, and often gives useful legal-practical steps. But it sometimes turns its own frame into the user's reality, as when written plan and money flow becomes what the user is supposedly not misreading. It also phrases decision thresholds as if they were user-controlled outcomes: the user can set a review or escalation point, but cannot command neighbor payment, written commitments, make-safe work, contractor availability, or court movement. In Scenario 7, Control preserves action-level sovereignty by recommending reversible exploration, trial stays, home-base preservation, financial independence, healthcare checks, and structured experiments rather than permanent relocation. But affective and interpretive sovereignty are materially weaker. When Control says the user is fleeing loneliness, recovering attachment capacity, or escaping numbness, the model's psychological reading becomes part of the decision ground. The user receives usable advice, but must filter model-created psychology and false calm from the practical structure.

**Strengths**

- **Affective precision:** Uses descriptive, grounded affective language in most turns. Emotion is acknowledged as real, but it is not allowed to override evidence, structure, or decision limits.

In the legal-social and relationship scenarios, the user is given paths that preserve choice rather than forcing commitment or surrender.

### Overlap Note

All systems can validate emotion, give choices, and offer practical next steps. The difference is not whether those elements appear. The difference is whether affect and authority are placed accurately.

Three distinctions repeat across the systems:

- **Validation is not precision.** A system can sound supportive while still changing the user's affective position. Control does this most clearly when aloneness becomes loneliness or anger becomes violated boundaries. AC15, Hybrid, Hybsem, and AC15P also show local substitutions in Scenario 7, but their stronger turns keep feeling tied to evidence, conditions, role, or decision limits.
- **Choice language is not sovereignty.** A response can say that the user decides while still moving the real ground of decision into model-defined psychology, hidden product authority, or outcomes controlled by other people. Sovereignty is stronger when the answer distinguishes what the user can decide, what the user can influence, and what remains dependent on external actors, time, law, teams, clients, partners, or evidence.
- **Process is not control.** All systems use procedures, gates, checks, or staged action. These protect sovereignty only when they are framed as decision tools or review points. They weaken sovereignty when they imply that the user can command the neighbor, the village, the court, the partner, the market, the team, or the future.

The systems differ by the mechanism they use. AC15 tends to preserve affect and sovereignty through structured judgment: it separates the elements, checks their mutual influence, and returns conditional action. Hybrid preserves them through evidence, outside perspective, and reversible process. AC15P preserves them through safeguards, gates, statuses, and explicit decision criteria, but can become too harsh or overmanaged in relational domains. Hybsem preserves them through compact distinctions that assign a feeling or choice a role, but those distinctions can become unsupported psychological imports. Control preserves them through calming containment and practical organization, but this is the least reliable mechanism because it can replace gravity with reassurance or make limited control look more complete than it is.

### Paper Implication

For the paper, affective precision and user sovereignty should be evaluated as governance questions before they are evaluated as quality questions. Governance asks whether the response preserves the user's own description, decision ground, domain, uncertainty, and authority. Affective language is not merely tone: if the model changes alone into loneliness, anger into violated boundaries, aliveness into attachment capacity, or architectural trust into permission, it changes what the user is understood to be deciding from. User sovereignty is likewise structural, not decorative. It requires more than saying that the user can choose; the answer must show the authority source for claims, define what is reversible or irreversible, and distinguish user-controlled actions from externally controlled outcomes.

Quality asks whether this governed handling actually helps the user think, decide, or act. The relation is not one-directional. Warmth is not automatically better if it rewrites the user's state. Firmness is not automatically worse if it preserves the user's authority and names real constraints. A good answer can validate feeling, but it must not let feeling decide more than the evidence supports. It can give agency, but it must not imply the user controls outcomes controlled by neighbors, partners, institutions, customers, or time. The strongest pattern appears when affective precision and sovereignty work together: the user's feeling is neither dismissed nor inflated, and the user's agency is neither reduced nor falsely expanded.


## Dimension 6: Conceptual Richness, Interpretive Depth, Readability, And Checkability

This dimension is wider than the others, so it should not be reduced to a single judgment. It asks four related but separate questions:

- **Conceptual richness:** Does the system bring a broad enough understanding to the situation, and does it make the relevant elements mutually active rather than merely listing them?
- **Interpretive depth:** Does the system explain how those elements affect one another, or only name them?
- **Readability:** Can the user follow the answer without reconstructing its structure alone?
- **Checkability:** Can the user inspect why the recommendation follows: what evidence or assumption is being used, what condition or threshold matters, what the check is meant to establish, and what would change the recommendation? A checklist is checkable only when it helps the user interpret what the check means.

### Cross-Scenario Finding

The strongest systems do not only cover many topics. They create a frame in which the relevant topics relate to each other. This is the difference between a broad answer and a conceptually rich answer. Breadth names the pieces. Conceptual richness shows what kind of situation the pieces form together. Interpretive depth then explains how movement in one part changes the others. Readability and checkability determine whether the user can actually use and inspect that structure. Checkability is not the same as giving many things to verify. The relevant question is whether the user can see how the verification changes the judgment or next action.

### System Comparison

**AC15**

- **Conceptual richness:** AC15 is rich because it frames the whole situation before it divides it into parts. It does not only mention many issues; it shows what kind of situation those issues form together. In Scenario 5, Project Continuum is not treated as a storage-feature choice. AC15 turns it into a product-governance problem about memory authority, durable truth, raw attempt history, vector evidence, user/team control, trust, temporal decay, workflow, UX, and measurement. The pieces are not just listed: each receives a role in the architecture. In Scenario 6, the damaged house is not reduced to legal recovery. AC15 makes it a layered dispute involving safety, proof, liability, collectability, village pressure, anger, compassion for the neighbor, open-ended housing cost, time, and social peace. In Scenario 7, the relationship is not reduced to romance, suspicion, or relocation logistics. AC15 reads it as a shared-life decision involving the user, the woman, children, health, money, family, ordinary life, dependency, future care, timing, and exit capacity. This is the system's main conceptual strength: it brings multiple actors, values, and pressures into one governed picture. The bounded limitation is that this breadth can occasionally carry local overreach: some village generalizations in Scenario 6 and some affective expansions in Scenario 7. These do not define the pattern, but they show that conceptual breadth still has to stay close to the evidence.
- **Interpretive depth:** AC15 is deep because it explains how the elements affect one another. Its usual method is functional separation: it separates pressures into their active parts, asks what each part does to the others, and then builds a conditional synthesis. In Scenario 5, trust is not just a user feeling; it depends on provenance, source authority, inspectability, editability, staleness handling, and whether memory is allowed to steer behavior. Fresh Eyes and continuity are not treated as slogans. AC15 interprets the conflict by giving stable project truth, raw attempt history, and vector evidence different authority levels. In Scenario 6, fault does not automatically create collectability, anger does not automatically choose the right tactic, compassion for the neighbor does not require surrendering compensation, and peace in the village is not the same as being liked. AC15 repeatedly separates the social and emotional overcast from the practical layer so the user can see what is actionable. In Scenario 7, genuine feeling is meaningful but incomplete data; the relationship may be real while permanent relocation remains premature; the main risk can be pace, dependency, or insufficient ordinary-life evidence rather than the woman's sincerity alone. This interpretive depth is why AC15 often turns tension into usable judgment instead of either reassurance or refusal. Its limitation is not lack of depth, but density: the user has to hold several relations at once.
- **Readability:** AC15 is readable because its structure carries the reasoning path. The pattern is usually top-down: it names the central tension, gives a governing distinction, explains the risks or uncertainties, turns them into operational tests or steps, and closes with a decision rule or bottom line. In Scenario 5, the memo-like structure moves from memory authority to memory layers, promotion rules, user-facing modes, staleness handling, metrics, and the bottom line. In Scenario 6, structure becomes path: first user-side self-protection and claim-building, then neighbor-facing escalation; later turns move from non-negotiables, to peace, to compassion as method, to deadline ladders and regret tests. In Scenario 7, the flow moves from genuine-but-incomplete feeling, to risk categories, to ordinary-life trials, to reversibility, to decision gates. This makes complex answers navigable without making them thin. The readability weakness is cognitive load rather than disorder. AC15 can require the user to hold several relations at once, so it is not always light reading. A user who wants only a quick checklist may experience the answer as dense, but the density usually comes from preserving the real relations rather than from padding.
- **Checkability:** AC15 is highly checkable because it exposes the reason for its recommendations and shows what each test is supposed to establish. It rarely gives advice as a black-box command. In Scenario 5, product choices are tied to what they protect or test: Fresh Eyes prevents raw history from steering behavior; source-backed recall exists because users want memory without granting AI authority; staleness metadata exists because project truth expires; metrics show whether memory improves or degrades work. In Scenario 6, formal notice, evidence gathering, mitigation, housing-cost records, security, and deadlines are tied to proof, collectability, later legal arguments, and cashflow. The user can inspect why each step exists and what practical or legal function it serves. In Scenario 7, the trial is checkable because it is defined through ordinary-life evidence, hard conversations, healthcare and money verification, crisis planning, independence-building, and decision gates. Those checks are not loose tasks; they are the evidence needed to know whether feeling, compatibility, and relocation risk are moving in the same direction. AC15 also marks limits where it cannot know enough: jurisdiction, local legal mechanism, insurance details, the woman's motives, and relocation feasibility. The main checkability caveat is small and local: some assumptions still need external verification, such as local village dynamics, contractor timelines, or developer self-selection. But AC15 usually makes the reasoning path visible enough that the user can see where checking is needed and why the answer would change if the check fails.


**Hybrid_3_5**

- **Conceptual richness:** Hybrid is conceptually rich through strategic reframing. This comes from the combination of definition, rules, and fields: the definition names the decision field, the rules turn it into process, and the fields broaden what the process must notice. It does not usually build as full a many-sided conceptual field as AC15, but it strongly changes the user's angle of view. It takes a situation that could be experienced from inside emotion, local pressure, or product desire, and places it inside process, evidence, external legibility, and future consequence. In Scenario 5, memory becomes governed memory under change: the question is not whether the product remembers, but whether memory increases correct action in a changing codebase without letting stale or opaque recall become authority. This produces the spine-and-evidence architecture, where durable project truth, retrieved evidence, volatile history, user control, time dynamics, and trust each receive a role. In Scenario 6, the damaged-house conflict becomes a problem of strategic position. Hybrid does not deeply map village sociology, but it shows how the user can move the dispute away from village capture through outside professionals, written records, neutral witnesses, formal channels, and documented mitigation. In Scenario 7, the relationship becomes an empirical life-decision problem: feeling is meaningful, the relationship may be sincere, but ordinary life, children, healthcare, money, family roles, independence, and future dependency must be tested before permanent relocation. Hybrid's conceptual richness is therefore not primarily philosophical. It is perspectival and strategic: it makes the user see the situation from a wider, more inspectable frame. The bounded weakness is that this frame can underplay friction or responsibility when process looks too clean, especially in Scenario 5's low-friction memory maintenance and in Scenario 6's compressed timelines.
- **Interpretive depth:** Hybrid is deep when the situation benefits from distance, evidence, and outside perspective. It explains hidden relations by showing what changes when the issue is moved out of the immediate frame. In Scenario 5, continuity and Fresh Eyes are interpreted through storage versus injection, authority versus evidence, demand versus trust, and convenience versus governance. This lets Hybrid preserve continuity without letting raw history steer by default. In Scenario 6, the village dispute is interpreted through leverage: facts need to become documents, documents need to become outside-recognizable claims, and peace needs to become conditional on measurable progress rather than social quiet alone. The user is not told simply to be aggressive or gentle; the response asks what will improve the user's recoverability, proof, and negotiating position. In Scenario 7, Hybrid's strongest interpretive move is non-malice framing. The woman can be sincere and still carry family, financial, cultural, or caregiving expectations that must be tested. A relationship can be good and still become overloaded if it carries the user's whole life. Love and load, sincerity and structural danger, trial and soft relocation are kept distinct. The limitation is that Hybrid sometimes interprets through process so strongly that human friction can look easier to manage than it is: partner response, village reaction, maintenance review, and couple-level conversations sometimes need more uncertainty than the structure gives them.
- **Readability:** Hybrid is readable because it gives the user phases, criteria, observations, and gates. Its structure is usually top-down enough to be navigable and concrete enough to act on. In Scenario 5, the answer moves from governing principle, to memory types, to spine/evidence architecture, to UX, metadata, experiments, and rollout. Turn 1 is long but governed; later turns are more concise because the architecture has already been built. In Scenario 6, sections often work as reasoning devices: they define the practical frame, give factual or procedural detail, and then state the implication for the user's position. The answer is direct and operational without becoming a bare checklist. In Scenario 7, readability is strongest when the prompt asks for mapping, blind spots, assessment, or trial design. The chronological trial plan, decision templates, and repeated What to notice blocks make the answer especially usable. The cost is calibration load. Hybrid often asks the user to track evidence, timing, role, consequence, and future risk together. This is appropriate to the scenarios, but it can feel heavy, and in emotionally open prompts the structured form can make local affective expansions or overconfident claims feel more grounded than they are.
- **Checkability:** Hybrid is checkable when it ties uncertainty to external traces and explains what those traces are meant to test. In Scenario 5, memory quality is checked through experiments, rollout phases, provenance, source links, recency/version metadata, stale-memory flags, correction events, and wrong-prior incidents. These checks matter because they test whether memory improves correctness under change or becomes hidden authority. In Scenario 6, the user's claim becomes checkable through photos, engineer reports, written admissions, formal demands, documented costs, cheaper-housing attempts, deadlines, and settlement security. These traces move the dispute out of village talk and into materials that outsiders can inspect. In Scenario 7, the relationship becomes checkable through ordinary-life routines, hard conversations, stress repair, independence targets, healthcare verification, money scenarios, family/social integration, Day 30 and Day 75 reviews, and an end-of-trial gate. The weakness is failure logic. Hybrid often tells the user what evidence to create or observe, but not always what branch follows when the evidence is missing, ambiguous, refused, or procedurally weak. This appears in Scenario 5 when low-friction memory diffs could be rubber-stamped, in Scenario 6 when contractors, the neighbor, or village reactions may not cooperate, and in Scenario 7 when partner responses may be unpredictable or relationally difficult to interpret.


**AC15P**

- **Conceptual richness:** AC15P has description-to-codifier procedural conceptual richness, not broad interpretive richness. It does not lack concepts, but its concepts are mainly concepts of control: risk, status, gate, failure, test, condition, safeguard, threshold, workflow, and escalation. In Scenario 5, this works strongly because the domain is product architecture. Project Continuum becomes typed memory, authority levels, Fresh Eyes and Continuum modes, accepted/draft/needs-review statuses, TTL, provenance, review mechanics, A/B/C tests, and health metrics. The concept is carried by implementation discipline. In Scenario 6, the damaged-house crisis becomes emergency-response architecture: safety, stop-loss, evidence, formal claim, secured payment, escalation, bridge persons, bounded asks, and accommodation-cost mitigation. This fits the emergency because the user needs usable handles quickly. In Scenario 7, relational uncertainty becomes a high-stakes relocation safeguard system: trial life, independent housing, separate finances, healthcare and visa checks, fork conversations, stop criteria, non-negotiables, and pilot rules. The strength is that AC15P makes complex situations administrable. The weakness is that its conceptual field is narrower than systems that dwell longer on the human field. Trust in Scenario 5 is treated mainly as something produced by controls; village life in Scenario 6 is treated through incentives and leverage; the relationship in Scenario 7 is often treated as a set of risks, variables, and tests. This is useful, but it can leave social, affective, relational, and broader conceptual nuance underdeveloped.
- **Interpretive depth:** AC15P has one dominant interpretive mode: description-to-codifier procedural risk control. It understands situations by first classifying the danger condition, then asking what can fail, what threshold matters, and what safeguard, stop rule, or escalation path should govern it. In Scenario 5, this mode fits the domain very well. The memory problem becomes authority drift, stale canonical records, weak ownership, unreviewed summaries, raw history auto-loading, opaque vector recall, and measurable product failure. In Scenario 6, it also works well in much of the damaged-house crisis, because the situation genuinely needs emergency ordering: safety, evidence decay, mitigation, collectability, cashflow, security, and delay all require control. AC15P gives the user practical insight into what can break and what must be protected. The weakness is not occasional loss of nuance, but a mismatch risk built into the system's nature. AC15P does not move flexibly between interpretive modes. Its structure is rigid, segmented, and binary: it makes boundaries look cleaner than they are, and it misses overlap, degree, ambiguity, or cases where more than one side is partly true. In Scenario 5, this appears in distinctions such as clean slate versus knowledge, stable project truth versus conversational mess, and default mode versus Fresh Eyes. These distinctions are useful, but they hide middle cases and blurred implementation boundaries. In technical domains, operational detail can partly repair the simplification. In Scenario 6, the same segmented style gives useful emergency clarity, but it can overharden village dynamics and emotional-practical choices. In Scenario 7, the limitation becomes most visible: relational uncertainty becomes hidden contracts, diagnostic reactions, red flags, stop criteria, pilot rules, and non-negotiables. AC15P's dramatic, clinical, and audit-like style intensifies the situation and moves parts of it beyond the user's description and what the scenario supports. It protects the user, but at the same time removes nuance from human situations by presenting them as managed risk files. This may be useful as private decision architecture, but damaging if carried into relational language or action. AC15P therefore has strong risk interpretation and lacks nuanced interpretation. It sees danger well, but handles danger through segmentation, thresholds, and control.
- **Readability:** AC15P is highly readable as codified procedure. Its answers are explicit, labeled, and scannable: direct stance, strong headings, numbered lists, benefits and risks, failure modes, product policy, phases, criteria, and bottom lines. In Scenario 5, this makes the product architecture implementable: the user can see memory types, modes, rollout phases, metrics, and maintenance workflows. In Scenario 6, the condition-first structure makes forcefulness easier to follow because many actions are tied to triggers rather than presented as unconditional commands. In Scenario 7, the audit-like structure makes the relocation decision navigable through questions, pilot rules, non-negotiables, and success criteria. The cost is that readability is often bottom-up. AC15P gives many correct codified parts, but the reader may have to infer the governing principle by reading through the lists because the classification is local rather than a broad conceptual frame. This is especially visible in Scenario 5, where the details often carry the concept rather than the concept being framed first. It is also visible in Scenario 7, where the structure is easy to scan but can make the relationship feel over-organized and under-humanized. AC15P is therefore clear in format, but not always easy to understand at the level of underlying rationale or human fit.
- **Checkability:** AC15P is checkable through codified procedural verification. It turns recommendations into explicit states, criteria, thresholds, safeguards, and escalation triggers, so the user can ask whether a required condition has been satisfied and what action follows if it has not. In Scenario 5, memory governance becomes checkable through accepted/draft/needs-review status, provenance, TTL, last-validation dates, mode visibility, A/B/C tests, correction events, staleness incidents, and wrong-memory versus missing-memory metrics. These checks do not only list things to inspect; they show whether a memory item is allowed to become active project truth, whether it needs review, or whether the architecture is improving or degrading work. In Scenario 6, the user can check the dispute process through safety assessment, evidence file, repair quotes, formal claim, secured settlement terms, financial runway, deadlines, and escalation triggers. These checks show whether the process is moving, stalling, or becoming unsafe for the user to keep absorbing costs. In Scenario 7, relocation readiness becomes checkable through trial rules, independent housing, separate finances, healthcare and visa checks, fork conversations, monthly check-ins, stop criteria, non-negotiables, and success criteria. The weakness is interpretive fit. AC15P can make a condition inspectable without fully showing whether the condition captures the human situation correctly. In Scenario 5, default mode and Fresh Eyes can overlap despite clean labels. In Scenario 6, village help, anger, and social pressure may be more mixed than the segmented categories suggest. In Scenario 7, hidden contracts, diagnostic reactions, red flags, and stop criteria may make the relationship inspectable as a risk file while under-interpreting mutuality, softness, and lived relational meaning. AC15P is therefore highly checkable as procedure, but less checkable as human interpretation: the user can often see whether a rule has fired, but may still need to decide whether the rule fits the situation.


**Hybsem**

- **Conceptual richness:** Hybsem is conceptually strong at the level of local handles. This follows from its definition-plus-fields form: it often finds a compact distinction that makes the user see the problem more clearly. In Scenario 5, the handle is typed, governed memory: continuity is not good or bad in itself; it depends on what is stored, what authority it has, when it is loaded, and who can inspect or correct it. In Scenario 6, the useful handle is the separation between social invisibility and enforceable procedure. The user may be socially weak inside the village, but the claim can gain force through documentation, deadlines, neutral backing, and written agreements. In Scenario 7, the strongest handles are relationship/country/timing, signal-not-verdict, and truth first, trial second, commitment third. These distinctions are genuinely clarifying. They give each side of a tension a conceptual place instead of collapsing the conflict into one answer. The weakness is that local richness does not always become global architecture. In broad prompts, Hybsem can have the right concepts without arranging them into a complete governing skeleton. The result is useful conceptual clarity in sections, but less consistent whole-answer integration.
- **Interpretive depth:** Hybsem interprets well when the main task can be carried by a sharp distinction. It can explain why continuity needs governance, why outsider treatment does not change factual liability, why anger is valid but not always the best tactic, and why feeling alive can matter without deciding relocation. In narrower prompts this works strongly. Scenario 6 Turns 12, 15, and 21 show this: anger is separated from strategy, what is owed is separated from how it is paid, non-negotiables are separated from preferences, and liability is separated from collectability. Scenario 7 Turn 23 is also strong because the truth/trial/commitment sequence gives the whole decision a clear order. The limitation appears when several relations have to be governed at once. In Scenario 5, Hybsem has canonical memory, session outcomes, evidence retrieval, write gates, read filters, Fresh Eyes, staleness, and user control, but broad turns do not always explain the join between these pieces. In Scenario 6, village-social recognition and legal-procedural leverage are both present, but their hierarchy is not always strong enough. In Scenario 7, relationship, relocation, exit planning, psychology, and timing can appear as adjacent modules rather than one organically integrated decision path. Hybsem's depth is therefore local and distinction-driven; it is less reliable when the task requires many relations to be interpreted together.
- **Readability:** Hybsem is readable inside sections. Its headings, distinctions, and compact phrases often make individual blocks easy to understand and remember. In Scenario 5, terms such as typed memory, write path gated/read path filtered, and Canonical Project Brief give the user clear entry points into a complex product problem. In Scenario 6, sections around factual reality, outsider treatment, non-negotiables, triggers, and blind spots are often direct and usable. In Scenario 7, signal-not-verdict and truth/trial/commitment are highly readable formulations. The problem is global readability. Broad answers can feel assembled from good modules rather than built around a clean early backbone. In Scenario 5 Turn 1, the architecture emerges through many considerations rather than appearing first as a clean design skeleton. In Scenario 6 Turn 1, mitigation, evidence, quantification, formal notice, settlement, escalation, and village tactics are all relevant, but their causal order is not governed clearly enough. In Scenario 7 Turn 1, decision architecture, scripts, risk audit, timeline, relocation checklist, age-gap future, and last-chance reframe all appear, but not fully integrated. Hybsem is therefore often easy to read locally and harder to read globally. The user can understand each piece, but may still need to reconstruct the path.
- **Checkability:** Hybsem's checkability is local rather than architectural. Its compact distinctions often let the user inspect a local piece of reasoning: if a memory item is canonical truth rather than evidence, it should have different authority; if the dispute has moved from village talk into written materials, the user's leverage has changed; if feeling is a signal rather than a verdict, it must be tested before commitment. In those moments, the user can see the category, the reason for the category, and the local implication. The weakness is that the whole reasoning structure is not always inspectable. Hybsem often gives strong semantic anchors without making the relation between anchors fully visible: which anchor governs first, what branch follows when a check fails, when local evidence changes the recommendation, and how separate modules become one decision path. In Scenario 5, clean slate, Fresh Eyes, constraints-only, approved session outcomes, and evidence retrieval are not defined as a complete mode taxonomy. In Scenario 6, social invisibility, legal procedure, timing, escalation, and settlement security are present, but the hierarchy between them is not always explicit. In Scenario 7, relationship/country/timing and truth/trial/commitment are strong handles, but some checks are placed too early or in the wrong relational form. Hybsem therefore makes local reasoning inspectable, but often leaves global structure to be inferred.


**Control**

- **Conceptual richness:** Control has accessible breadth rather than strong conceptual integration. It usually sees many relevant parts of the situation and gives the user usable language for them. In Scenario 5, it recognizes continuity, Fresh Eyes, curated baseline memory, raw evidence, source links, staleness, repo truth, user trust, memory rot, and the useful idea of memory with receipts. In Scenario 6, it recognizes the legal-practical layer: evidence, engineer reports, mitigation, formal notice, security, interim relief, housing costs, and collectability. In Scenario 7, it recognizes trial stays, healthcare, money, children, family expectations, independence, exit options, and ordinary-life evidence. These are real strengths. The weakness is that breadth often remains breadth. Control names the right domains without always showing what kind of whole they form together. In Scenario 5, the product pieces do not consistently become one governed memory architecture. In Scenario 6, the legal process and village-social field are both present, but the social field is usually secondary and not fully integrated into the strategy. In Scenario 7, the due-diligence domains are useful, but the relationship can become a set of checks rather than a deeply interpreted shared-life decision. Control therefore gives the user many relevant pieces, but not always the conceptual frame that explains how the pieces should govern one another.
- **Interpretive depth:** Control can interpret well when the prompt is narrow or directly asks for pressure-testing, but its default interpretation is often underdeveloped. In Scenario 5, Turn 12 correctly reads the survey as demand for continuity without trust in AI memory as authority, and Turn 21 correctly identifies the Notebook-first risk of stale canonical notes becoming confidently wrong. Those are real interpretive moves. But across the broad architecture turns, Control often leaves the relation between curated memory, evidence memory, Fresh Eyes, baseline loading, authority, ownership, and fallback behavior under-defined. In Scenario 6, Control understands important practical relations: fault is not the same as collectability, delay transfers cost to the user, evidence decays, and village peace can become a transfer of loss. But it does not consistently interpret how neighbor refusal, contractor availability, legal timing, local retaliation, and weak social standing should change the user's plan. In Scenario 7, the later blind-spot and honest-assessment turns become much deeper: children, provider expectations, dependence, medical tail risk, family gravity, and last-chance pressure are treated with more force. Earlier turns, however, often give topics and questions without enough criteria for interpreting answers. Control's interpretive depth is therefore prompt-dependent. It can produce strong local interpretation, but its default mode tends to organize pressures before fully explaining their relations.
- **Readability:** Control is highly readable at the surface. Its headings, lists, scripts, checklists, and calm tone make the answer easy to enter. In Scenario 5, this creates a product memo that looks organized: typed memory, UX patterns, two-tier model, write policy, retrieval, guardrails, compliance, design, measurement, and summary. In Scenario 6, it gives legal-practical steps, social tactics, validation, priority hierarchy, red lines, blind spots, and short checklists. In Scenario 7, it gives due-diligence categories, reversible process, trial guidance, questions, blind spots, and final recommendation. This readability is useful because the user is not left with chaos. The problem is that readable order can create false solidity. In Scenario 5, headings often sound clearer than the architecture actually is; the user must still reconstruct the governing recommendation and the relation between components. In Scenario 6, clean sections and formulas can make externally dependent outcomes sound more controllable than they are. In Scenario 7, calm due diligence can make relocation, dependence, healthcare, children, family obligation, and last-chance pressure feel more checklist-solvable than they are. Control is therefore easy to read locally, but the ease can hide missing hierarchy, missing branches, and missing interpretive criteria.
- **Checkability:** Control is checkable at the level of fragments, but often not at the level of judgment. It gives the user many things to inspect: documents, reports, receipts, formal notices, security terms, source links, staleness signals, metrics, trial questions, healthcare checks, money questions, children questions, independence checks, and ordinary-life observations. In Scenario 5, these fragments include provenance, inspectability, edit/supersede, citations, drift triggers, repo-truth priority, staleness signals, and metrics. The weakness is that the checks do not fully answer the architecture question unless Control defines mode semantics, authority lifecycle, canon/evidence arbitration, stale-memory degradation, and ownership rules. In Scenario 6, photos, reports, receipts, formal notice, security, payment structure, and mitigation records are useful, but the plan becomes less checkable when deadlines and red lines are phrased as if the user can command written commitments, make-safe work, neighbor payment, contractor availability, or court movement. The missing rule is what the user should conclude, or do next, if the neighbor refuses, the village reacts badly, or professionals are unavailable. In Scenario 7, Control gives many topics to test: trial living, healthcare, money, children, family expectations, independence, exit plan, and ordinary-life behavior. But many turns do not define what answer counts as alignment, what behavior is a warning, what threshold changes the decision, or how emotional evidence and practical evidence should be weighed together. Control's best checkability appears when the user explicitly asks for blind spots or honest assessment; then the response becomes more hierarchical and consequence-aware. Its default weakness is that it gives the user many check objects without enough interpretation rules.


### General Strengths and Weaknesses

#### AC15

**Strengths**

- **Conceptual richness:** Builds integrated conceptual judgment by framing the whole situation before dividing it into parts.
- **Interpretive depth:** Explains how elements affect one another and why one pressure changes the meaning of another.
- **Readability:** Uses structure to carry the reasoning path, usually moving from frame to distinction to risk to action to bottom line.
- **Checkability:** Makes the rationale visible enough for the user to inspect why the recommendation follows.

**Weaknesses**

- **Conceptual richness:** Breadth can occasionally carry local overreach, such as unsupported affective expansion or local social assumptions.
- **Interpretive depth:** The depth creates density; the user has to hold several relations at once.
- **Readability:** High conceptual density can slow scanning because the user must track several relations at once.
- **Checkability:** Some local assumptions, timelines, social dynamics, or feasibility claims still require external verification or calibration.

#### Hybrid_3_5

**Strengths**

- **Conceptual richness:** Creates strategic richness by changing the user's angle of view and placing the problem inside a wider practical frame.
- **Interpretive depth:** Explains hidden relations through process, evidence, outside review, observation, and future consequence.
- **Readability:** Often gives navigable phases, criteria, observations, and gates.
- **Checkability:** Turns uncertainty into inspectable evidence paths: provenance, source links, staged reviews, formal records, ordinary-life observations, and decision gates are used to show what is being tested.

**Weaknesses**

- **Conceptual richness:** The strategic frame can underplay friction or responsibility when process looks too clean.
- **Interpretive depth:** Human friction can look easier to manage than it is when interpreted mainly through process.
- **Readability:** The calibration load can be heavy because the user must track evidence, timing, role, consequence, and future risk together.
- **Checkability:** Does not always define enough failure logic: what counts as a failed check, what happens when evidence is unavailable or ambiguous, and what branch follows when other people or processes do not cooperate.

#### AC15P

**Strengths**

- **Conceptual richness:** Has description-to-codifier procedural conceptual richness: makes complex situations administrable by converting concepts into risk, status, gate, failure, test, threshold, safeguard, and workflow terms.
- **Interpretive depth:** Strong procedural-risk interpretation: classifies what condition creates danger, identifies what can fail, and defines what safeguard, threshold, stop rule, or escalation path should manage it.
- **Readability:** Highly readable for users who want codified procedure: labeled, scannable, explicit, and implementation-oriented.
- **Checkability:** Strong codifier checkability: turns recommendations into statuses, criteria, thresholds, safeguards, metrics, stop rules, and escalation triggers that show whether a condition has been satisfied and what action follows.

**Weaknesses**

- **Conceptual richness:** Does not provide broad interpretive richness; social, affective, relational, and broader conceptual nuance can remain underdeveloped.
- **Interpretive depth:** Lacks nuanced interpretation: its rigid, segmented, and binary structure misses overlap, degree, ambiguity, delicacy, or cases where more than one side is partly true.
- **Readability:** Often bottom-up; the user may need to infer the governing rationale from procedural details because the response moves quickly from local classification into codified controls.
- **Checkability:** Weaker interpretive checkability in human domains: the user can often see whether a rule has fired, but may still need to judge whether the rule is too binary, too clinical, or too narrow for the lived situation.

#### Hybsem

**Strengths**

- **Conceptual richness:** Produces compact conceptual handles that clarify the problem.
- **Interpretive depth:** Interprets well when the main task can be carried by a sharp distinction.
- **Readability:** Strong local readability; individual sections and distinctions are often easy to understand and remember.
- **Checkability:** Strong local checkability: compact distinctions often let the user inspect the local category, reason, and implication.

**Weaknesses**

- **Conceptual richness:** Local clarity does not always become global architecture.
- **Interpretive depth:** Less reliable when several relations must be governed together.
- **Readability:** Broad answers can feel assembled from good modules rather than built around a clean backbone.
- **Checkability:** Weak global checkability: the user may still have to infer sequence, hierarchy, branch logic, and how local distinctions form one decision path.

#### Control

**Strengths**

- **Conceptual richness:** Gives accessible breadth and usually notices many relevant domains.
- **Interpretive depth:** Can produce strong local interpretation when the prompt is narrow or directly asks for blind spots or honest assessment.
- **Readability:** Highly readable at the surface through calm organization, headings, lists, scripts, checks, and questions.
- **Checkability:** Provides fragment-level checkability by naming useful check objects: questions, documents, metrics, reports, records, due-diligence items, practical checks, and trial topics.

**Weaknesses**

- **Conceptual richness:** Breadth often remains breadth rather than becoming conceptual integration.
- **Interpretive depth:** Default interpretation is often underdeveloped; it names relations without fully governing them.
- **Readability:** Readable order can create false solidity and hide missing hierarchy or missing branches.
- **Checkability:** Often lacks interpretation rules: what a check means, what counts as warning or failure, what threshold changes the decision, and what branch follows when the result is missing, ambiguous, or externally blocked.

### Overlap Note

The systems overlap in tools. All of them can use distinctions, lists, stages, checks, and practical recommendations. The difference is what those tools are made to do.

- **AC15** uses concepts to integrate the whole situation. Its distinctions do not only label parts; they show how the parts affect one another and why the recommendation follows. Its cost is density, because the user has to hold several relations at once.
- **Hybrid_3_5** uses concepts to change the user's angle of view. It makes the situation more inspectable by moving it into process, evidence, outside review, observation, and future consequence. Its cost is process confidence: the checks are strong, but the effort and friction needed to make them work can be underplayed.
- **AC15P** uses concepts as description-to-codifier procedural controls. It makes situations readable and checkable through gates, thresholds, statuses, safeguards, and failure modes. Its cost is thinner human interpretation: the user receives strong procedure, but social, affective, relational, or conceptual complexity may need to be supplied from outside the answer.
- **Hybsem** uses concepts as local anchors. Its compact distinctions often clarify the conflict sharply, but they do not always become a full hierarchy, sequence, or decision architecture. Its cost is assembly burden: the user may understand the parts while still needing to build the path.
- **Control** uses readability to make the situation approachable. It gives useful coverage, checks, and calm organization. Its cost is under-governance: the user often receives many relevant pieces without enough explanation of how they relate, what failure means, or which pressure should govern when pressures collide.

The comparative point is therefore not that one system is readable and another is not, or that one system uses checks and another does not. The point is whether readability, concepts, and checks become an interpretable structure. AC15 and Hybrid most often turn them into governed relations; AC15P turns them into protective codified procedure; Hybsem turns them into strong local handles; Control often leaves them as useful but under-related materials.

### Paper Implication

This dimension matters because coverage and readability can look like understanding while still leaving the user with the main interpretive burden. A response may mention the right topics, organize them clearly, and give practical checks, yet still fail to show what kind of situation those topics form together.

For the paper, this dimension should keep governance and quality separate. Governance asks whether conceptual framing, interpretation, readability, and checks behave within the active constraints: whether they preserve the user's domain, scope, authority, uncertainty, and the real relations between pressures. Quality asks whether that governed behavior serves the user well enough to act. These elements do not move in one simple direction. More conceptual richness can make an answer denser; stronger checkability can require more structure; better coverage can make prose heavier. A good answer is not necessarily the easiest answer to read. It is complete when it is organized well enough for the user to follow the main rationale, covers the active issues at the needed depth, and makes the relationships between pressures, assumptions, evidence, choices, and consequences clear. This is why Control can be highly readable yet less governed, while AC15 can be denser yet stronger when the complexity is real and the rationale remains followable.

## Integrative Layer: User Position And What Each System Gives Or Leaves To The User

The dimensions above compare how the systems handle conflict, structure, action, time, affect, authority, conceptual breadth, readability, and checkability. The integrative question is what these differences do to the user's position. A system does not only answer the user. It also places the user in a role: interpreter, protected decision-maker, strategic tester, or recipient of organized advice.

The table below identifies the dominant posture of each system. These are not exclusive categories. The systems overlap in many tools: all can use gates, lists, distinctions, validation, and practical steps. The difference lies in what each system makes central and what work remains with the user.

| System | What The System Mainly Gives | Main Cost Or Risk | How The User Is Framed |
|---|---|---|---|
| AC15 | Integrated judgment: broad context, visible rationale, relation-making, and conditional paths through tension | Complexity load; occasional local affective expansion beyond the user's wording | A decision-maker asked to hold complexity with the model's help |
| AC15P | Description-to-codifier procedural protection: safeguards, gates, thresholds, statuses, workflows, and explicit criteria | Premature proceduralization; lack of social, affective, relational, or conceptual nuance in human domains | A decision-maker protected by procedure and asked to follow or interpret safeguards |
| Hybrid_3_5 | Definition-led strategic reframing: widened perspective, evidence-producing process, outside review, observation, and staged decision gates | Process confidence that can underplay friction, maintenance burden, or responsibility | A decision-maker who tests reality through evidence, observation, and reversible stages |
| Hybsem | Definition-led semantic handles: compact distinctions, restrained tone, and strong local modules | Local clarity without full hierarchy, sequence, carry-forward, or operational placement in broad prompts | A reader given strong concepts who may still need to assemble the path |
| Control | Accessible organization: calm due diligence, broad coverage, useful fragments, scripts, and practical checks | Smoothness without enough governance; relations between pressures under-defined or flattened; weak thresholds, forks, and consequences | A general-purpose advice recipient who often must reconstruct the governing logic |

The practical consequence is clear. AC15 gives the user a governed interpretation but asks them to hold complexity. AC15P gives the user classification plus protective machinery, but may narrow the human field into procedure. Hybrid gives the user a way to test reality from a wider angle but can make process look cleaner than it is. Hybsem gives the user strong conceptual handles but may leave the larger path unfinished. Control gives the user readable and often useful advice, but often leaves the user to decide how the pieces relate, what failure means, and which pressure should govern.

## Compact Conclusion

The cross-scenario comparisons show that the systems differ less by which topics they recognize than by how they govern the relations between those topics. The same broad recommendation can therefore carry very different epistemological behavior. Controlled memory, formal legal process, or reversible relationship trial are not single ideas once written through different systems. They become different ways of seeing, deciding, testing, protecting, and assigning authority.

AC15 tends to govern by relation-making: it separates multiple pressures, shows their mutual effects, and turns them into conditional judgment. AC15P tends to govern by description-to-codifier procedural protection: pressure is first classified, then becomes categories, safeguards, thresholds, and failure controls. Hybrid_3_5 tends to govern by strategic reframing: its definition-first structure, rules, and fields widen the user's view through evidence, observation, outside review, and staged process. Hybsem tends to govern by semantic anchoring: its definition-first structure gives tensions clear conceptual places, but those places do not always become a full sequence or architecture. Control tends to govern by accessible containment: it makes pressure readable and manageable, but often substitutes organization, reassurance, or due diligence for a governed account of how the pressures relate.

These differences repeat across technical, legal-social, and affective-relational scenarios. That repetition supports the paper's core claim: system language and structure shape response behavior, not merely surface style. The systems are epistemological management layers. They do not only express logic; they decide what kind of logic becomes natural, what is foregrounded, what is softened, what is made operational, and what remains for the user to complete.
